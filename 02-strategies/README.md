# 02 — Investing Strategies

Every serious investing approach is a bet about *where* the market makes mistakes and *why* you can profit from them. Indexing bets that it mostly does not, so the winning move is to own everything cheaply. Value and growth investors bet that individual companies are mispriced and that patient analysis reveals it. Factor and momentum investors bet that certain measurable traits earn premiums that persist because of risk or human behavior. All-weather investors decline to bet at all and build for every regime. Special-situations investors hunt where the market is structurally forced to sell.

This section explains each school deeply enough to actually follow it: the lineage, the evidence for and against, the metrics, the failure modes, and a step-by-step "how to actually do it." Read the index file first — it is the baseline every other strategy must beat after costs and taxes — then pick the one or two approaches that match your temperament and available time. Combining a passive core with one deliberate tilt is how most successful individual investors end up.

## Comparison of strategies

| Strategy | Time commitment | Risk vs. market | Evidence quality | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Index & passive](index-and-passive-investing.md) | ~1 hour/year | Market risk, nothing extra | Overwhelming (SPIVA, Sharpe's arithmetic) | Everyone; the default and the benchmark |
| [Value](value-investing.md) | Hours/week reading filings | Similar to market long-run; multi-year lags | Strong over a century; weak 2007–2020 | Patient readers who enjoy accounting |
| [Growth](growth-investing.md) | Hours/week | Higher; multiple compression is the killer | Mixed; long-run edge belongs to price-disciplined (GARP) growth | Those who understand technology and business models |
| [Dividend & income](dividend-and-income-investing.md) | 2–5 hours/year with ETFs | Slightly below market (quality tilt); sector concentration risk | Moderate; dividend *growth* is a good quality proxy, yield alone is not | Retirees and anyone who needs cash flow to stay invested |
| [Factor](factor-investing.md) | 1–2 hours/year with ETFs | Market plus tracking error; multi-year lags | Strong academic record; real-world premiums smaller | Long-horizon investors comfortable with looking different |
| [Momentum & trend following](momentum-and-trend-following.md) | 5 min/month, but must be followed every time | Lower drawdowns, more small losses, lags V-recoveries | Century of data for trend; post-publication decay for dual momentum | Investors who sell in crashes and need a rule to stop them |
| [Quantitative & systematic](quantitative-and-systematic-investing.md) | Varies; 1–10 hours/year for simple rules | Depends on the rule; backtests overstate | Strong for simple published rules; overfitting is rampant | Engineers and anyone who trusts rules more than gut |
| [Macro & all-weather](macro-and-all-weather-investing.md) | 1–2 hours/year | Roughly half the market's volatility and drawdown; lags bull markets | Good 50-year backtests; 2022 was a real test | Near-retirees, the risk-averse, early-retirement planners |
| [Options](options-strategies.md) | Hours/week | Ranges from hedged to catastrophic | BXM buy-write matches market with less volatility (1988–2006); retail option buyers lose on average | Concentrated-position holders (collars); disciplined income sellers |
| [Contrarian & special situations](contrarian-and-special-situations.md) | Hours/week reading Form 10s and proxies | Idiosyncratic; uncorrelated but lumpy | Strong for spin-offs (two academic studies); mixed elsewhere | Experienced investors with a 10%–20% sleeve to devote |
| [Small-cap & microcap](small-cap-and-microcap-investing.md) | Hours/week for individual names; 1 hour/year via funds | Higher volatility, illiquidity, failure rate | Strong for small *value/quality*; weak for size alone | Diligent researchers; anyone via AVUV/DFSV/S&P 600 funds |

## How to use this section

- **If you have under two hours a year**: read file 1, optionally file 8, and stop. You will beat most professionals.
- **If you want one tilt on top of an index core**: files 5 (factors), 6 (trend), or 4 (dividend growth) are the ones with evidence and ETF implementations.
- **If you want to pick stocks**: files 2, 3, 10, and 11, in that order, and budget several hours a week indefinitely.
- **If you hold a concentrated position or need income now**: files 9 and 4.

Every file ends with a "How to actually do it" checklist and a sourced reading list. The sources are the real thing — academic papers, fund-company research, and primary documents — because the details matter and summaries (including these) drift.
