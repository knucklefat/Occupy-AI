# 09 — Behavioral Finance

The largest, most reliable drag on most people's investment returns is not fees, taxes, or bad fund selection. It is the investor. Morningstar's annual "Mind the Gap" study finds the average dollar earns roughly a point a year less than the fund it sits in, entirely because of when it arrives and leaves; over a working lifetime that compounds into a fifth to a half of the final result. The cause is not stupidity. It is standard human equipment — loss aversion, overconfidence, recency, herding — operating exactly as designed, in an environment it was never designed for.

This section covers both halves of the problem. The first half is diagnosis: what the biases are, how prospect theory explains them, how they produce bubbles and panics, and why the information you consume makes them worse. The second half is defense: written policy statements, decision systems, crash playbooks, and the mindset lessons from Housel and others that turn knowing into doing. Read the diagnosis once; keep the defenses where you can find them in a crash.
