# 04 — Analysis: How to Evaluate an Individual Investment

Everything in this section answers one question: given a specific stock, bond, or fund, how do you decide whether it is worth owning and at what price? The first half is **fundamental analysis** — reading the financial statements and the 10-K, turning them into ratios, estimating what the business is worth, and judging the moat and the people running it. Then comes **screening**, the craft of finding candidates worth this effort. The second half covers **technical analysis** — price, volume, trends, indicators, and chart patterns — with an honest account of what academic research does and does not support, framed as a tool for timing and risk control rather than prediction. Two final guides extend the same discipline to **bonds** and **funds/ETFs**: know what you own, what it costs, and what could go wrong. Every file ends with a step-by-step "how to actually do it" and links to primary sources.

## Suggested reading order

New to analysis: financial statements → 10-K → ratios → valuation → moats → management → screening. Then technical analysis and chart patterns as a timing overlay. Bonds and funds can be read independently.
