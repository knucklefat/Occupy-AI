# Investment Policy Statement Template

**In one sentence:** A fill-in-the-blank contract with your future self that records your goals, allocation, rules, and forbidden behaviors while you are calm — so that when markets or emotions run hot, the decision has already been made.

An Investment Policy Statement (IPS) is the single most useful document a self-directed investor can write. Institutions have used them for decades; the Bogleheads wiki adapted the idea for individuals. The value is not in the planning — it's in reading it during a crash, a bubble, or a persuasive dinner-party stock tip, and following it. Keep it to two or three pages. Review it annually and after major life events, and change it only in writing, on a calm day, never in response to what the market did last month.

Two versions follow: a **blank template** with prompts and example values, and a **fully completed example** for a fictional 40-year-old.

---

## Part 1 — Blank template

Copy everything between the lines into your own document and replace the bracketed prompts.

---

### INVESTMENT POLICY STATEMENT

**Owner(s):** [Your name(s)]
**Date written:** [YYYY-MM-DD]  **Last reviewed:** [YYYY-MM-DD]  **Next review due:** [YYYY-MM-DD]

#### 1. Purpose

This document states how I will invest, why, and what I will do when I am tempted to deviate. I wrote it when I was calm. When I want to change it, I will wait [30 days], re-read it, and revise it in writing — not in a brokerage app.

#### 2. Goals

List each goal with a target amount (today's dollars), target date, and priority.

| # | Goal | Target amount | Target date | Priority (must / want) | Account(s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Retirement — replace X% of spending] | [$___] | [Year] | Must | [401(k), IRA, taxable] |
| 2 | [Emergency fund — N months of expenses] | [$___] | [Now] | Must | [HYSA] |
| 3 | [Home down payment / child's college / sabbatical] | [$___] | [Year] | Want | [Taxable / 529] |

#### 3. Time horizon

- Primary horizon (years until I start withdrawing from the main portfolio): [__ years]
- Expected withdrawal period after that: [__ years]
- Money needed within 5 years is held in [cash / short-term bonds] and is *not* part of the long-term allocation.

#### 4. Risk tolerance

*Ability* to take risk (objective: horizon, job stability, other income, dependents): [Low / Medium / High — because ___]
*Willingness* to take risk (emotional: how I actually behaved in past declines): [Low / Medium / High — because ___]
*Need* to take risk (does reaching my goals require high returns?): [Low / Medium / High — because ___]

The lowest of the three governs.

**Loss I have decided I can tolerate in a bad year without changing the plan:** [__%] of the portfolio, roughly [$___]. Historically a [__/__] stock/bond portfolio has fallen about that much in its worst years.

#### 5. Target asset allocation and rebalancing bands

| Asset class | Target % | Acceptable range | Fund(s) used |
|---|---|---|---|
| US stocks | [__%] | [__–__%] | [e.g., Total US Stock Market index] |
| International stocks | [__%] | [__–__%] | [e.g., Total International index] |
| Bonds | [__%] | [__–__%] | [e.g., Total Bond Market index] |
| Cash (inside portfolio, if any) | [__%] | [__–__%] | [Money market] |
| Other (REITs, TIPS, etc.) | [__%] | [__–__%] | [ ] |
| Individual stocks / speculative | [max __%] | [0–__%] | [Listed in Appendix] |

Glide path: I will [add __% to bonds every __ years / follow a target-date fund / hold this allocation until age __, then move to __/__].

#### 6. Asset location

- Bonds and anything tax-inefficient (REITs, high-yield) go in: [401(k) / Traditional IRA]
- Highest expected-growth assets go in: [Roth IRA / HSA]
- Broad stock index funds in taxable are eligible for tax-loss harvesting with these partners: [Fund A ↔ Fund B]

#### 7. Rebalancing rules

- I rebalance [once a year on ___] **and/or** [whenever any asset class moves outside its range].
- Method, in order: (1) direct new contributions to the underweight asset, (2) trade inside tax-advantaged accounts, (3) trade in taxable only if still outside range, using lots with losses or the smallest gains.
- I do **not** rebalance in reaction to news, forecasts, or my own feelings.

#### 8. Contribution plan

| Account | Amount | Frequency | Automated? | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 401(k) | [__% of salary] | Per paycheck | Yes | 1 (to full match), 5 (to max) |
| HSA | [$___/mo] | Monthly | Yes | 3 |
| IRA ([Roth/Trad]) | [$___/mo] | Monthly | Yes | 4 |
| Taxable | [$___/mo] | Monthly | Yes | 6 |

Savings rate target: [__%] of gross income, rising [1%] with each raise. Windfalls (bonus, inheritance, tax refund): [__% invested per allocation, __% to goals, __% free].

#### 9. What I will never do

- [ ] Sell stocks because the market fell. A decline is a rebalancing signal, not a selling signal.
- [ ] Buy anything I can't explain to a friend in two sentences.
- [ ] Use margin, leverage, options I don't understand, or leveraged/inverse ETFs.
- [ ] Put more than [__%] in any single stock, or more than [__%] in individual stocks combined.
- [ ] Put more than [__%] in crypto or other speculative assets.
- [ ] Act on a tip, a headline, a forecast, or a hot fund's recent returns.
- [ ] Pay more than [0.25%] in annual fund fees on any core holding, or any advisory fee based on assets without a written reason.
- [ ] Change this allocation more than once every [12 months], or within [30 days] of a decision to change it.
- [ ] Try to time the market — including "waiting for a pullback" with new money.
- [ ] [Add your own known weaknesses here.]

#### 10. What I will do in a crash (30%+ decline)

1. Nothing for [48 hours].
2. Re-read this document.
3. Rebalance if bands are breached, and harvest losses in taxable per section 7.
4. Continue every automated contribution unchanged.
5. Consider a Roth conversion if income is low this year.
6. Follow [market-crash-playbook.md](market-crash-playbook.md).

#### 11. Monitoring and review

- Quarterly ([15 minutes]): update [portfolio-review-template.md](portfolio-review-template.md).
- Annually ([January]): full checkup per [annual-financial-checkup-checklist.md](annual-financial-checkup-checklist.md), including this IPS.
- Benchmark: a blended index matching my target allocation. Underperforming it by [1%/year over 3 years] triggers a cost and implementation review — not a strategy change.
- Life events that trigger an out-of-cycle review: marriage/divorce, birth, job loss, inheritance, health change, retirement within 10 years.

#### 12. Advisors and helpers

- I use / do not use an advisor: [ ]. If I do: fee-only, fiduciary, verified on [IAPD/BrokerCheck], fee: [ ].
- My partner/executor knows this document exists at: [location].

**Signed:** ____________________  **Date:** __________

## Part 2 — Completed example: Maya Torres, age 40

*Fictional. Numbers are illustrative.*

---

### INVESTMENT POLICY STATEMENT

**Owner:** Maya Torres
**Date written:** 2026-01-10  **Last reviewed:** 2026-01-10  **Next review due:** 2027-01-10

#### 1. Purpose

This is my plan, written on a calm Saturday. I'm 40, a project manager, single, no children, stable job, $500k current portfolio. My worst investing mistake was selling half my 401(k) to cash in March 2020 and buying back 25% higher. This document exists so I never do that again. Any change requires a 30-day wait and a rewrite of this page.

#### 2. Goals

| # | Goal | Target amount | Target date | Priority | Account(s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Retire at 62 with $60k/yr spending (today's dollars) → ~$1.5M plus Social Security | $1,500,000 | 2048 | Must | 401(k), Roth IRA, HSA, taxable |
| 2 | Emergency fund, 6 months ($24k) | $24,000 | Done | Must | HYSA |
| 3 | Sabbatical year at 50 | $60,000 | 2036 | Want | Taxable |

#### 3. Time horizon

- Primary horizon: 22 years to retirement.
- Withdrawal period: 30+ years after that.
- Sabbatical money (goal 3) shifts to short-term Treasuries starting 2033, three years before I need it.

#### 4. Risk tolerance

- Ability: High — long horizon, stable income, no dependents.
- Willingness: **Medium** — I sold in 2020. I know this about myself.
- Need: Medium — a 5% real return gets me there; I don't need to swing for 8%.

Willingness governs. **Tolerable bad-year loss: 30% (~$150k).** An 80/20 portfolio lost about 30–35% in 2008; a 70/30 lost about 25–30%. I'm choosing 75/25 and committing to it.

#### 5. Target asset allocation and rebalancing bands

| Asset class | Target % | Range | Fund(s) used |
|---|---|---|---|
| US stocks | 50% | 45–55% | Total US Stock Market index (VTI / FSKAX / SWTSX) |
| International stocks | 25% | 20–30% | Total International index (VXUS / FTIHX / SWISX) |
| Bonds | 25% | 20–30% | Total Bond Market index (BND / FXNAX) + Series I bonds |
| Individual stocks | max 5% | 0–5% | Two positions, listed in Appendix, in Roth IRA only |

Glide path: add 5% to bonds at 50, 55, and 60 (ending 60/40 at retirement).

#### 6. Asset location

- All bonds in the 401(k) (Traditional).
- Roth IRA holds US stocks (highest growth, tax-free forever) and the two individual stocks.
- HSA invested 100% in Total US Stock index; I pay medical costs out of pocket and save receipts.
- Taxable holds US + international index ETFs. TLH partners: VTI ↔ ITOT; VXUS ↔ IXUS.

#### 7. Rebalancing rules

- Every January, and whenever any class breaches its range (checked at each quarterly review).
- New contributions go to whatever is most underweight. Trades happen in the 401(k) first. Taxable is touched only for harvesting losses.
- Rebalancing days are the *only* days I place trades.

#### 8. Contribution plan

| Account | Amount | Frequency | Automated | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 401(k), Traditional | 12% of salary ($15,600) + 5% match | Per paycheck | Yes | 1 |
| HSA | $4,400/yr via payroll | Per paycheck | Yes | 2 |
| Roth IRA | $625/mo ($7,500) | Monthly, 1st | Yes | 3 |
| Taxable brokerage | $800/mo | Monthly, 15th | Yes | 4 |

Savings rate: 24% of gross including match; +1% every raise. Bonus: 70% to taxable, 30% guilt-free.

#### 9. What I will never do

- Sell any stock fund because the market fell. Never again.
- Buy anything I couldn't explain to my sister in two sentences.
- Use margin, options, or leveraged/inverse ETFs.
- Hold more than 5% total in individual stocks, or more than 2.5% in any one.
- Hold any crypto beyond 1% — currently 0%.
- Act on anything from social media, a newsletter, a coworker, or CNBC.
- Pay an expense ratio over 0.15% on any core fund, or any percentage-of-assets advisory fee.
- Change this allocation more than once per year, or within 30 days of deciding to.
- Hold cash "waiting for a dip." New money is invested the day it arrives.
- Check balances more than once a week. (Deleted the app from my phone.)

#### 10. What I will do in a crash

1. Nothing for 48 hours. Go for a run.
2. Re-read this page, especially section 1.
3. If bands are breached, rebalance in the 401(k) — which means *buying* stocks.
4. Harvest losses in taxable: VTI→ITOT, VXUS→IXUS. Turn off dividend reinvestment for 31 days.
5. If I've lost my job that year, convert Traditional IRA/401(k) money to Roth up to the top of the 12% bracket.
6. Confirm all automations are still on. Then stop reading financial news until the next quarterly review.

#### 11. Monitoring and review

- Quarterly: portfolio review template, 15 minutes, first Sunday of Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct.
- Annually: full checkup in January; this IPS re-read and re-signed.
- Benchmark: 50% US total market / 25% international / 25% aggregate bond. If I trail it by more than 1%/yr over 3 years, I audit fees and tax drag — I do not change strategy.
- Out-of-cycle review triggers: marriage, child, job change, inheritance, health event, or reaching age 50.

#### 12. Advisors and helpers

- No ongoing advisor. I'll pay a fee-only planner (verified on IAPD, flat fee) for a one-time review at 50 and again at 60.
- This document and my account list are in the shared folder my sister can access; she's my POA.

**Signed:** Maya Torres  **Date:** 2026-01-10

## Tips for writing yours

- **Short beats complete.** A one-page IPS you re-read beats a ten-page one you don't.
- **The "never" list is the most valuable section.** Fill it with your *actual* past mistakes, not generic ones.
- **Ranges should be wide enough to avoid constant trading** (±5 percentage points is typical) but tight enough to catch real drift.
- **Write the crash section as instructions to a scared person**, because that's who will read it.
