# Annual Financial Checkup Checklist

**In one sentence:** A twice-a-year maintenance routine — a December version for things that must happen before the tax year closes, and a January version for resetting the year ahead — covering rebalancing, contribution limits, beneficiaries, tax harvesting, fees, insurance, estate documents, security hygiene, and required distributions.

Most financial damage doesn't come from bad decisions; it comes from things nobody looked at. This checklist exists so that once a year, someone looks. Block two hours in December and two in January. Print it, check boxes, and file it with the date — next year's version starts by reading last year's notes.

**Key 2026 figures used below** (verify each year; these change with inflation): 401(k)/403(b)/457 employee limit $24,500, age 50+ catch-up $8,000, age 60–63 "super catch-up" $11,250; IRA limit $7,500, age 50+ catch-up $1,100; HSA self-only $4,400 (family limit is higher; age 55+ catch-up $1,000); annual gift exclusion $19,000 per recipient; RMDs (required minimum distributions — forced withdrawals from pre-tax retirement accounts) start at age 73 for those born 1951–1959 and 75 for those born 1960 or later.

## December version — "close the year"

Deadline-driven items. Most must be completed by December 31 (or the last trading day).

### 1. Tax-loss and tax-gain harvesting review
*Why it matters:* In a taxable account, selling a losing position lets you deduct the loss against gains (and up to $3,000/year against ordinary income), while selling a winner in a low-income year can lock in gains at a 0% rate. Neither happens automatically.
- [ ] Pull unrealized gains/losses for every taxable holding.
- [ ] For each position with a meaningful loss: sell it and immediately buy a *similar but not identical* fund (e.g., swap one total-market index fund for another provider's) to stay invested. Avoid buying the same or a "substantially identical" security 30 days before or after, in *any* account including IRAs, or the loss is disallowed (the "wash sale" rule).
- [ ] Turn off automatic dividend reinvestment on the sold fund for the 30-day window, or it can trigger a wash sale.
- [ ] If your taxable income is under the 0% long-term capital gains ceiling ($49,450 single / $98,900 married filing jointly for 2026 — including the gains themselves), consider *tax-gain harvesting*: sell appreciated long-term holdings, pay 0%, and rebuy immediately (no wash-sale rule for gains) to reset your cost basis higher.
- [ ] Record every harvest: date, security, loss/gain amount, replacement fund. Carryforward losses live for years — track them.

### 2. Max out what has a December 31 deadline
- [ ] 401(k)/403(b)/TSP: check year-to-date contributions on your last pay stub. If short of the limit and affordable, raise the final paycheck deferrals. (IRA and HSA contributions can be made until the April tax deadline, but payroll deferrals cannot.)
- [ ] Employer match true-up: if you front-loaded contributions and hit the limit early, confirm your plan has a "true-up" or you may have forfeited match.
- [ ] Solo 401(k) / SEP-IRA if self-employed: employee portion generally must be elected by year-end; check your plan's rules.
- [ ] 529 contributions: many states require a December 31 contribution for the current-year state tax deduction.

### 3. Roth conversion decision
*Why it matters:* Converting pre-tax IRA money to Roth means paying tax now to never pay it again. It's most valuable in a year when your income is unusually low (job gap, early retirement, sabbatical). Conversions cannot be undone.
- [ ] Estimate this year's taxable income. Identify how much room remains in your current tax bracket.
- [ ] If there is room and cash outside the IRA to pay the tax, consider converting up to the top of the bracket.
- [ ] Check the knock-on effects: ACA premium subsidies, IRMAA (Medicare premium surcharges, based on income from two years prior), Social Security taxation, and financial aid formulas all key off this year's income.

### 4. Required minimum distributions (RMDs)
- [ ] If you're at RMD age (or hold an inherited IRA), confirm the full required amount was withdrawn. The penalty for missing it is 25% of the shortfall (10% if corrected quickly).
- [ ] Consider a Qualified Charitable Distribution (QCD) directly from an IRA to charity if you're 70½+ — it satisfies the RMD and is excluded from income.
- [ ] Set next year's RMD to be paid automatically.

### 5. Charitable giving
- [ ] Donate appreciated shares (held over a year) instead of cash — you deduct the full value and never pay the capital gain.
- [ ] If you take the standard deduction most years, consider "bunching" several years of giving into a donor-advised fund in one year.

### 6. Gifts and estate deadlines
- [ ] Annual exclusion gifts ($19,000 per recipient per giver in 2026) must be completed — checks cleared, transfers settled — by December 31 to count for this year.
- [ ] 529 contributions for children/grandchildren count as gifts; five-year "superfunding" elections are available.

### 7. Flexible spending accounts
- [ ] Spend down any FSA balance that doesn't roll over (check your plan's grace period or carryover amount).

### 8. Quick-look rebalance
- [ ] Compare current allocation to target. If any asset class is outside its band, rebalance now inside tax-advantaged accounts first (no tax consequence), using taxable accounts only where harvesting already justifies a sale.

### 9. Year-end notes
- [ ] Write a five-line summary: total contributions this year, harvests done, conversions done, decisions made, anything you're worried about. Next year's checkup starts here.

## January version — "set up the year"

Reset and prevention items. Do these in the first two weeks of the year.

### 1. Update contribution automations for the new limits
- [ ] 401(k)/403(b)/457: new employee limit ÷ number of paychecks. Add catch-up if you turn 50 (or 60–63 for the super catch-up) at any point this year.
- [ ] IRA: new limit ÷ 12 for monthly automation, or lump-sum in January if you can (earlier money compounds longer).
- [ ] HSA: new limit ÷ 12 (or via payroll to also save FICA taxes). Confirm the HSA cash is being *invested*, not sitting in the cash bucket.
- [ ] If your income may exceed the Roth IRA phase-out ($153,000–$168,000 single / $242,000–$252,000 married in 2026), plan a backdoor Roth instead: contribute to a non-deductible Traditional IRA, convert promptly, and make sure you hold no other pre-tax IRA balances (the "pro-rata rule").
- [ ] Prior-year IRA/HSA contributions can still be made until the April tax deadline — do them now if you didn't finish.

### 2. Full rebalance
*Why it matters:* Left alone, a 60/40 portfolio drifts toward 80/20 over a long bull market — right before the crash you weren't sized for.
- [ ] Record actual allocation across *all* accounts combined (a single spreadsheet; see [portfolio-review-template.md](portfolio-review-template.md)).
- [ ] Compare to the targets and bands in your IPS.
- [ ] Rebalance in this order: (1) redirect new contributions, (2) trade inside tax-advantaged accounts, (3) trade in taxable accounts only if still needed and only using lots with small gains or losses.
- [ ] Note the date in your IPS.

### 3. Fee audit
- [ ] List every fund's expense ratio. Weighted average across the portfolio: target under 0.20%. Anything over 0.50% needs a written justification or a replacement.
- [ ] List every account-level fee: advisory fees (as a % of assets), account maintenance fees, 401(k) plan administration fees (in the plan's annual fee disclosure), inactivity fees.
- [ ] Multiply total annual fees in dollars by 25. That's roughly what they cost you over a career in today's dollars. Decide whether each is worth it.
- [ ] Check for uninvested cash earning nothing in brokerage sweep accounts; move it to a money-market fund or invest it.

### 4. Beneficiary and titling review
*Why it matters:* Beneficiary designations override your will. An ex-spouse listed on an old 401(k) will inherit it.
- [ ] Log in to every retirement account, life insurance policy, HSA, and annuity. Confirm primary *and* contingent beneficiaries are correct.
- [ ] Add transfer-on-death (TOD) designations to taxable brokerage accounts and payable-on-death (POD) to bank accounts where appropriate, so they skip probate.
- [ ] Confirm jointly-held accounts are titled the way you intend.

### 5. Estate documents
- [ ] Will: still reflects your wishes, executor still willing and able, guardian for minor children still correct.
- [ ] Durable financial power of attorney and healthcare directive/proxy: exist, are signed, and the named person knows where they are.
- [ ] If you have a revocable trust: confirm accounts and property are actually titled in the trust's name (an unfunded trust does nothing).
- [ ] Update the "if I die" letter: account list, logins location, advisor contacts, where the documents are.

### 6. Insurance review
- [ ] Term life: coverage still roughly 10–12× income if anyone depends on you; term length still covers the years until dependents are independent.
- [ ] Disability: long-term coverage exists (through work or individually); understand "own-occupation" vs "any-occupation" definitions.
- [ ] Umbrella liability: consider $1M+ once net worth or income is meaningful; it's cheap.
- [ ] Home/renters/auto: coverage limits reflect current replacement cost; deductibles set as high as your emergency fund comfortably allows.
- [ ] Health: confirm plan selection was optimal in open enrollment; if on an HDHP, HSA is funded.

### 7. Emergency fund
- [ ] Still 3–6 months of *current* expenses? Expenses tend to rise; top it up.
- [ ] Still earning a competitive rate? Move it if your bank quietly dropped its yield.

### 8. Credit and identity
- [ ] Pull free credit reports from all three bureaus (AnnualCreditReport.com) and check for accounts you don't recognize.
- [ ] Confirm the credit freeze is still in place at Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. If you thawed it for a loan, refreeze.
- [ ] Set up an IRS Identity Protection PIN so nobody can file a tax return in your name.
- [ ] Create or verify your my Social Security account online (before a fraudster does it for you) and check your earnings record for errors.

### 9. Password and two-factor hygiene
- [ ] Every financial login: unique password from a password manager, two-factor authentication using an authenticator app or hardware key (SMS is a weak fallback).
- [ ] The email account attached to your financial accounts is the master key — it gets the strongest protection of all.
- [ ] Review which third-party apps have access to your brokerage/bank data; revoke anything you no longer use.
- [ ] Set up account alerts for logins, withdrawals, and address changes.

### 10. Tax preparation
- [ ] Gather 1099s, W-2s, harvest records, conversion records (Form 1099-R), and non-deductible IRA basis (Form 8606 — if you did a backdoor Roth, this form is mandatory and frequently missed).
- [ ] Review last year's withholding: a large refund means you're lending the government money; a large bill may trigger underpayment penalties. Adjust your W-4.
- [ ] If self-employed or with large investment income: set quarterly estimated payment reminders.

### 11. Goals and IPS review
- [ ] Reread your IPS. Has anything changed — marriage, child, job, inheritance, health, risk tolerance after last year's markets? Update deliberately, in writing, on a calm day. Do not change the allocation because of what markets did last year.
- [ ] Update your net worth statement. Compare to last January. The trend matters, not the number.
- [ ] Set this year's contribution rate target (e.g., +1% from last year) and calendar the two review dates for next year.

### 12. RMD and withdrawal planning (if retired or near it)
- [ ] Calculate this year's RMD from the December 31 balance and the IRS life-expectancy table; schedule it.
- [ ] Review the withdrawal plan against [retirement-readiness-checklist.md](retirement-readiness-checklist.md): which accounts to draw from first, and whether Roth conversions make sense before RMDs begin.
- [ ] If within two years of Medicare: understand that this year's income sets Medicare premiums two years from now (IRMAA), so time large gains or conversions accordingly.
