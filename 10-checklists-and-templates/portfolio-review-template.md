# Portfolio Review Template

**In one sentence:** A one-page form for a 15-minute monthly or quarterly review — holdings versus targets, performance against a benchmark, contributions made, fees paid, and a decisions log — that keeps you informed enough to stay disciplined and busy enough not to tinker.

The purpose of a review is *not* to find something to change. It's to confirm the machine is running (contributions in, allocation in range, costs low), record anything you decided, and close the laptop. Most quarters the correct outcome is "no action." Use the quarterly version; go monthly only if you're actively harvesting losses or managing withdrawals in retirement. Keep every completed review in one folder — the sequence is your investing diary, and it's where you'll catch your own patterns.

**Jargon defined once:** *Benchmark* is a simple mix of index funds matching your target allocation; you compare against it to see whether your implementation (fees, cash drag, tinkering) is costing you. *Drift* is how far an asset class has moved from its target. *Weighted expense ratio* is your average fund fee, weighted by how much you hold in each.

## Tips

- **Combine accounts first.** Reviewing each account separately is how people end up 90% stocks in one and 90% bonds in another and think they're balanced.
- **Benchmark honestly.** If you hold 75% stocks, don't compare yourself to the S&P 500. Compare to a 75/25 blend.
- **The decisions log is the real product.** A year of "no action, all in band" entries is evidence of a working plan. A year of trades is a prompt to re-read the IPS.
- **If the review takes more than 30 minutes, the portfolio is too complicated.** Simplify at the next annual checkup.
