# Newsletters and Blogs

**In one sentence:** Thirty-eight writers who will make you a better investor if you read them regularly, sorted by what they are good for, with a note on cost and who each one suits — every site checked live in September 2026.

The trap with financial newsletters is that the loudest ones sell fear or hot tips. Nothing on this list does either. The bar for inclusion: the author has skin in the game or a long public record, writes clearly, and you would be better off for having read a year of their work. Free unless marked. "Substack-style freemium" means the free tier is genuinely useful, not a teaser.

## Start here: the evidence-based core

| Publication | Author | Cadence, cost | Why read it, who it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| [A Wealth of Common Sense](https://awealthofcommonsense.com/) | Ben Carlson (Ritholtz Wealth) | Several posts a week, free | Answers real reader questions with charts and no jargon. The best single blog for someone who wants sensible perspective on whatever is scaring the market this week. |
| [The Irrelevant Investor](https://theirrelevantinvestor.com/) | Michael Batnick (Ritholtz Wealth) | Weekly-ish, free | Short, data-driven, often about behavior and market history. Batnick's "The Big Lessons" posts are worth searching for. |
| [Of Dollars and Data](https://ofdollarsanddata.com/) | Nick Maggiulli | Weekly (Tuesday), free | Personal finance questions answered with data instead of opinion: lump sum vs. DCA, when to sell, how much to save. Author of *Just Keep Buying*. |
| [Collaborative Fund blog](https://collabfund.com/blog/) | Morgan Housel and colleagues | Irregular, free | Housel's essays on behavior, history, and compounding, the raw material for *The Psychology of Money*. Search the archive for "The Psychology of Money" (2018) and "Getting Rich vs. Staying Rich." |
| [Bogleheads wiki](https://www.bogleheads.org/wiki/Main_Page) | Community volunteers | Reference, free | Not a blog but the most reliable reference for account types, asset allocation, tax-efficient placement, and withdrawal strategies. When a question has a settled answer, it is here. |
| [HumbleDollar](https://humbledollar.com/) | Jonathan Clements and contributors | Daily, free | Personal finance essays by ordinary people and retired professionals, curated by the former WSJ columnist. Warm, practical, and unusually honest about aging and money. |
| [Oblivious Investor](https://obliviousinvestor.com/) | Mike Piper, CPA | Weekly (Monday), free | Short, precise posts on taxes, Social Security, and low-maintenance portfolios. Piper writes the clearest explanations of tax rules anywhere online. |
| [JL Collins](https://jlcollinsnh.com/) | JL Collins | Occasional, free | The "Stock Series" — 30-odd posts that became *The Simple Path to Wealth*. Read the series once; you will not need most other advice. |
| [Early Retirement Now](https://earlyretirementnow.com/) | Karsten Jeske ("Big ERN"), PhD economist | Monthly-ish, free | The Safe Withdrawal Rate series (60+ parts, still going) is the most rigorous free treatment of retirement withdrawal math. For people within ten years of retiring, essential. |
| [Mr. Money Mustache](https://www.mrmoneymustache.com/) | Pete Adeney | A few posts a year, free | The founding blog of the FIRE movement. Read the 2012 post "The Shockingly Simple Math Behind Early Retirement." Preachy, but the math is right. |
| [The Evidence-Based Investor](https://www.evidenceinvestor.com/) | Robin Powell | Several posts a week, free | UK journalist covering the academic case for indexing and the industry's habit of ignoring it. Good for a non-US perspective. |
| [Behavioural Investment](https://behaviouralinvestment.com/) | Joe Wiggins | Weekly, free | Short essays on the psychology of fund selection and market timing, by a UK fund researcher. Author of *The Intelligent Fund Investor*. |
| [Jason Zweig](https://jasonzweig.com/) | Jason Zweig (WSJ "Intelligent Investor" column) | Irregular, free (WSJ column itself is paywalled) | Zweig's own site with essays, reading lists, and his "Devil's Financial Dictionary." Thirty years of catching the industry in the act. |
| [Charlie Bilello's blog](https://bilello.blog/) | Charlie Bilello (Creative Planning) | Weekly "The Week in Charts," free | A chart-heavy weekly tour of markets, rates, housing, and valuations. The fastest way to stay informed without reading news. |

## Valuation, stock analysis, and business quality

| Publication | Author | Cadence, cost | Why read it, who it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Musings on Markets](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/) | Aswath Damodaran (NYU Stern) | Several posts a month, free | The "Dean of Valuation" values real companies (Tesla, Nvidia, Uber) in public with his spreadsheets attached, and updates his equity risk premium and industry data every January. Essential for anyone who wants to value a stock. |
| [Base Hit Investing](https://basehitinvesting.substack.com/) | John Huber (Saber Capital) | Monthly-ish, freemium | Patient, quality-focused stock analysis and thoughts on what makes an investment process work. Huber's older posts on ROIC and "the three sources of return" are classics. |
| [Yet Another Value Blog](https://www.yetanothervalueblog.com/) | Andrew Walker | Weekly, freemium | Special situations, merger arb, and quirky small-caps, with a companion podcast. For active stock pickers who enjoy detail. |
| [The Acquirer's Multiple](https://acquirersmultiple.com/blog/) | Tobias Carlisle | Daily, free | Deep-value screens, superinvestor 13F updates, and transcripts of the "Value After Hours" podcast. Carlisle's books explain the method. |
| [The Rational Walk](https://www.rationalwalk.com/) | Ravi Nagarajan | Weekly, free | Thoughtful essays on Berkshire, Markel, and value investing, plus an annual review of the major shareholder letters. Calm and unhurried. |
| [Safal Niveshak](https://www.safalniveshak.com/) | Vishal Khandelwal | Weekly, freemium | India-based value investing writer with a global readership. Best on behavior, checklists, and the discipline of doing nothing. |
| [Novel Investor](https://novelinvestor.com/) | Novel Investor | Weekly, free | Book notes on almost every investing classic, investor quotes indexed by person, and a weekly "Weekend Reads" roundup. A good starting point for research. |
| [Alpha Architect blog](https://alphaarchitect.com/blog/) | Wesley Gray, Jack Vogel, Larry Swedroe, and researchers | Several posts a week, free | Plain-English summaries of new academic papers on factors, momentum, value, and trend-following. The best bridge between the journals and practice. |
| [Verdad Weekly Research](https://verdadcap.com/weekly-research) | Dan Rasmussen and Verdad team | Weekly, free | Short, data-heavy pieces on private equity, small-cap value, credit cycles, and country selection, often puncturing an industry sales pitch. |
| [AQR Insights](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Perspectives) | Cliff Asness and AQR | Monthly-ish, free | Asness's Perspectives essays: the sharpest public defense of quant and factor investing, and of humility about it. Funnier than you expect. |
| [Philosophical Economics](https://www.philosophicaleconomics.com/) | "Jesse Livermore" (pseudonym) | Dormant since 2020, free archive | Long, original essays on valuation, profit margins, and market structure. "The Single Greatest Predictor of Future Stock Market Returns" (2013) and "Upside-Down Markets" (2020) are still discussed. Read the archive. |

## Macro, markets, and finance journalism

| Publication | Author | Cadence, cost | Why read it, who it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Money Stuff](https://www.bloomberg.com/account/newsletters/money-stuff) | Matt Levine (Bloomberg) | Daily on weekdays, free | The most-read finance newsletter in the world, and the funniest. Explains whatever happened on Wall Street today (SPACs, index arbitrage, crypto, activist fights) so that you actually understand it. Free by email even though Bloomberg is paywalled. |
| [The Big Picture](https://ritholtz.com/) | Barry Ritholtz | Daily, free | Morning link roundups and commentary on markets, economics, and behavior, plus transcripts of the Masters in Business podcast. |
| [Abnormal Returns](https://abnormalreturns.com/) | Tadas Viskanta | Daily, free | Since 2005, the daily curated list of the best finance writing on the internet. If you follow one link blog, follow this one. |
| [TKer](https://www.tker.co/) | Sam Ro, CFA | Several posts a week, freemium | Macro and market data explained for long-term investors, with a recurring "the stock market is not the economy" theme. Good for beginners who want to understand headlines. |
| [Calculated Risk](https://www.calculatedriskblog.com/) | Bill McBride | Weekly summary plus [housing newsletter](https://calculatedrisk.substack.com/), free/freemium | The housing and macro data blog that called the 2008 bust and the 2009 bottom. McBride stopped daily posts in January 2026 but continues the weekly summary and a housing-focused Substack. |
| [Klement on Investing](https://klementoninvesting.substack.com/) | Joachim Klement | Daily on weekdays, free | A London strategist summarizes one academic finding a day on markets, behavior, or economics. Short and reliable. |
| [Net Interest](https://www.netinterest.co/) | Marc Rubinstein | Weekly (Friday), freemium | Deep dives on banks, payments, exchanges, and insurers by a former financials hedge fund manager. The best writing on how the financial system's plumbing works. |
| [Bits about Money](https://www.bitsaboutmoney.com/) | Patrick McKenzie (patio11) | Monthly, free | Long explainers on how payments, banking, and fintech actually work, from someone who built parts of them at Stripe. Read "Deposit insurance" and "The business of credit card rewards." |
| [Doomberg](https://newsletter.doomberg.com/) | Anonymous team (the green chicken) | Several posts a week, paid with free previews (roughly $300/year) | Energy, commodities, and geopolitics analysis with a contrarian, pro-nuclear, pro-hydrocarbon bent. Very influential in energy investing circles; a Substack top earner. |
| [Lyn Alden Investment Strategy](https://www.lynalden.com/) | Lyn Alden | Monthly free newsletter; paid research tier | Long-form macro on debt cycles, currencies, energy, and Bitcoin. Alden explains monetary plumbing patiently and is careful to show her work. |
| [Grant's Interest Rate Observer](https://www.grantspub.com/) | James Grant | 24 issues a year, paid (about $2,085/year) | Since 1983, the contrarian's journal of credit and interest rates. Expensive and worth it for professionals; individuals can get most of the value from Grant's free podcast and interviews. |

## Business strategy and technology (for investors)

| Publication | Author | Cadence, cost | Why read it, who it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/) | Ben Thompson | Weekly free article; daily updates paid (about $15/month) | Aggregation Theory and a decade of analysis of Apple, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Nvidia. If you own tech stocks, this is the strategy context. |
| [The Diff](https://www.thediff.co/) | Byrne Hobart | One free issue a week; three more paid | Finance meets technology: how companies, markets, and incentives interact. Dense, original, and read by a lot of fund managers. |
| [Not Boring](https://www.notboring.co/) | Packy McCormick | Weekly, free | Optimistic deep dives on companies and technology trends. Note that McCormick is also a venture investor and sometimes writes about companies he backs; he discloses this. Good for understanding the bull case. |
| [Investor Amnesia](https://investoramnesia.com/) | Jamie Catherwood | Occasional, free | Financial history: bubbles, panics, and forgotten episodes, with primary sources. Posting slowed after 2024 but the archive and the "Sunday Reads" back catalogue are excellent. |
| [Value Investor Insight](https://valueinvestorinsight.com/) | Whitney Tilson and John Heins (founders) | Monthly, paid (from about $29/month) | Long interviews with value fund managers about their process and current ideas. The archive back to 2005 is a library of how good investors think. |

## How to use this list without drowning

- Pick two from the first section (Carlson or Batnick, plus Maggiulli or Piper), one from the macro section (Levine), and one link blog (Abnormal Returns). That is under 30 minutes a day.
- Add a valuation writer (Damodaran) only if you pick individual stocks.
- Use an RSS reader (Feedly, Inoreader, NetNewsWire) so the blogs come to you and the algorithms do not.
- Unsubscribe from anything that makes you want to trade. That is the test.

## Key takeaways

- The best free investing writing online comes from practitioners at firms that do not need to sell you anything (Ritholtz Wealth writers, Damodaran, AQR, Verdad, Alpha Architect).
- Matt Levine's Money Stuff is free despite Bloomberg's paywall and is the single most useful daily read for understanding how markets actually work.
- The Bogleheads wiki and Mike Piper answer most practical questions definitively; check them before reading opinions.
- Paid newsletters worth their price for serious investors: Stratechery (tech), The Diff (finance/tech), Net Interest (financials), Value Investor Insight (value managers). Grant's is for professionals.
- A link blog (Abnormal Returns) plus an RSS reader beats any social media feed for staying informed without being manipulated.
