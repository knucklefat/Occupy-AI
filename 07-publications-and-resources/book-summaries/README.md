# Book Summaries

**In one sentence:** Study-grade summaries of individual investing books — the frameworks, rules, and numbers each one teaches, written in our own words so the ideas are searchable and linkable from the rest of the library.

Each summary follows the book's structure, pulls out the mental models a reader is meant to walk away with, links them to the relevant files elsewhere in the library, and ends with the book's limits. They are not replacements for the books; each one links to where to borrow or buy the original.

## Summaries

- [One Up on Wall Street](one-up-on-wall-street.md) — Peter Lynch (1989). The amateur's edge, the six categories of stocks, the PEG rule, the two-minute drill, the numbers to check, and category-specific selling rules.

## Key takeaways

- Read the summary to decide whether the book deserves your time, and to retain the framework after you've read it.
- Where a summary's idea has better evidence elsewhere in the library, the summary links to it rather than repeating the book's claim uncritically.
