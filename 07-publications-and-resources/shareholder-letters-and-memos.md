# Shareholder Letters and Memos

**In one sentence:** The best free investing education on earth is written by practitioners reporting to their own investors, and this page tells you where each archive lives and which letter to open first.

Why letters? Books are edited for a general audience; letters are written by people with their own money on the line, to people whose money they manage, usually right after something went wrong. That makes them more honest than almost anything else in print. Every archive below was checked in September 2026. All are free unless marked.

## The core canon

### Berkshire Hathaway — Warren Buffett (1977–2024) and Greg Abel (2025–)
- **Archive:** [berkshirehathaway.com/letters/letters.html](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/letters.html) — every letter since 1977 as HTML or PDF. The site is plain text and looks like 1998; that is intentional.
- **Also:** [All letters in one PDF](https://uploads-ssl.webflow.com/60e3655ca778911eb64b2a00/60f0773bd7a92410fed4ccbb_All-Berkshire-Hathaway-Letters.pdf) (third-party compilation, useful for search) and the topic-organized book *The Essays of Warren Buffett* (see books file).
- **Read first:** 1983 (goodwill and economic vs. accounting earnings), 1987 (Mr. Market and the "Sainted Seven"), 1993 (risk vs. volatility, "Beta"), 2008 (what to do in a panic), and 2014 (the 50th anniversary letter on Berkshire's next 50 years). Buffett's 2024 letter, published February 2025, was his last as CEO before handing to Greg Abel.
- **Who it is for:** Everyone. Written for a general reader on purpose; Buffett said he imagines writing to his sisters.

### Buffett Partnership Letters (1957–1970)
- **Archive:** [Ivey Business School PDF](https://www.ivey.uwo.ca/media/2975913/buffett-partnership-letters.pdf); [Novel Investor's notes and index](https://novelinvestor.com/notes/buffett-partnership-letters-by-warren-buffett/).
- **Read first:** January 1962 ("The Ground Rules") and January 1967 (where he explains why the game is getting harder and lowers his targets).
- **Who it is for:** Anyone running a small, concentrated portfolio. This is Buffett before he was rich, hunting cigar butts and arbitrage, and it is far more applicable to a small investor than the modern Berkshire letters.

### Howard Marks — Oaktree Capital memos (1990–)
- **Archive:** [oaktreecapital.com/insights/memos](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memos) — every memo since 1990, plus audio versions and the "Behind the Memo" podcast.
- **Read first:** "The Most Important Thing" (2003), "Risk" (2006), "It's Not Easy" (2015), "You Bet!" (2020), and "Sea Change" (Dec 2022) on the end of the 40-year decline in rates. Then "Is It a Bubble?" (Dec 2025).
- **Who it is for:** Anyone who wants to think clearly about risk and cycles. Marks writes in plain English and almost never uses a number he does not need.

### Nomad Investment Partnership — Nick Sleep & Qais Zakaria (2001–2014)
- **Archive:** [Full collection PDF (IGY Foundation)](https://igyfoundation.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Full_Collection_Nomad_Letters_.pdf). Also published as a book by Stripe Press in 2025.
- **Read first:** The 2004 letters on "scale economies shared" (Costco, Amazon), then the final letter (2014) on why they closed a fund that had returned 20% a year.
- **Who it is for:** Long-term concentrated investors. Nomad held Amazon, Costco, and Berkshire for a decade and explained, in real time, why patience was the edge. The Costco letter alone has changed how thousands of people think about business quality.

### Jeff Bezos — Amazon shareholder letters (1997–2020) and Andy Jassy (2021–)
- **Archive:** [aboutamazon.com/about-us/shareholder-letters](https://www.aboutamazon.com/about-us/shareholder-letters) (recent letters) and [Amazon IR](https://ir.aboutamazon.com/annual-reports-proxies-and-shareholder-letters/default.aspx) (full archive with the annual reports).
- **Read first:** [1997](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/2020-letter-to-shareholders) (reprinted at the end of every subsequent letter: "It's all about the long term"), 2004 (why free cash flow per share beats earnings), 2014 (the three big ideas), and [2020](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/2020-letter-to-shareholders) (Bezos's last as CEO).
- **Who it is for:** Anyone analyzing a growth business. Bezos wrote more clearly about cash flow, customer obsession, and long-term thinking than most investors do.

### Jamie Dimon — JPMorgan Chase annual letters (2005–)
- **Archive:** [jpmorganchase.com/ir/annual-report](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/ir/annual-report) — letters 2015–2025 with page counts (they run 40–60 pages; the [2025 letter](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/ir/annual-report/2025/ceo-letter-to-shareholders) is 48).
- **Read first:** The most recent one, then 2020 (COVID and the banking system) and 2022 (geopolitics and "storm clouds").
- **Who it is for:** Macro-minded investors and anyone who owns bank stocks. Dimon is the rare CEO who writes candidly about the economy, regulation, and his own company's mistakes. Skim the corporate sections; read the "state of the world" section.

|---|---|---|---|
| **Markel Group** (Tom Gayner) | [mklgroup.com shareholder letters](https://www.mklgroup.com/who-we-are/shareholder-letters) — 1986 to 2025 | 2022 and 2024 | The "baby Berkshire" run by a thoughtful investor. | **Pershing Square Holdings** (Bill Ackman) | [pershingsquareholdings.com](https://pershingsquareholdings.com/) — annual and interim reports contain the letter; e.g. [2025 Annual Report](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/18175039/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-2025-Annual-Report.pdf) | The most recent annual report's letter | Concentrated, activist, and self-explanatory. ## Where to find letters that are not linked above

- [Dataroma](https://www.dataroma.com/) — tracks 80+ superinvestors' 13F holdings and links to letters where public.
- [Hedge Fund Alpha investor letters](https://hedgefundalpha.com/investor-letters/) and [Buyside Digest](https://www.buysidedigest.com/) — republish quarterly letters from Greenlight, Third Point, Akre, and dozens of smaller funds.
- [The Rational Walk](https://www.rationalwalk.com/) — Ravi Nagarajan's free site with summaries of Berkshire, Markel, and Semper Augustus letters each spring.
- [Novel Investor](https://novelinvestor.com/) — book and letter notes, investor quotes indexed by name.
- [Value Investing World](https://valueinvestingworld.substack.com/) — a long-running Substack that links to new fund letters, interviews, and articles as they appear (it is where the Semper Augustus letters get announced each year).

## Suggested reading plan

If you have one weekend: Berkshire 1983, 1987, 1993, 2008; Marks "Risk" and "Sea Change"; Nomad's Costco letters; Bezos 1997 and 2004.

If you have a month: Read all Berkshire letters from 1977 forward, one per evening. Then all Marks memos from 2000. Then Nomad start to finish. That is roughly 1,500 pages and a better education than most MBA finance tracks.

## Key takeaways

- Letters are written by people with their money on the line, to people whose money they manage, which makes them more honest than books.
- Start with the four archives that are complete and free: Berkshire, Oaktree memos, Nomad, and Amazon.
- The best letters explain mistakes (Giverny's "Podium of Errors," Watsa on deflation hedges, Marks on 2008); read those sections first.
- Constellation's Mark Leonard stopped writing because his letters were too useful to competitors — that is the standard the others are measured against.
- Hedge-fund letters move around online; Hedge Fund Alpha, Buyside Digest, and Dataroma are the aggregators to bookmark.
