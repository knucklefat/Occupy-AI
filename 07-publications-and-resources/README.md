# 07 — Publications and Resources

**In one sentence:** This section is a curated, annotated map of the best books, letters, newsletters, podcasts, data tools, papers, courses, communities, and official guides for investors — with every link checked, every entry explained, and a ten-item shortlist for people who want the most value for the least time.

There is more good investing material available for free today than a professional could have bought at any price thirty years ago: every Berkshire letter, Damodaran's entire MBA valuation course, the Fama-French factor data, the full text of every SEC filing since 2001, and thoughtful writers who publish weekly for nothing. The problem is no longer access; it is selection. Most "best resources" lists are affiliate-link farms or one person's unexamined favorites.

This section tries to be neither. Each entry was checked live in September 2026, carries a short note on why it is worth your time and who it suits, and is marked with its cost if it is not free. Where a famous resource has a real flaw — a house bias, a new paywall, a record of bad timing — the note says so. Pick two or three things from each file, use them for a year, and you will be better informed than someone who sampled everything.

## If you only read or follow 10 things

1. **[Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letters](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/letters.html)** — start with 1983, 1987, 1993, and 2008. Free, and the best investing education ever written for a general reader.
2. **[The Psychology of Money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Psychology_of_Money)** by Morgan Housel — one weekend, and it fixes the behavior that costs most investors more than any fee.
3. **[The Little Book of Common Sense Investing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Little_Book_of_Common_Sense_Investing)** by John Bogle — the plan most people should follow, in 200 pages.
4. **[Howard Marks's memos](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memos)** — read "Risk," "It's Not Easy," and "Sea Change," then subscribe. Free.
5. **[Money Stuff](https://www.bloomberg.com/account/newsletters/money-stuff)** by Matt Levine — the free daily newsletter that explains what actually happened in finance today, and is funny about it.
6. **[A Wealth of Common Sense](https://awealthofcommonsense.com/)** by Ben Carlson — the sanest regular commentary on markets for a non-professional. Free.
7. **[Damodaran's valuation course](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/webcastvalonline.htm)** — free, complete, and the only thing on this list you need if you want to value companies yourself.
8. **[Rational Reminder](https://rationalreminder.ca/)** podcast — the evidence on factors, fees, and retirement from the academics who produced it. Start with the episodes featuring Eugene Fama and Ken French.
9. **[Bogleheads wiki](https://www.bogleheads.org/wiki/Main_Page)** — the settled answers to practical questions (which account, what allocation, where to hold what for taxes). Check it before asking anyone.
10. **[FINRA BrokerCheck](https://brokercheck.finra.org/) and [IAPD](https://adviserinfo.sec.gov/)** — two minutes to check the record of anyone who wants to manage your money. Use them every time.

Honorable mentions, in case one of the ten does not suit you: Sharpe's three-page ["Arithmetic of Active Management"](https://web.stanford.edu/~wfsharpe/art/active/active.htm); the [Nomad Investment Partnership letters](https://igyfoundation.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Full_Collection_Nomad_Letters_.pdf); [Ben Felix's YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@BenFelixCSI); [FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/) for any macro fact; and [Portfolio Charts](https://portfoliocharts.com/) for testing an allocation for free.

## Key takeaways

- The best investing education is free: Berkshire and Oaktree letters, Damodaran's course, the academic data libraries, and a handful of practitioner blogs.
- Selection beats volume; pick two or three resources per file and use them for a year rather than sampling everything.
- Prefer sources with skin in the game or an academic record over sources with something to sell, and read each source's house bias against it.
- Official sources (Investor.gov, IRS publications, BrokerCheck, IAPD) are the final word on rules and records; use them before opinions.
- If a resource makes you want to trade more, it is entertainment; the ones on this list mostly make you want to trade less.
