# Seth Klarman

**In one sentence:** Klarman has run the Baupost Group for over 40 years as a risk-first value investor, willing to hold huge cash balances for years, hunt in obscure corners of the market where forced sellers create bargains, and write the out-of-print book (*Margin of Safety*) that value investors treat as scripture.

## Who he is

Seth Klarman (born 1957) studied at Cornell and Harvard Business School, worked for Max Heine and Michael Price at Mutual Shares, and in 1982 was hired by Harvard professor William Poorvu and three partners to run a family investment pool that became The Baupost Group. He has led it ever since, growing it into one of the largest hedge funds in the world (roughly $25–30 billion at its peak, largely from endowments and foundations). Baupost is notable for being long-biased, largely unleveraged, willing to invest across public equities, distressed debt, private assets, and real estate, and for returning capital to clients when it can't find bargains. Klarman's 1991 book *Margin of Safety* was printed in a small run, never reissued, and used copies sell for over a thousand dollars.

## Track record (verified, with caveats)

- **Baupost Group, 1982–present:** commonly reported as roughly **20% annualised over the fund's first three decades**, with only a handful of losing years. Source: [Wikipedia, The Baupost Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Baupost_Group), which relies on press reports. **This is the most important caveat in this file:** Baupost is private, reports only to clients, and the 20% figure has circulated for years without a primary public document behind it. Treat it as the consensus estimate, not a verified number.
- **Recent decade:** the same sources report far weaker results, roughly **4% a year from 2014 to 2024**, during which Baupost's assets fell as clients redeemed and Klarman held 30–50% in cash for long stretches.
- **What is verifiable:** Baupost's size (over $25 billion in the early 2020s), its long-only, low-leverage approach, and its consistently large cash positions, all of which appear in regulatory filings and client letters that have leaked to the press.

## Core philosophy

**Risk first, returns second.** Klarman's ordering is deliberate. Most investors ask how much they can make; Baupost asks how much it can lose. A permanent loss of 50% requires a 100% gain to recover, so the arithmetic favours avoiding losses over chasing gains.

**Margin of safety, extended.** Graham's idea, applied to every asset class. Klarman looks for investments where even a pessimistic analysis of value leaves room above the price. When no such investments exist, he holds cash, sometimes for years, and considers that a position rather than a failure.

**Where bargains come from.** Klarman's contribution to value investing is a taxonomy of *why* things get mispriced: forced selling by institutions that can't hold a downgraded bond or a spun-off small cap; index deletions; complexity that scares off analysts; illiquidity; distress; and plain neglect. He hunts where the seller has a reason other than value to sell.

**Bottom-up, absolute return.** Baupost doesn't measure itself against an index. It aims to make money in absolute terms over multi-year periods, which is why it can sit in cash while the S&P rallies without feeling pressure to chase.

**Catalysts.** Klarman prefers cheap assets with an identifiable event that will close the gap between price and value (a liquidation, a merger, a debt restructuring), because catalysts shorten the holding period and reduce dependence on the market's mood.

**Contrarianism with humility.** He argues that value investing is intellectually simple but psychologically brutal: you are buying what others are selling in a panic and you will look wrong for a long time before you look right.

## Rules you can steal

- **Cash is a position.** If nothing meets your standards, hold cash and wait. Opportunity cost is real but permanent loss is worse.
- **Ask why the seller is selling.** Best bargains come from sellers who don't care about value: index funds, forced liquidations, tax-loss sellers, panicked institutions.
- **Insist on a margin of safety in every asset class.** Bonds, real estate, and private deals need the same discipline as stocks.
- **Prefer catalysts.** An identifiable event that will surface value beats hoping the market notices.
- **Think in absolute terms.** "I lost less than the index" is still losing.
- **Avoid leverage.** It converts temporary declines into permanent losses.
- **Bottom-up over top-down.** Klarman does not build portfolios from macro views; he builds them one bargain at a time.
- **Be willing to look wrong for years.** Set your investors' (or your own) expectations for multi-year judgment, not quarters.
- **Return capital when there's nothing to buy.** For a personal investor, this means don't force money into the market because it's there.

## Best things he's written or said

- *Margin of Safety: Risk-Averse Value Investing Strategies for the Thoughtful Investor* (1991). Out of print; PDF copies circulate. The chapters on where bargains come from and on "the search for value" are the most useful.
- Preface to the sixth edition of Graham and Dodd's *Security Analysis* (2008): a concise 20-page summary of his worldview.
- [Seth Klarman on Masters in Business with Barry Ritholtz (2026)](https://ritholtz.com/2026/06/mib-seth-klarman/): a rare long interview.
- Baupost's annual letters, which are private but frequently quoted in the financial press.

## Criticisms and limitations

- **The record can't be checked.** The 20% figure is legend rather than data; the last decade's roughly 4% is well documented and poor.
- **Cash drag in long bull markets.** Holding 30–50% cash through 2012–2021 cost Baupost enormously relative to a simple index fund and led to significant client redemptions.
- **Scale.** At $30 billion, Baupost cannot buy the small, obscure bargains Klarman describes in his book; its opportunity set has shrunk to large distressed situations and private deals.
- **Not replicable in structure.** Individuals can hold cash and hunt for forced sellers, but cannot participate in bankruptcy negotiations or private-asset deals.
- **Contrarian macro warnings.** Klarman has warned about bubbles and policy risk almost continuously since 2010, which was correct in spirit and expensive in practice.

## Key takeaways

- Baupost's reported ~20% annual record over its first three decades is widely cited but not publicly verifiable; its recent decade (~4% a year) is verifiable and weak.
- Order your questions: how much can I lose, then how much can I make.
- Bargains come from sellers with non-value reasons to sell; hunt there.
- Cash is a legitimate position when nothing meets your standard, but the cost in long bull markets is real.
- Prefer investments with catalysts so you don't depend on the market's mood.
- Value investing is simple in theory and brutal in practice: expect to look wrong for years.
