# Crypto Glossary

**In one sentence:** About a hundred terms an investor will meet in crypto research, news, and exchange interfaces, each defined in a line or two, with cross-references to the fuller explanations elsewhere in this section.

## A

- **Address** — A string of letters and numbers derived from a public key; the "account number" you give someone to receive crypto.
- **Airdrop** — Free distribution of tokens to wallets, usually to reward early users or generate hype; often taxable as income.
- **Altcoin** — Any cryptocurrency other than bitcoin. See [Altcoins and Tokenomics](altcoins-and-tokenomics.md).
- **AMM (automated market maker)** — A smart contract that prices trades by formula against a pool of tokens instead of an order book (Uniswap). See [DeFi Explained](defi-explained.md).
- **APR / APY** — Annual percentage rate (simple) versus yield (compounded). DeFi quotes are often APY on an unsustainable rate.
- **ASIC** — Application-specific integrated circuit; a chip built solely to mine one algorithm (SHA-256 for bitcoin).
- **Attestation** — An accountant's snapshot confirmation that an issuer's reserves existed at one moment; weaker than an audit.

## B

- **Base fee** — The protocol-set portion of an Ethereum transaction fee, burned under EIP-1559.
- **Bear / bull market** — Extended down / up trend; crypto bears have historically been 75–90% drawdowns.
- **BIP / EIP** — Bitcoin / Ethereum Improvement Proposal; the formal process for protocol changes.
- **Block** — A batch of transactions added to the chain; every ~10 minutes on Bitcoin, ~12 seconds on Ethereum.
- **Block reward** — New coins paid to the miner or validator who adds a block (3.125 BTC as of 2026).
- **Blockchain** — A ledger of blocks linked by hashes and maintained by a distributed network. See [How Blockchains Work](how-blockchains-work.md).
- **Bridge** — A protocol that moves assets between blockchains by locking on one side and issuing wrapped tokens on the other; historically the most-hacked category.
- **Burn** — Permanently destroying tokens by sending them to an unspendable address; reduces supply.

## C

- **CEX (centralized exchange)** — A company-run exchange that custodies your funds (Coinbase, Binance, Kraken).
- **Cold storage / cold wallet** — Keys kept on a device never connected to the internet. See [Wallets, Keys, and Custody](wallets-keys-and-custody.md).
- **Collateral** — Assets pledged to back a loan or derivative position; DeFi loans are over-collateralized.
- **Confirmation** — Each block added after the one containing your transaction; six is the bitcoin convention for finality.
- **Consensus** — The rules by which nodes agree on the valid chain (proof of work, proof of stake).
- **Custodial** — An arrangement where a third party holds your private keys.

## D

- **DAO (decentralized autonomous organization)** — A group governed by token votes executed via smart contracts; typically controls a protocol's treasury.
- **DApp** — Decentralized application; a front-end for interacting with smart contracts.
- **DEX (decentralized exchange)** — An exchange run by smart contracts with no custodian (Uniswap, Curve, Hyperliquid).
- **DeFi** — Decentralized finance; lending, trading, and derivatives built as smart contracts.
- **Depeg** — When a stablecoin trades meaningfully away from its target price. See [Stablecoins](stablecoins.md).
- **Difficulty** — A network-adjusted number that keeps proof-of-work block times steady as hash power changes.
- **Double-spend** — Spending the same coin twice; the problem blockchain consensus exists to prevent.
- **Drawdown** — Peak-to-trough decline in price.

## E

- **EIP-1559** — The 2021 Ethereum upgrade that introduced the burned base fee.
- **ERC-20** — The Ethereum token standard; almost all tokens on Ethereum (including USDC and USDT) follow it.
- **ERC-721** — The Ethereum NFT standard.
- **ETF (spot crypto)** — A fund holding actual coins in custody, traded on a stock exchange; U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs launched January 2024.
- **Exploit** — An attack that abuses a flaw in smart-contract code or system design to drain funds.

## F

- **FDV (fully diluted valuation)** — Price × total supply, including tokens not yet released; the honest way to value a token.
- **Fee switch** — A governance decision to route a share of protocol fees to token holders.
- **Finality** — The point at which a transaction cannot be reversed; probabilistic on Bitcoin, explicit on Ethereum (~13 minutes).
- **Fork** — A protocol rule change; a contested hard fork splits the chain into two coins.
- **Funding rate** — Periodic payment between long and short holders of perpetual futures that anchors the perp to spot.

## G

- **Gas** — The unit of computational work on Ethereum; gas fees are paid in ETH.
- **GENIUS Act** — The July 2025 U.S. law regulating payment stablecoins (1:1 reserves, licensed issuers, no issuer-paid yield).
- **Governance token** — A token that confers voting rights over a protocol, often without any claim on revenue.
- **Gwei** — One billionth of an ETH; the unit gas prices are quoted in.

## H

- **Halving** — The scheduled 50% cut in bitcoin's block reward every 210,000 blocks (~4 years); next expected ~April 2028.
- **Hard fork** — A rule change incompatible with older software.
- **Hardware wallet** — A dedicated device that stores keys offline and signs transactions (Ledger, Trezor, Coldcard).
- **Hash** — A fixed-length cryptographic fingerprint of data; links blocks and drives proof-of-work mining.
- **Hashrate** — Total computing power securing a proof-of-work network (~930 EH/s for bitcoin in Sept 2026).
- **Hot wallet** — A wallet whose keys are on an internet-connected device.

## I

- **ICO / IDO / TGE** — Initial coin offering / initial DEX offering / token generation event; the moment a token first becomes tradable.
- **Impermanent loss** — The underperformance of a liquidity-pool position versus simply holding the assets when prices move.
- **Inflation (token)** — The rate at which new tokens are issued; staking "yield" is often just inflation.

## K

- **KYC** — Know-your-customer identity verification required by regulated exchanges.

## L

- **Layer 1 (L1)** — A base blockchain with its own consensus (Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana).
- **Layer 2 (L2)** — A network built on top of an L1 to process transactions cheaply and settle back to it (Arbitrum, Base, Lightning).
- **Lightning Network** — Bitcoin's payment-channel L2 for fast, cheap small payments.
- **Liquid staking** — Staking via a protocol that issues a tradable receipt token (Lido's stETH).
- **Liquidation** — Forced sale of collateral when a leveraged position falls below its maintenance threshold.
- **Liquidity pool** — Tokens deposited into an AMM contract so others can trade against them.
- **Low float** — A small share of total supply circulating at launch, implying heavy future unlocks.

## M

- **Market cap** — Price × circulating supply; understates true valuation when float is low.
- **Maxi (maximalist)** — Someone who believes only one chain (usually bitcoin) matters.
- **Memecoin** — A token with no function whose value is purely attention (DOGE, PEPE).
- **Mempool** — The waiting room of broadcast but not-yet-confirmed transactions.
- **The Merge** — Ethereum's September 2022 switch from proof of work to proof of stake.
- **MEV (maximal extractable value)** — Profit block producers can extract by reordering, inserting, or censoring transactions; a hidden tax on users.
- **Miner** — A participant who expends computing power to add blocks in proof of work.
- **Mining pool** — Miners combining hash power and splitting rewards; a few pools produce most bitcoin blocks.
- **Multisig** — A wallet requiring M of N keys to sign; the standard for treasuries and large holdings.

## N

- **NFT (non-fungible token)** — A unique token representing ownership of a specific digital or physical item.
- **Node** — A computer running blockchain software; a full node validates every block independently.
- **Nonce** — A number miners vary to search for a valid hash; also a transaction counter in Ethereum accounts.

## O

- **On-chain / off-chain** — Recorded on the blockchain versus handled elsewhere (an exchange's internal ledger, an L2).
- **Optimistic rollup** — An L2 that assumes transactions are valid and allows a challenge window (Arbitrum, Optimism, Base).
- **Oracle** — A service that feeds external data (prices) to smart contracts; Chainlink is the standard.
- **Order book** — A list of bids and asks; used by CEXs and Hyperliquid, not by AMMs.

## P

- **Peg** — The target price a stablecoin aims to hold (usually $1).
- **Perpetual futures (perps)** — Futures contracts with no expiry, kept near spot by funding rates; crypto's dominant derivative.
- **Private key** — The secret number that controls an address's funds; whoever holds it owns the coins.
- **Proof of stake (PoS)** — Consensus where validators lock coins as a bond and are penalized for misbehavior.
- **Proof of work (PoW)** — Consensus where miners expend energy on hashing to earn the right to add blocks.
- **Public key** — Derived from the private key; used to verify signatures and generate addresses.

## R

- **Real yield** — Returns paid from actual protocol fees in ETH or stablecoins, not from token emissions.
- **Rehypothecation** — Re-lending or re-pledging customer collateral; banned for GENIUS Act stablecoin reserves.
- **Rollup** — An L2 that executes transactions off-chain and posts compressed data plus proofs to the L1.
- **Rug pull** — A scam where developers drain a project's liquidity and vanish.
- **RWA (real-world assets)** — Tokenized versions of off-chain assets such as Treasuries, credit, or real estate.

## S

- **Satoshi (sat)** — One hundred-millionth of a bitcoin; also the pseudonym of bitcoin's creator.
- **Seed phrase** — 12 or 24 words that encode a wallet's master key; anyone with it controls the funds.
- **Sequencer** — The (usually single, centralized) computer that orders transactions on a rollup.
- **SHA-256** — The hash function bitcoin uses.
- **Slashing** — Destruction of a validator's staked coins as punishment for provable misbehavior.
- **Slippage** — The difference between expected and executed price on a trade, worse in thin liquidity.
- **Smart contract** — Code deployed on a blockchain that executes automatically when called. See [Ethereum and Smart Contracts](ethereum-and-smart-contracts.md).
- **Soft fork** — A backward-compatible rule tightening.
- **Stablecoin** — A token pegged to a fiat currency, ideally backed by cash and T-bills.
- **Staking** — Locking coins to help secure a proof-of-stake chain in exchange for rewards.
- **Supply (circulating / total / max)** — Tokens tradable now / all tokens created / the hard cap.

## T

- **Taproot** — Bitcoin's 2021 soft fork improving privacy and enabling more complex scripts.
- **Testnet** — A copy of a blockchain using worthless tokens, for development.
- **Token** — An asset issued on top of an existing blockchain (as opposed to a chain's native coin).
- **Tokenomics** — A token's supply, distribution, emission schedule, and value-accrual design.
- **TPS** — Transactions per second; a throughput measure that is easy to inflate with empty transactions.
- **TVL (total value locked)** — Dollar value of assets deposited in DeFi protocols, tracked by DefiLlama.
- **Two-factor authentication (2FA)** — A second login factor; use an authenticator app or hardware key, not SMS.

## U

- **Unlock** — Scheduled release of previously locked tokens (team, investor) into circulation; usually bearish.
- **UTXO** — Unspent transaction output; bitcoin's accounting model, in which "balances" are sums of discrete coins.

## V

- **Validator** — A participant who proposes and attests to blocks in proof of stake, with staked coins at risk.
- **Vesting** — The schedule by which allocated tokens become transferable; a "cliff" is an initial period with no release.

## W

- **Wallet** — Software or hardware that manages private keys and signs transactions; it does not "hold" coins.
- **Whale** — A holder large enough to move markets.
- **Whitepaper** — A project's founding technical document; Bitcoin's is nine pages from 2008.
- **Wrapped token** — A token on one chain representing an asset locked on another (WBTC is bitcoin on Ethereum); carries custodian or bridge risk.

## Z

- **ZK-rollup** — An L2 that proves transaction validity with zero-knowledge cryptography rather than a challenge window (zkSync, Starknet).
- **Zero-knowledge proof** — A cryptographic method of proving a statement is true without revealing the underlying data.

## Key takeaways

- The vocabulary splits into four families: how chains work (hash, block, consensus, node), how ownership works (key, seed, wallet, custody), how tokens are valued (FDV, float, unlock, emissions), and how DeFi works (AMM, TVL, liquidation, real yield).
- "Market cap" and "APY" are the two most misleading numbers in crypto; always ask for FDV and the source of the yield.
- Every custody term reduces to one question: who holds the private key?
- L1 versus L2 and rollup terms describe where a transaction is processed; the security always traces back to the base chain.
- Regulatory terms (GENIUS Act, attestation, KYC) increasingly determine which products are available to U.S. investors.
- When a term is unfamiliar, the fuller explanations are in the linked files of this section.
