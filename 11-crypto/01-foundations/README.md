# Crypto Foundations

**In one sentence:** This section explains how crypto actually works, from the mechanics of a blockchain to the economics of tokens, for an investor who wants to understand what they would be buying before deciding whether to buy any of it.

Crypto is the most contested asset class in this library. Its supporters see a new monetary system and a rebuilt financial stack; its critics see a speculative casino propped up by leverage and stablecoins of uncertain quality. Both camps make arguments worth understanding, and the goal here is to give you enough mechanical knowledge to evaluate them yourself rather than to persuade you either way.

The files build on one another. Start with how blockchains work, then bitcoin and Ethereum (the two assets that account for most of the market and have survived multiple cycles), then stablecoins (crypto's most-used product), then the far riskier world of altcoins and DeFi. The custody file is practical and matters the moment you own anything. The glossary is a reference for the rest.

All current figures are stamped "as of September 2026" and will age; the mechanisms will not.

## Key takeaways

- A blockchain is a shared ledger where ownership is a private key and rule changes require broad agreement; decentralization buys censorship resistance at the cost of speed and forgiveness.
- Bitcoin and Ethereum are the only two assets that have held their rank across multiple cycles; both have lost more than half their value repeatedly.
- Stablecoins are crypto's most-used product and, since the GENIUS Act, a regulated one; their supply is the cleanest measure of money entering or leaving the system.
- Most altcoins die: roughly 72% of tokens that ever reached the top 100 are defunct. Value tokens on FDV and read the unlock schedule.
- DeFi yields are either fees paid by users or dilution paid in printed tokens; know which one you are earning.
- The custody decision (ETF, exchange, or your own keys) matters more to most investors' outcomes than which coin they pick.
- The serious skeptical case (no cash flows, correlation to risk assets, reliance on leverage and Tether) deserves the same attention as the bull case.
