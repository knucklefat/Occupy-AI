# Scam Taxonomy

**In one sentence:** Crypto scams took an estimated $14–17 billion worldwide in 2025 and $11.4 billion from Americans alone (Chainalysis and FBI figures), and almost all of them run on the same three levers: a plausible story, manufactured urgency, and a payment rail that cannot be reversed.

## The scale, date-stamped

| Source | Period | Figure |
|---|---|---|
| Chainalysis 2026 Crypto Crime Report | calendar 2025 | ~$14B in confirmed on-chain scam receipts, projected to ~$17B as more addresses are identified; average scam payment $2,764, up from $782 in 2024 |
| Chainalysis | 2025 | Impersonation scams up ~1,400% year over year; AI-assisted scam operations earned ~4.5x more per operation than non-AI ones |
| Chainalysis | 2025 | Stablecoins were ~84% of illicit transaction volume; total illicit volume at least $154B (inflated by a sanctioned Russian stablecoin) |
| FBI IC3 Annual Report | 2025 | 181,565 crypto-related complaints, $11.37B in losses (+22%); crypto investment fraud alone $7.23B from 61,559 complaints; people over 60 lost $4.43B |
| FBI IC3 | 2025 | Recovery scams: 10,516 complaints, $1.4B; crypto ATM/kiosk scams: 13,460 complaints, $389M; romance/confidence: $929M |
| FTC Data Spotlight | Jan 2021 – Mar 2022 | $1B+ reported lost in crypto by 46,000 people; median loss $2,600; nearly half began on social media |

Reported figures understate the truth because victims are ashamed and because many losses are never traced on-chain.

## The taxonomy

Jargon: a **wallet drainer** is malicious code that empties a wallet once you sign a transaction; a **liquidity pool** is the pot of tokens that lets a coin be traded; **FDV** is fully diluted valuation, the price times all tokens that will ever exist.

| Scam | How it works | The tell | Who it targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rug pull** | Developers launch a token, attract buyers, then withdraw the liquidity pool or mint unlimited supply and sell. "Soft rugs" simply abandon the project. | Anonymous team, liquidity not locked, contract allows minting or blocks selling ("honeypot"), most supply in a few wallets. | Memecoin and DeFi buyers |
| **Pump-and-dump** | A group accumulates a thin coin, coordinates hype on Telegram/X/TikTok, sells into the retail buying they created. | Sudden volume on a coin nobody discussed last week; "insider" group chats promising the next 100x. | Small-cap traders |
| **Memecoin sniping and insider launches** | Launch platforms let anyone create a coin in minutes; insiders and bots buy in the first seconds and sell to the crowd. Celebrity-linked coins in early 2025 (the TRUMP token, Argentina's LIBRA) had most supply in insider wallets; LIBRA fell over 90% within hours of launch and analyses estimated hundreds of millions in retail losses. | Coin promoted by a famous person or politician; team controls most supply; price chart is a spike and a cliff. | Anyone on social media |
| **Pig butchering** | A stranger builds a relationship over weeks (dating app, "wrong number" text, LinkedIn), then introduces a "trading platform" that shows fake profits. Withdrawals are allowed at first, then blocked until you pay "taxes" or "fees." Run at industrial scale from compound operations in Southeast Asia; the largest crypto investment fraud category by dollars. | Unsolicited contact, romance or friendship that turns to investing, a platform you cannot find on any app store, profits too smooth. | Adults 30–70, often educated and financially secure |
| **Fake exchanges and apps** | Cloned websites or sideloaded apps that mirror real exchanges. Deposits go to the scammer. | URL is slightly wrong; app not in official store; support pushes you to deposit more to "unlock" funds. | New users |
| **Phishing and wallet drainers** | A fake airdrop, mint or support message asks you to "connect wallet" and sign a message; the signature grants token approvals that drain the wallet. Address poisoning sends dust from an address that looks like one you use, so you copy the wrong one. Chainalysis counted ~158,000 personal wallet compromises affecting ~80,000 victims (~$713M) in 2025. | Anything asking you to sign to "verify," "claim," or "recover"; urgency; a Discord DM from "support." | Active on-chain users |
| **Ponzi yield platforms** | Pay old investors with new deposits, dressed as a trading bot (BitConnect, ~$2.4B, 2018), a wallet app (PlusToken, China, 2019, roughly $2–3B), or a lending business paying 18% (Celsius, $4.7B owed, 2022). | Fixed high yields regardless of market; referral bonuses; vague explanation of where returns come from; withdrawals slow before they stop. | Yield-seekers, retirees |
| **Fake celebrity giveaways** | "Send 1 ETH, get 2 back" livestreams and posts using Musk, Saylor or exchange CEO likenesses; now often deepfake video. | Nobody doubles your money for sending it to them. Ever. | Everyone |
| **Romance scams** | Overlap with pig butchering; the relationship is the product and crypto is the extraction rail. IC3 logged $929M in romance/confidence losses in 2025. | Never meets in person; money needed for an emergency or "investment." | Lonely, widowed, isolated |
| **Recovery scams** | After a loss, "recovery agents," "law firms" or "blockchain forensics" contact you (often from the same criminals) promising to get funds back for an upfront fee. IC3: $1.4B in 2025. | Any recovery service that finds you first, or charges before results. Legitimate law enforcement never asks for crypto. | Prior victims |
| **AI-deepfake scams** | Cloned voices of relatives asking for urgent transfers; deepfake CEOs on video calls; AI-written pig-butchering scripts. Chainalysis found AI-enabled operations far more profitable per operation in 2025. | Urgency plus a request to pay in crypto or via a crypto ATM; refusal to use a known callback number. | Older adults, employees |
| **Crypto ATM scams** | Impersonating government, utilities or tech support, the caller directs the victim to feed cash into a crypto kiosk and scan a QR code. $389M in 2025 (IC3), rising fast. | No agency collects fines in bitcoin. | Older adults |

## The shared mechanism

Every scam above exploits the same features of crypto that its advocates prize: transactions are final, custody is self-directed, and there is no bank fraud department to call. Add social media (where the FTC found nearly half of crypto fraud starts) and the scam industry gets free distribution. The fastest-growing categories in 2025 were impersonation and AI-assisted fraud, both of which lower the criminal's cost per victim.

The counterintuitive point: sophistication does not protect you. Pig-butchering victims are disproportionately professionals; drainer victims are active DeFi users who signed something in a hurry. What protects you is process.

## Countermeasures / How to apply

- Treat any unsolicited financial contact as a scam by default, including from "friends" whose accounts may be compromised. Verify through a second channel you already had.
- Never sign a wallet transaction you did not initiate from a site you typed in yourself. Use a separate "hot" wallet with small balances for minting and airdrops; keep long-term holdings in a wallet that never connects to anything.
- Before buying a small token: is liquidity locked, can the contract mint or freeze, what share of supply do the top ten wallets hold? If you cannot check, do not buy.
- Fixed yields above roughly the risk-free rate plus a few points require a clearly explained source of return that does not depend on new deposits. If the explanation is "trading bot," "arbitrage" or "proprietary strategy," walk away.
- No celebrity is giving away crypto. No government agency accepts bitcoin fines. No recovery firm that contacts you first is legitimate.
- Establish a family rule: any urgent money request by phone or video is confirmed by calling back on a known number, with a pre-agreed safe word.
- If you are scammed: stop sending money, document everything, report to IC3 (US) or your national equivalent, and expect no recovery. Then beware the recovery-scam wave that follows.

## Key takeaways

- Global crypto scam revenue was ~$14–17B in 2025; US victims reported $11.4B in crypto-related losses to the FBI.
- Pig butchering is the largest category by dollars; it targets competent adults, not the naive.
- Impersonation and AI-assisted scams were the fastest-growing categories in 2025.
- Older Americans bear the biggest losses: $4.4B in crypto fraud for the 60+ group in 2025.
- Rug pulls, honeypots and insider memecoin launches are structural features of permissionless token creation, not rare events.
- Recovery scams re-victimize; anyone who contacts you offering to recover funds is a scammer.
- Finality of transactions means prevention is the only defense; there is no chargeback.
- Process beats intelligence: separate wallets, second-channel verification, and a hard "no" to unsolicited offers.
