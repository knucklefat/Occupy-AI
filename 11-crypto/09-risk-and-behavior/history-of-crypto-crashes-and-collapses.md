# History of Crypto Crashes and Collapses

**In one sentence:** Crypto has produced a major crash roughly every 18–36 months since 2011, and almost every one was some combination of leverage, a counterparty that was quietly insolvent, and a yield that was too good to be true.

## Why study the wreckage

Every cycle brings a new crop of investors who have never seen a 75% drawdown and a new set of institutions that promise "this time the plumbing is safer." The pattern below is not a prediction, but it is a base rate. If you own crypto, you should assume something on this list will happen again while you own it, and decide in advance how you will behave.

Jargon, defined once: a **drawdown** is the fall from a peak to the next trough. **Leverage** means trading with borrowed money; a **liquidation** is the exchange force-closing a leveraged position when losses eat the trader's collateral. A **depeg** is a stablecoin trading away from the $1 it promises. **Contagion** is one firm's failure causing losses at the firms that lent to it.

## The timeline

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Feb 2014 | Mt. Gox halts withdrawals and files for bankruptcy | ~850,000 BTC missing (200,000 later found); the exchange handled ~70% of BTC volume | ~650,000 BTC lost; 127,000 creditors; repayments began only in July 2024 | An exchange is a counterparty. "Not your keys, not your coins" was born here. |
| Jan 2018 – Dec 2018 | The ICO bust | Thousands of tokens sold on whitepapers; most had no product | BTC −84% from Dec 2017 peak; ETH −94%; typical ICO token −90% or worse | New-issue manias end the same way in every asset class. |
| Jan 2018 | BitConnect shuts down | "Trading bot" promising ~1% per day was a Ponzi | ~$2.4B (DOJ/SEC) | Guaranteed daily yield is a confession, not a product. |
| 12–13 Mar 2020 | "Black Thursday" | COVID panic hit a leveraged market; BitMEX liquidation engine and MakerDAO auctions failed under load | BTC roughly halved in two days (about $7,900 to under $4,000) | Crypto is a risk asset; it does not hedge a global liquidity panic. |
| Apr–Jul 2021 | China mining ban, leverage flush | Hashrate fell by roughly half within weeks; overleveraged longs liquidated on 19 May 2021 | BTC ~$64,900 (Apr) to ~$29,000 (Jul), about −55% | Regulatory shocks are real; network survived, prices did not. |
| 7–13 May 2022 | Terra/Luna collapse | Algorithmic stablecoin UST depegged; LUNA minted to infinity; ~19.5% Anchor yield had attracted ~$18B | ~$40–45B of market value erased in a week | A stablecoin backed by its own sister token is a bank with no assets. |
| Jun–Jul 2022 | Three Arrows Capital (3AC), Celsius, Voyager | 3AC (claimed ~$10B AUM) defaulted after Terra and the GBTC discount; it owed ~$3.5B. Celsius ($4.7B owed) and Voyager ($666M lent to 3AC) froze withdrawals and filed | Millions of customers became unsecured creditors | Yield platforms were re-lending your coins to hedge funds. |
| 6–11 Nov 2022 | FTX collapse | Customer deposits (~$8B hole) had been lent to sister fund Alameda | Second-largest exchange gone in a week; BlockFi and Genesis followed | Audited financials, celebrity backing and regulatory lobbying proved nothing about solvency. |
| 10–13 Mar 2023 | SVB failure, USDC depeg | Circle had $3.3B of reserves at Silicon Valley Bank; USDC traded to ~$0.87 | Weekend of panic; peg restored after FDIC backstop | Even "fully reserved" stablecoins carry bank risk. |
| 5 Aug 2024 | Yen carry-trade unwind | Global deleveraging hit crypto first (24/7 markets); BTC briefly near $49,000 from ~$70,000 in late July; ETH −20% in a day; roughly $1B+ liquidated | Sharp, short | Crypto is where the world sells first on weekends and holidays. |
| 21 Feb 2025 | Bybit hack | Compromised signing interface; North Korea's Lazarus Group stole ~$1.5B of ETH | Largest theft in crypto history (Chainalysis) | Even large, solvent exchanges can lose everything in one transaction. |
| 10 Oct 2025 | The "10/10" liquidation cascade | Tariff headline hit record open interest; $19B of leveraged positions closed in 24h, 1.6M accounts liquidated, $3.2B in a single minute; USDe traded to $0.65 on Binance; top-100 tokens averaged roughly −58% intraday wicks | Open interest fell from ~$217B to ~$123B | Leverage plus exchange-specific pricing turned a 14% BTC move into a total wipeout for altcoin longs. |
| Oct 2025 – 2026 | Bear market and shakeout | BTC ~$126,000 intraday peak (October 6, 2025) to a roughly $58,500 daily-average low (July 1, 2026); eight straight weeks of ETF outflows totaling over $8B; corporate treasuries and miners sold; ~$1B of exploits in H1 2026 (Drift ~$295M on April 1, Kelp DAO ~$293M on April 18, per DefiLlama); over 100 projects shut down by Aug 2026 | About −53% for BTC (daily-average basis); 70–90% for many altcoins | Token-funded businesses die when the token falls; ETFs brought new flows in and took them out just as fast. |

Figures as of September 2026; treat the 2026 low as provisional since the bear market may not have ended.

Ether:

| Cycle | Peak (approx.) | Trough (approx.) | Drawdown |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2018 | ~$1,430 (Jan 2018) | ~$82 (Dec 2018) | ~−94% |

Two things stand out. First, the drawdowns have been getting shallower as the asset has grown, but "shallower" still means losing half or more. Second, ETH and everything smaller has fallen harder than BTC in every cycle; the 2025–26 shakeout repeated this with altcoins down 70–90%.

## The recurring mechanics

**Leverage amplifies everything.** Black Thursday, May 2021, August 2024 and October 2025 were all fundamentally the same event: too many borrowed longs, a headline, and liquidation engines selling into a market where market makers had pulled their bids. The October 2025 episode added a new wrinkle: exchanges pricing collateral (like USDe or wrapped staked ETH) from their own thin order books rather than external feeds, so a local depeg triggered liquidations that had nothing to do with the asset's real value.

**Yield without a visible source is borrowed from the next depositor.** BitConnect (2018), Anchor's 19.5% on UST (2022), Celsius and Voyager's "up to 18%" (2022), and the 12% USDe promotions before October 2025 all paid returns that no legitimate activity could generate at that scale.

**Contagion runs through opaque lending.** Terra's collapse killed 3AC, 3AC's default killed Voyager, weakened Celsius and BlockFi, and the same lenders were then hit by FTX. None of the customers knew their coins were three loans deep.

**Regulation and macro matter more than crypto wants to admit.** China's ban, SVB's failure, the tariff shock and 2026's ETF outflows were all exogenous. Crypto's 24/7 trading makes it the first place the world deleverages.

## Countermeasures / How to apply

- Assume a 50–80% drawdown in BTC and 80–95% in anything smaller over any multi-year holding period, and size the position so that outcome is survivable. See [risk-management-rules-for-crypto.md](risk-management-rules-for-crypto.md).
- Never hold leveraged long positions overnight or over weekends in crypto. Every cascade above happened at a time of thin liquidity.
- Treat any yield above short-term Treasury rates plus a few points as a counterparty risk you are underwriting, not "interest."
- Keep the majority of long-term holdings off exchanges. Every exchange failure on this list took customers' coins with it.
- Write down your crash behavior now, using the main [market crash playbook](../../10-checklists-and-templates/market-crash-playbook.md), and add a crypto clause: no adding to altcoins during a cascade, no "buying the dip" with borrowed money.

## Key takeaways

- Major crashes have arrived every 1.5–3 years; a base rate, not an anomaly.
- BTC has lost 77–93% in each completed cycle; ETH and altcoins lost more.
- Leverage plus thin weekend liquidity is the engine of every flash crash; October 2025's $19B liquidation is the current record.
- Every exchange, lender and stablecoin on the list looked healthy until days before it failed.
- Unsustainable yield preceded every Ponzi-like collapse: BitConnect, Terra, Celsius.
- Contagion spreads through re-lending that customers cannot see.
- The 2025–26 bear market added new failure modes: ETF outflows, token-funded businesses, and treasury-company selling.
- Survival is a sizing decision made before the crash, not a trading decision made during it.
