# The Retail Loss Problem

**In one sentence:** Bitcoin has been one of the best-performing assets of the past fifteen years, and the best available evidence still says that most of the people who bought it lost money, because they bought when prices were rising and sold, or were sold to, when prices fell.

## The headline evidence

The most careful study is from the Bank for International Settlements (BIS), the central banks' research arm. Its 2022 working paper built a database of crypto exchange app downloads and usage across 95 countries from 2015 to 2022 and matched it against prices.

| Finding (BIS Working Paper 1049, Nov 2022; Bulletin 69, Feb 2023) | Detail |
|---|---|
| Most users lost money | Assuming each user bought on the day they downloaded an app, an estimated **73–81%** of retail users worldwide were sitting on losses on their bitcoin by late 2022 |
| Price drives adoption, not the other way around | New users arrived after price rises; two natural experiments (China's mid-2021 mining ban, Kazakhstan's early-2022 unrest) confirmed the causal direction |
| Who bought | Roughly **40% of new users were men under 35**, the most risk-seeking demographic |
| Who sold to whom | After the Terra collapse (May 2022) and FTX (Nov 2022), the largest holders ("whales") reduced positions while the smallest holders ("shrimps") bought, so retail absorbed the losses and the exit liquidity |
| Systemic impact | The 2022 crash had little effect on the broader financial system; the losses were concentrated in households |

The mechanism is the important part. Retail crypto adoption behaves like "feedback trading": people buy because the price went up, which is the definition of buying high. Meanwhile large holders, who bought years earlier, use those inflows to sell.

## Corroborating evidence

- **Chainalysis** has repeatedly found that new-user growth and web traffic to exchanges spike after major price rallies, and that realized gains in bull years are heavily concentrated among early holders in a few countries.
- **FTC and FBI data** show the losses that never appear in price charts: US consumers reported $11.4B in crypto-related fraud losses in 2025 alone (IC3), on top of investment losses.
- **Academic studies of trading behavior** in stocks (Barber and Odean's work on overconfident retail traders, the "behavior gap" between fund returns and investor returns) apply with extra force to an asset that trades 24/7 with 50–100x leverage available. Retail perpetual-futures traders on major exchanges are, by the exchanges' own disclosures in several jurisdictions, net losers in the large majority.
- **The 2025–26 cycle** repeated the pattern in a new wrapper: spot Bitcoin ETFs made buying easy, inflows surged into the October 2025 top around $126,000, and then eight consecutive weeks of outflows exceeding $8B in aggregate accompanied the slide toward $58,000. Meanwhile the October 10, 2025 cascade liquidated 1.6 million leveraged accounts in one day.

## Where the gains went

Bitcoin's supply is extremely concentrated by the standards of any asset. Early miners, early adopters, and a small number of corporate and fund holders own a large share of the coins that will ever exist. When a new wave of buyers arrives, the coins they buy come from someone, and on net that someone is an earlier, larger holder. The BIS data show this directly in the 2022 crashes.

This is not a conspiracy; it is arithmetic. In an asset with no cash flow, one holder's realized gain is another holder's purchase price. Returns are a function of *when* you bought relative to everyone else, and the crowd, by construction, arrives late.

| Buyer cohort (approx.) | Entry price range | Outcome by Sep 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-2017 | under $1,000 | Extraordinary gains even after a 50% drawdown |
| Late 2017 top | ~$15,000–20,000 | Underwater for three years, then large gains if held |
| 2021 top | ~$50,000–69,000 | Underwater for two years, then modest gains at 2026 prices |
| Q4 2025 top | ~$100,000–126,000 | Down 40–50% as of mid-2026 |
| Altcoin buyers at any top | varies | Most tokens 80–95% below prior highs; many defunct |

The table shows the second half of the problem: "just hold" worked for bitcoin if you survived the drawdown *and* you were holding bitcoin rather than the coin that was popular that year. The 2018 ICO tokens, the 2021 NFT and "alt L1" boom, and the 2024–25 memecoins largely never recovered.

## Why "number go up" captures people

- **Visibility of winners.** Social media shows the person who turned $5,000 into $500,000. It does not show the 80% who lost, because losing is silent. See the survivorship-bias entry in the main library's [cognitive biases field guide](../../09-behavioral-finance/cognitive-biases-field-guide.md).
- **Price as the only signal.** With no earnings or yield, price *is* the narrative. A rising price feels like evidence the thesis is right; a falling price feels like a discount. Both feelings are wrong as often as right.
- **Unit bias and low price points.** A coin at $0.0001 "only needs to reach a penny." Market cap, not price per unit, determines the required inflow, but the brain works in units.
- **Regret and FOMO.** Everyone knows someone who "would have" been rich. The pain of a missed gain is a stronger motivator than the fear of an actual loss, right up until the loss happens.
- **Frictionless access.** Apps, ETFs and 24/7 markets remove the pause that used to sit between impulse and trade.

## Trading versus holding

Across every dataset that separates the two, the pattern is the same: frequent traders underperform holders, leveraged traders underperform unleveraged, and holders of the top one or two assets outperform holders of a rotating basket. The reasons are mechanical (fees, spreads, funding rates, liquidations) and behavioral (buying strength, selling weakness).

| Approach | Typical outcome in the evidence | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| Leveraged perpetual trading | Large majority lose; October 2025 alone liquidated $19B | Ruin |
| Frequent spot trading of altcoins | Underperforms holding BTC/ETH; high tax drag | Slow bleed plus occasional rug |
| Buy-and-hold BTC, bought at a top | Underwater 2–3 years, then recovers in every completed cycle so far | Selling at the bottom; a cycle that does not recover |
| Dollar-cost averaging BTC/ETH over a full cycle | Positive in every completed cycle to date | Concentration; regulatory or technical shock |
| Holding through an exchange or yield account | Same as above minus counterparty failure | Losing everything for reasons unrelated to price |

Note the honest caveat in the third and fourth rows: "every completed cycle so far" is a sample of four. It is a base rate, not a law.

## Countermeasures / How to apply

- Decide your allocation in a calm moment and write it down; do not increase it because the price rose. Rising price is the signal that you are late, not early.
- Buy on a schedule (monthly, for example) across a full cycle rather than in a lump when the news is exciting. This is the only retail behavior with consistently positive outcomes in the data.
- Hold the largest, oldest assets for the core. Treat everything else as speculative with a separate, small budget you can lose entirely.
- Never use leverage. Leveraged retail traders are net losers in every dataset.
- Measure your own results honestly: track cost basis, fees and taxes, and compare to simply holding BTC or a broad stock index. Most people who do this discover their trading subtracted value.
- Read [the behavior gap](../../09-behavioral-finance/the-behavior-gap.md) in the main library; crypto is the behavior gap with the volume turned up.

## Key takeaways

- BIS estimated 73–81% of retail bitcoin app users worldwide were losing money by late 2022, because adoption follows price rises.
- Around 40% of new buyers were men under 35; the demographics of risk-seeking, not of long-term investing.
- In the 2022 crashes, large holders sold while small holders bought; retail was the exit liquidity.
- Gains are concentrated among early and large holders by arithmetic: no cash flow means one holder's gain is another's entry price.
- "Just hold" worked for BTC in every completed cycle, but not for most altcoins, and only if you survived a 50–90% drawdown without selling.
- Leverage and frequent trading are net losers for retail; dollar-cost averaging into the largest assets is the only approach with consistently positive evidence.
- The 2025–26 cycle repeated the pattern through ETFs: record inflows at the top, record outflows on the way down.
- Your allocation should be set by what you can afford to lose, not by what you might gain.
