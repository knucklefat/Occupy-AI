# Crypto Market Psychology

**In one sentence:** Crypto takes every bias catalogued in behavioral finance and removes the frictions that used to slow them down: no closing bell, leverage on demand, a price per coin designed to look cheap, and a social feed that rewards conviction over accuracy.

The main library's [cognitive biases field guide](../../09-behavioral-finance/cognitive-biases-field-guide.md), [prospect theory and loss aversion](../../09-behavioral-finance/prospect-theory-and-loss-aversion.md) and [market bubbles and manias](../../09-behavioral-finance/market-bubbles-and-manias.md) cover the underlying psychology. This file covers what crypto adds.

## The environment is the problem

| Feature of crypto markets | Bias it amplifies | Observable result |
|---|---|---|
| Trades 24/7/365 | Checking compulsion, recency bias | Price-app opens before coffee; weekend and 3 a.m. decisions |
| Leverage of 20–125x available to anyone in minutes | Overconfidence, house-money effect | Oct 10, 2025: 1.6M accounts liquidated in a day; $19B gone |
| Prices quoted per coin with no fundamentals to anchor | Unit bias, anchoring | "$0.0002 only needs to hit a penny" |
| Tokens created in minutes; thousands per day | Lottery-ticket preference, salience | Memecoin launches; 100+ projects dead in 2026 |
| Influencers paid in the tokens they promote | Authority bias, social proof | Pump culture; celebrity coins collapsing within hours (LIBRA, Feb 2025) |
| Ideological communities around each chain | Tribalism, confirmation bias | "Maximalism"; muting critics; holding through 90% drawdowns |
| Four-year halving narrative | Pattern-seeking, narrative fallacy | "This cycle is different" every cycle, in both directions |
| No cash flows | Price is the only signal | Rising price reads as validation; falling price reads as a discount |

## Always-on markets and checking frequency

A stock investor gets 6.5 hours of trading and two closed days a week. A crypto holder gets none of that. Studies of frequent portfolio checking (the "myopic loss aversion" literature) show that the more often you look, the more losses you see (losses hurt roughly twice as much as equivalent gains feel good), and the more likely you are to act. Crypto's volatility makes every glance a coin flip with an emotional charge. The practical consequence: many holders report worse sleep, more impulsive trades, and a growing share of decisions made on a phone in the small hours, exactly when liquidity is thinnest and moves are most violent (the October 2025 cascade peaked late on a Friday evening in the US).

## Leverage and liquidation psychology

Leverage transforms a volatile asset into a binary one. At 20x, a 5% move against you erases the position; at 100x, a 1% move does. A **liquidation** is not a stop-loss you chose; it is the exchange selling your collateral at the worst possible moment, often into a cascade of others being liquidated at the same time. The psychology is a trap: a few early wins on leverage feel like skill (the house-money effect), losses get "revenge traded" at higher leverage, and the funding fees on perpetual futures quietly bleed the position between moves. Exchanges know this; leverage is the product, and the liquidation engine is a revenue line. The October 10, 2025 event showed one more layer: collateral priced from an exchange's own thin order book (USDe at $0.65 on one venue while ~$1 elsewhere) liquidated traders who were not even wrong about the market.

## FOMO and unit bias

Fear of missing out is the emotion that follows a rising price, and unit bias is the cognitive shortcut that makes small numbers look like opportunity. A coin at $0.001 with 100 trillion supply has the same market cap as a coin at $100 with a billion supply; the required inflow to double is identical. But "I can own a million of them" and "it only needs to go to a penny" are the stories that sell memecoins to newcomers. Combine unit bias with an influencer promising "the next Dogecoin" and you have the standard pump funnel.

## Influencer and pump culture

Crypto is unusual in that the people promoting an asset are frequently paid in that asset, so their incentive is to sell it to you and exit. This is disclosed rarely, enforced weakly, and packaged as community. The tells: guaranteed targets, "this is not financial advice" followed by financial advice, screenshots of gains without cost basis, urgency ("last chance before listing"), and hostility toward anyone asking where the returns come from. Celebrity and political tokens in early 2025 compressed the whole cycle into hours, with insider wallets holding most supply.

## Tribalism and maximalism

Each major chain has a community that defines itself against the others. Tribal identity is psychologically rewarding and analytically ruinous: it makes selling feel like betrayal, criticism feel like attack, and a 90% drawdown feel like loyalty. Maximalists were right about some things (most altcoins did go to zero) and wrong about others (dismissing every risk to their own asset), which is exactly what tribal reasoning produces: correct about the out-group, blind about the in-group.

## "This cycle is different"

Every bull market has a reason the old drawdowns will not recur: institutional adoption (2017 futures, 2021 corporate treasuries, 2024 ETFs), regulatory clarity, the halving. Every bear market has a reason it is over forever: the Mt. Gox, ICO, Terra/FTX and 2026 shakeout narratives. Both are versions of the same narrative fallacy. The 2025–26 cycle was a clean demonstration: ETF flows were supposed to dampen volatility, and instead concentrated it, with record inflows near the top and eight straight weeks of outflows on the way down. The honest position is that the base rate (large drawdowns every cycle, altcoins worse than BTC) has held for four cycles, and "different this time" needs evidence, not a story.

## Countermeasures

| Problem | Countermeasure |
|---|---|
| Checking compulsion | Delete price apps from the phone; check a fixed number of times per week; price alerts only at pre-set action levels |
| Leverage | A written rule: no leverage, no exceptions; if you must trade, use only spot and a separate small account |
| FOMO | A cooling-off rule: any new purchase waits 72 hours from the moment you first wanted it; buy on a calendar schedule, not on news |
| Unit bias | Always convert to market cap and required inflow before buying anything small; ask "who is selling me this?" |
| Influencers | Assume every promoter is paid in the token; mute anyone who posts price targets; get information from sources that would be embarrassed to be wrong |
| Tribalism | Deliberately read the best critics of what you own (Molly White's timeline is one place to start); if a critique makes you angry rather than curious, that is data |
| Narrative fallacy | Keep a decision journal with the thesis, the price, and what would make you wrong; review it in the next cycle |
| Sleep and health | Hard rule: no trades between 10 p.m. and 8 a.m. local, and none on weekends; those windows are where cascades and regret live |
| Loss aversion after a drawdown | Pre-commit the crash plan (see [risk-management-rules-for-crypto.md](risk-management-rules-for-crypto.md) and the main [market crash playbook](../../10-checklists-and-templates/market-crash-playbook.md)) |

## Key takeaways

- Crypto does not create new biases; it removes the frictions that contained the old ones.
- 24/7 trading plus loss aversion means frequent checking leads directly to worse decisions.
- Leverage turns volatility into ruin; October 2025's $19B liquidation in one day is the reference case.
- Unit bias ("only needs to reach a penny") is the engine of memecoin losses; think in market cap and required inflows.
- Promoters are usually paid in the token they promote; treat every price target as an advertisement.
- Tribal identity makes selling feel like betrayal and blinds holders to their own asset's risks.
- "This cycle is different" has been said in every cycle; the base rate of 50–90% drawdowns has held in all four.
- The countermeasures are all pre-commitments: rules written when calm that remove decisions when not.
