# Sentiment Indicators

**In one sentence:** Sentiment indicators measure how the crowd feels, and their only reliable use is contrarian at the extremes: "extreme fear" has been a good time to buy on a 6-to-12-month view, "extreme greed" a good time to trim, and everything in between is noise.

## What sentiment can and cannot do

Crypto is a retail-heavy, narrative-driven market, so crowd psychology matters more than in bonds or large-cap equities. But sentiment is a coincident indicator: it describes what price just did. Its predictive value comes only at extremes, where the crowd has already acted (everyone who was going to panic-sell has sold) and the marginal flow reverses. Two rules follow. First, only act on readings in the top or bottom 10 to 15% of history. Second, extremes can persist for weeks in strong trends, so scale in rather than going all-in on the first "extreme fear" print.

## Crypto Fear & Greed Index (alternative.me)

**What it measures.** A daily 0-to-100 composite published by alternative.me since 2018. Components and weights as stated on its methodology page: volatility (25%, current volatility and drawdowns versus 30- and 90-day averages), market momentum and volume (25%), social media (15%, X/Twitter engagement on Bitcoin hashtags), surveys (15%, currently paused), Bitcoin dominance (10%, rising dominance read as fear), and Google Trends (10%). Below 25 is "extreme fear", above 75 "extreme greed". Note that it is a Bitcoin index; altcoin sentiment can differ.

**Where to get it free.** [alternative.me Fear & Greed Index](https://alternative.me/crypto/fear-and-greed-index/) (chart, history and a free API), [CoinMarketCap Fear & Greed](https://coinmarketcap.com/charts/fear-and-greed-index/) (its own version), [BitDegree tracker](https://www.bitdegree.org/cryptocurrency-prices/fear-and-greed-index).

**How to read it.** Since two of the six inputs are effectively price momentum and volatility, the index mostly re-describes the last 30 days of price. That is fine: its job is to tell you where in the emotional range the crowd sits. Historical extremes: the index printed single digits and low teens at the March 2020 crash, the June 2022 and November 2022 lows, and in the low 20s during the February and summer 2026 drawdowns. It spent weeks above 85 in February 2021, November 2021 and briefly in late 2024. Each of those clusters, in hindsight, was near a major turning point on a 6-to-12-month horizon.

**As of September 2026.** The index printed 56 ("Greed") on September 11, down from 69 the day before and 74 a week earlier, and up from 27 ("Fear") a month earlier. That is a full swing from fear to greed in five weeks, matching the 25% August rally, and the quick drop from 69 to 56 in one day reflects a price wobble around $77,000 to $80,000. Neither extreme; no contrarian signal either way.

**Track record.** Buying when the index is under 20 and holding a year has been profitable in every instance since 2018, though drawdowns of a further 20 to 30% occurred after several of those buys before recovery. Selling above 85 would have missed part of the 2021 and 2024 runs but avoided most of the subsequent declines. Studies of the index as a short-term trading signal find little edge; it is a positioning tool.

## Google Trends

**What it measures.** Relative search interest for terms like "bitcoin", "buy bitcoin", "crypto" or "ethereum", indexed to 100 at the peak of the selected window.

**Where to get it free.** [Google Trends: "bitcoin", worldwide, 5 years](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?date=today%205-y&q=bitcoin), [Google Trends: "buy bitcoin"](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?date=today%205-y&q=buy%20bitcoin).

**How to read it.** Search interest spikes when mainstream attention arrives, which is late in a rally or during a crash (people search during scary drops too, so check the direction of price). The December 2017 and May 2021 peaks in "bitcoin" searches coincided almost exactly with price tops. The striking feature of 2024–25 was how little search interest rose despite record prices: the all-time high near $126,000 in October 2025 came with search volume a fraction of the 2021 peak, one of the clearest signs that the ETF-era buyer is not the retail searcher. Watch the 5-year view; a reading above 50 on that scale has not been seen since 2021.

**Track record.** Excellent at flagging retail-driven blow-offs in 2013, 2017 and 2021. Its silence in 2025 means it will not flag an institution-driven top; it still works for retail-driven ones.

## App-store rankings

**What it measures.** The rank of Coinbase, Robinhood, Binance, Crypto.com or Phantom in Apple's App Store and Google Play overall (not just finance) charts. A top-10 overall ranking means the general public is downloading a crypto app in large numbers.

**Where to get it free.** [Apple App Store top free apps (US)](https://apps.apple.com/us/charts/iphone/top-free-apps/36), [Google Play top charts](https://play.google.com/store/apps/top), [Sensor Tower free rank history (limited)](https://app.sensortower.com/), community trackers on X that post daily Coinbase rank.

**How to read it.** Coinbase hit number 1 overall in the US App Store in May 2021 (weeks after the April top), briefly in November 2021 (the cycle top), and again around the November 2024 election rally. A top-5 overall rank is a clear "retail is here" flag; a rank outside the top 100 is normal in quiet markets. As of September 2026 crypto apps are not in the top 50 overall, consistent with the muted retail participation visible in Google Trends.

**Track record.** Same profile as Google Trends: a reliable retail-euphoria marker that rarely triggers, and does not apply to institution-driven moves.

## Social volume and social sentiment (LunarCrush, Santiment)

**What it measures.** LunarCrush aggregates mentions, engagement and "creator" activity across X, Reddit, YouTube and TikTok into scores per asset ("Galaxy Score", "AltRank", social dominance). Santiment tracks social volume (mentions per keyword), social dominance (share of all crypto chatter), and a "weighted sentiment" that scores posts positive or negative. Both offer limited free views.

**Where to get it free.** [LunarCrush](https://lunarcrush.com/) (free tier shows top-line social metrics), [Santiment app (free charts with delay)](https://app.santiment.net/), [Santiment Insights (free posts)](https://insights.santiment.net/), [The TIE (free research)](https://www.thetie.io/insights/).

**How to read it.** The two most useful patterns, per Santiment's own repeated observations: (1) a spike in social volume for a specific asset that far outpaces its price move tends to mark a short-term top for that asset ("everyone is talking about it" means the buyers have arrived); (2) a rise in "bottom"/"dip" chatter and mocking of Bitcoin during a drawdown tends to come near local lows. Social dominance of Bitcoin versus altcoins also tracks risk appetite: when altcoins dominate conversation, the market is in its speculative phase.

**Track record.** Useful for individual tokens on short horizons, especially memecoins, where social volume is close to the whole story. For Bitcoin, social data mostly duplicates the Fear & Greed index. Bot activity and paid promotion distort raw mention counts, so prefer engagement-weighted measures.

## Funding rates as a sentiment gauge

**What it measures.** Perpetual futures funding (see the derivatives page) doubles as a positioning-based sentiment reading: it measures what leveraged traders are actually paying to hold their view, not what they say.

**Where to get it free.** [Coinglass funding heatmap](https://www.coinglass.com/FundingRate), [Coinglass long/short ratio](https://www.coinglass.com/LongShortRatio).

**How to read it.** Funding at or above 0.05% per 8 hours across major exchanges is "greed you can measure"; sustained negative funding is fear. The retail long/short ratio on Binance and OKX is a weaker cousin: retail is usually net long, so a reading of 70%+ long accounts during a drop is a crowded-long warning. Because money is on the line, funding extremes are more reliable than survey or social extremes, but their horizon is days to a couple of weeks.

**Track record.** The best-tested contrarian sentiment gauge in crypto on short horizons. February 28, 2026 (funding at about minus 6% annualized after the Iran strikes) was followed by a squeeze higher, one of many such examples.

## "Everyone at the party" contrarian signals

**What it measures.** Anecdotal but repeatedly useful signals that mainstream attention has peaked. There is no dataset; the indicator is a checklist.

**How to read it.** Historical tops have featured several of the following at once: Bitcoin on the cover of general-interest magazines (the "magazine cover indicator"), Super Bowl crypto advertisements (February 2022, three months after the top), celebrities and athletes launching tokens, taxi drivers and relatives asking how to buy, exchanges sponsoring stadiums (Crypto.com Arena naming deal announced November 2021, the month of the top), record IPO or SPAC activity in crypto companies (Coinbase's direct listing, April 14, 2021, the day of the spring top), and "supercycle" or "this time is different" essays going mainstream. Bottoms have featured the mirror image: obituaries for Bitcoin, mainstream outlets declaring crypto "over", conferences half-empty, and friends who bought at the top swearing off the asset.

**Where to get it free.** No URL; keep a running note. The [99Bitcoins "Bitcoin obituaries" list](https://99bitcoins.com/bitcoin-obituaries/) is a light-hearted but real tally of mainstream "Bitcoin is dead" articles, which cluster at bottoms.

**Track record.** Uncanny at the three retail-driven tops (2013, 2017, 2021) and at the 2015, 2018 and 2022 bottoms. Notably quiet at the October 2025 top: there was no Super Bowl-style mania, no magazine cover wave, no app-store spike. The ETF-era top looked more like a liquidity peak than a retail party, which is itself the lesson: anecdotal euphoria signals catch retail tops, and the next top may again be driven by institutions and liquidity rather than by your neighbor.

## How to use it

1. Check the Fear & Greed index weekly. Do nothing between 25 and 75. Below 20, start a scale-in plan over weeks; above 80, take partial profits or stop adding.
2. Use Google Trends and app-store ranks as the "retail arrived" flag. If both spike while on-chain valuation is stretched, that is the classic top setup.
3. Use funding as the short-horizon check: is the crowd paying to be long or short right now?
4. For altcoins and memecoins, social volume relative to price is the dominant indicator; a social spike without a matching fundamental story is an exit.
5. Keep your own "party" checklist and revisit it when price is at an extreme. Be aware it may stay silent in an institution-driven cycle.
6. Never use sentiment alone. It tells you the crowd's mood, not whether the crowd is wrong; combine with valuation (MVRV) and flows.

## Key takeaways

- Sentiment is coincident with price; its edge is contrarian and only at extremes (roughly the top and bottom 15% of readings).
- The alternative.me Fear & Greed index is 50% price momentum and volatility by construction, so it re-describes the last month; readings under 20 have been good 6-to-12-month buying zones, readings over 85 good trimming zones.
- Google Trends and app-store ranks reliably flag retail manias, but they did not flag the 2025 top because that cycle's buyer was institutional.
- Funding rates are the most trustworthy short-term sentiment gauge because they measure money at risk.
- Social volume is decisive for memecoins and small tokens, mostly redundant for Bitcoin.
- The "everyone at the party" checklist worked at every retail top and bottom; expect it to stay quiet if the next cycle is again liquidity-driven.
- As of September 11, 2026: Fear & Greed at 56 (Greed), up from 27 a month earlier; Google Trends and app-store ranks subdued; funding neutral. Mood has recovered, retail has not arrived.
