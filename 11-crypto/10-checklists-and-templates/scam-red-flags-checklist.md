# Crypto Scam Red Flags Checklist

**In one sentence:** The tells that identify almost every crypto scam (guaranteed returns, urgency, unsolicited contact, "send to receive," seed phrase requests, fake support, celebrity giveaways, screenshot profits, unknown apps), the verification steps that take five minutes and defeat most of them, and exactly what to do — and not do — if you have already been hit.

**The core idea:** Crypto scams are not sophisticated. They are the same handful of scripts run at enormous scale, and they work because they exploit emotion (greed, fear, loneliness, urgency) rather than technology. The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center reports crypto investment fraud as the single largest category of reported losses, and Chainalysis identifies "pig butchering" — long-con investment fraud that starts as a friendship or romance — and high-yield investment scams as the most lucrative. If you recognize the script, you are immune to it.

**The one rule that beats most of them:** *No legitimate person, company, wallet, or exchange will ever ask for your seed phrase, ask you to send crypto in order to receive crypto, or guarantee a return.* A request for any of those is not a warning sign of a scam; it **is** the scam.

---

## Part 1 — The tells

Tick every box that applies. **One tick is enough to walk away.** Two or more is certainty.

### Promises and pitch
- [ ] **Guaranteed returns**, "risk-free," "can't lose," or a specific high yield ("2% daily," "30% monthly"). Only scammers guarantee profits.
- [ ] **Urgency:** "limited spots," "closes tonight," "price doubles at midnight," "the wallet must be verified within 24 hours."
- [ ] Vague explanation of *how* the returns are generated ("AI trading bot," "arbitrage," "liquidity mining") that falls apart when you ask for specifics.
- [ ] **Screenshot profits:** photos of account balances, Lamborghinis, or "testimonials." All trivially faked.
- [ ] You are told to keep it secret, or not to tell your bank, family, or "people who don't understand crypto."

### How they reached you
- [ ] **Unsolicited contact:** a DM on X/Instagram/Telegram/Discord/LinkedIn/WhatsApp, a "wrong number" text, a dating-app match, a comment reply, or a stranger in a group chat.
- [ ] A new online friend or romantic interest who, after days or weeks of rapport, mentions a crypto investment they or their "uncle" or "mentor" is doing well with. This is the pig-butchering script, verbatim.
- [ ] A request to **move the conversation** to WhatsApp, Telegram, or Signal.
- [ ] An **ad** or sponsored search result for a wallet, exchange, or "claim" page.
- [ ] Someone claiming to be **support staff** (exchange, wallet maker, MetaMask, Ledger, "blockchain support") who contacted you first. Real support never initiates contact and never DMs.
- [ ] A **job offer** that requires buying crypto, paying a fee, or "completing tasks" on a platform with a balance you cannot withdraw.
- [ ] An email or text claiming to be from Amazon, Microsoft, your bank, the IRS, the police, or a government agency that wants payment in crypto. **No government or legitimate business accepts crypto as payment for fines, taxes, or fees.**

### What they ask you to do
- [ ] **Send crypto to receive more crypto** ("giveaway," "doubling," "airdrop deposit," "gas fee to unlock"). This is never real. Not from Elon Musk, not from a founder, not from a "verified" account.
- [ ] Provide your **seed phrase / recovery phrase / private key** — to "verify," "sync," "migrate," "claim," "recover," or "secure" a wallet. Never, for any reason, to anyone.
- [ ] Enter a seed phrase on a **website** or in a **form**, or "import" your wallet into a new app.
- [ ] Install **remote-access software** (AnyDesk, TeamViewer) or a **screen-sharing** session "so support can help."
- [ ] Download a wallet, exchange app, or "trading platform" from a link rather than the official store/site — or use a platform you have never heard of that shows big gains but adds "taxes," "fees," or "verification deposits" when you try to withdraw.
- [ ] Deposit at a **crypto ATM** while someone stays on the phone with you.
- [ ] **Connect your wallet** to a site to claim a surprise airdrop, NFT, or "compensation."
- [ ] Sign a message or transaction you do not understand ("just click confirm to verify ownership").
- [ ] Pay an **upfront fee** to unlock, release, or recover funds.
- [ ] Pay someone who says they can **recover** crypto you already lost.

### Platform and token tells
- [ ] The **URL is slightly wrong**: an extra letter, a different TLD (.co instead of .com), a hyphen, a subdomain trick (`coinbase.secure-login.example`).
- [ ] The "exchange" or "platform" is not on any regulator's register, has no legal entity name, and has reviews only on its own site.
- [ ] A token with the **same name/ticker as a real project** but a different contract address.
- [ ] A token where **you cannot sell** (honeypot), where the deployer holds most of the supply, where liquidity is unlocked, or where the mint/freeze authority is still active.
- [ ] Unrealistic yield on a brand-new protocol with no audits and anonymous team (see [defi-protocol-safety-checklist.md](defi-protocol-safety-checklist.md)).
- [ ] **Address poisoning:** a transaction in your history from an address whose first and last characters match one you have used. Never copy addresses from history.
- [ ] Blackmail: an email claiming to have compromising footage and demanding crypto. Do not pay; report.

## Part 2 — Verification steps (five minutes, defeats most scams)

- [ ] **Verify the contract address.** Look the token up on CoinGecko or CoinMarketCap, copy the contract address from *there* (or from the project's official site, reached by typing the URL), and compare character by character to what you are about to buy. Different address = different token = scam.
- [ ] **Run a token scanner** before buying anything small-cap: rugcheck.xyz (Solana) or similar tools for EVM chains. Look for: mint authority revoked, freeze authority revoked, liquidity locked or burned, top-10 holder concentration, whether the token is sellable, mutable metadata.
- [ ] **Check the domain.** Type the exchange or wallet URL yourself, or use your bookmark. Hover over links. Check the certificate/organization name in the browser. Search "[site name] scam."
- [ ] **Verify support channels** from the company's official site (typed, not clicked). Then compare: did the person who contacted you come through one of those channels? Almost never.
- [ ] **Look up the license.** For a US "exchange" or "platform": search FinCEN's MSB registry (msb.fincen.gov), the NMLS Consumer Access site for state licenses, and the SEC/CFTC/state-regulator warning lists. Unregistered = do not send money. Also check the regulator's list of *fake* registrations and cloned firms — scammers copy real firms' names and license numbers.
- [ ] **Reverse-image search** profile photos of new online contacts who bring up crypto.
- [ ] **Check the "verified" account.** Celebrity giveaway accounts are impersonations; look at the handle, follower history, and whether the real account posted it. The real one never did.
- [ ] **Review approvals.** Go to revoke.cash (or your chain's explorer approval tool) and see what contracts you have given permission to move your tokens. Revoke anything you do not recognize or no longer use.
- [ ] **Simulate before signing.** Use a wallet or extension that previews what a transaction will do. If the preview shows assets leaving that you did not intend, reject.
- [ ] **Ask one person** who is not involved. Say it out loud. Scams survive on secrecy and speed; describing the pitch to a skeptic breaks both.
- [ ] **Test-withdraw.** Before trusting any platform with more, deposit a small amount, make a trade, and withdraw *everything* back to your own wallet. If withdrawal requires a fee, tax, or verification deposit, it is a scam.

## Part 3 — If you have been scammed

**Speed matters, but so does not making it worse.** Do these in order.

### Stop the bleeding (first hour)
- [ ] **Stop sending money.** Immediately, including any "fee," "tax," or "unlock" payment. Every additional payment is lost.
- [ ] **If a seed phrase or private key was exposed:** move all remaining funds from that wallet to a *new* wallet with a *new* seed, right now, from a clean device. The old wallet is permanently compromised. Do not "just change the password."
- [ ] **If an exchange account was compromised:** change the password from a clean device, revoke all sessions and API keys, change 2FA, contact the exchange's official support (typed URL) and ask them to freeze withdrawals.
- [ ] **If you signed a malicious approval:** revoke it immediately (revoke.cash / explorer tool), then move remaining assets to a fresh wallet.
- [ ] **If remote-access software was installed:** disconnect the device from the internet, then wipe and reinstall it before using it for anything financial again.
- [ ] **Do not tip off the scammer** that you know or that you have reported. Do not engage further.

### Preserve evidence (first day)
- [ ] Record every **wallet address** you sent to, every **transaction ID (hash)**, the **dates, amounts, and asset types**, and the exchange or wallet you sent from.
- [ ] Screenshot all **communications** (profiles, usernames, phone numbers, emails, chat logs), the **platform URL**, and any "account balance" pages.
- [ ] Write a dated timeline while it is fresh.

### Report (first week)
- [ ] **FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center:** ic3.gov. Include the transaction details above; the FBI's Operation Level Up has used IC3 data to identify and notify victims, and law enforcement traces on-chain funds from these reports.
- [ ] **FTC:** ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
- [ ] **CFTC** (cftc.gov/complaint) and **SEC** (sec.gov/tcr) for investment platforms.
- [ ] **Your exchange** (official support channel) — they can sometimes flag or freeze funds that landed at another regulated exchange, and their compliance teams work with law enforcement.
- [ ] **State regulator / attorney general**, and — if a bank transfer or card was involved — your bank.
- [ ] If an impersonated brand was involved (Ledger, Coinbase, MetaMask, a celebrity), report the impersonation to that brand and to the platform where it happened.

### Chain analysis and recovery — what is realistic
- [ ] Blockchain transactions are public and permanent. Investigators (and some exchanges) can trace where funds went; if they landed at a regulated exchange, a freeze is sometimes possible. This is why transaction IDs matter in your report.
- [ ] Realistic expectation: **most stolen crypto is not recovered.** Reporting still matters — it feeds investigations, supports future restitution processes, and may be needed for tax treatment of the loss.
- [ ] **Recovery scams are the second wave.** Anyone who contacts you offering to recover the funds — "blockchain forensics," "a law firm," "a hacker," "an FBI agent," a "recovery expert" in the comments — for an upfront fee is running the same scam again on the same victim list. The FBI explicitly warns about this. Legitimate recovery never requires paying first.
- [ ] Talk to a **tax professional** about whether the loss is deductible; the rules for theft losses versus investment losses are specific and have changed in recent years.
- [ ] Consider talking to someone. Victims of long-con fraud routinely report shame and isolation; the fraud was professionally engineered to produce exactly that. It is a crime that happened to you, not a character flaw.

## Quick reference card

| If you see… | It is… | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| "Send 1 ETH, get 2 back" | Giveaway scam | Ignore, report the account |
| "Enter your recovery phrase to verify" | Seed phishing | Close it, never type the phrase |
| Support DMs you first | Impersonation | Block; contact real support via typed URL |
| New online friend mentions crypto investing | Pig butchering | End contact, reverse-image search, report |
| Platform shows gains but wants a "tax" to withdraw | Fake investment platform | Stop paying; report; the balance was never real |
| Wrong-number text that turns friendly | Pig butchering opener | Delete, do not reply |
| Someone will "recover" your lost crypto for a fee | Recovery scam | Do not pay; report |
| Government/utility/Amazon wants crypto payment | Impersonation | No legitimate entity accepts crypto for fines or bills |
| Airdrop needs you to "connect wallet" and sign | Wallet drainer | Do not connect; use a burner if you must; check approvals after |
