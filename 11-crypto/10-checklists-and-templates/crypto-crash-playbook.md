# Crypto Crash Playbook

**In one sentence:** A numbered, break-glass procedure for the day crypto falls 30–50% in a week — confirm your coins are where you think they are, then do nothing for 48 hours, then re-read your policy and execute only the two or three pre-written actions (rebalance, harvest losses, sweep to cold storage) while refusing every "rescue," every leverage temptation, and every recovery scam that crashes reliably produce.

**Read this while nothing is wrong.** It is written short and numbered because you will not read carefully when it is needed. It is the crypto supplement to the main library's [Market Crash Playbook](../../10-checklists-and-templates/market-crash-playbook.md); read that first for the general principles (do nothing first, then follow the plan). This file covers what is different about crypto: bigger drawdowns, faster cascades, counterparty failures, and a scam wave that follows every crash.

**Context to keep in mind:** Bitcoin has fallen 50% or more from a high at least six times since 2011, including drawdowns of roughly 80% in 2014–15, 2018, and 2022, and most altcoins fell 90%+ in each. Every prior drawdown was followed by a new high for bitcoin; most altcoins never recovered. Crashes are the normal operating condition of this asset class, not a surprise, and your allocation was sized (in your IPS) for exactly this.

---

## Phase 0 — Before the crash (do this today)

- [ ] Your [crypto IPS](crypto-investment-policy-statement-template.md) is written, with a maximum allocation, rebalance bands, and a crash protocol section.
- [ ] Your long-term coins are in self-custody or a regulated ETF — not on an exchange, not lent out, not in a yield product. If they are, fix that now; in a crash, withdrawals get paused.
- [ ] Your seed backup was tested in the last 12 months and you know where it is.
- [ ] You have a "crypto rebalancing cash" amount already set aside for buying at the lower band — and it is not your emergency fund.
- [ ] You have written down, in one sentence, what you did during the last crypto crash and how it felt.
- [ ] You know your tax lots and which ones are underwater (your tax tool shows this).
- [ ] You have identified which positions you would harvest losses on and what you would buy back (crypto is not currently subject to the wash-sale rule in the US, but confirm the current law at the time; see [tax-record-keeping-checklist.md](tax-record-keeping-checklist.md)).

---

## Phase 1 — Hour 0: verify custody, then stop

1. [ ] **Confirm your coins exist where you think they do.** Open your hardware wallet or watch-only app and confirm balances on-chain. Confirm exchange balances *and* that withdrawals are functioning (try a tiny test withdrawal if you hold anything meaningful there). This is the only urgent action.
2. [ ] **If an exchange or lender you use has paused withdrawals**, that is a solvency signal, not a technical glitch. Stop depositing, document your balance (screenshots, statements), and read Phase 5.
3. [ ] **If withdrawals work and your balance there exceeds your IPS cap, sweep to cold storage now.** Counterparty failures cluster in crashes.
4. [ ] **Then close the apps.** Delete price widgets from your home screen. Mute the crypto group chats.
5. [ ] **Do nothing else for 48 hours.** No buys, no sells, no "just a little" of either. Put a calendar reminder 48 hours out labeled "open the playbook, Phase 2."

**Why 48 hours:** Cascading liquidations (leveraged traders being force-sold, which pushes prices lower, which triggers more force-selling) typically play out over one to three days. Acting inside that window means trading against a machine. Acting after it means trading against a plan.

---

## Phase 2 — Hour 48: re-read the plan

6. [ ] Re-read your crypto IPS from the top, out loud if that helps. Note the crash protocol section and the rebalance bands.
7. [ ] Compute your current crypto allocation as a percentage of investable assets. Write it down next to the target and the lower band.
8. [ ] Re-read your written thesis for each holding. Ask *only*: "Is the reason I bought this still true?" Not "will the price recover." If the thesis is intact, price is noise. If the thesis is broken (protocol exploited, team gone, token depegged for good), that is a thesis-break sale per your IPS, regardless of price.
9. [ ] Look at the sentence you wrote about last time. You are likely feeling the same thing. It passed then.

---

## Phase 3 — Execute only the pre-written actions

10. [ ] **Rebalance rule.** If crypto is below your lower band, buy back up to target — *from the designated rebalancing cash only*, in two or three tranches over a couple of weeks rather than all at once. If it is inside the bands, do nothing. Do not "buy the dip" beyond the target; that is a new allocation decision, and the IPS says those happen at scheduled reviews.
11. [ ] **Tax-loss harvest.** For lots that are underwater in taxable accounts: sell the lot, book the loss, and buy back the same or an equivalent asset per your pre-written plan. Harvested losses offset gains and up to $3,000 of ordinary income per year in the US, and carry forward. Record every trade (date, units, proceeds, basis) as you go — your tax tool will thank you.
12. [ ] **Sweep exchange balances** to cold storage if you have not already.
13. [ ] **Revoke DeFi approvals** and, if you hold DeFi positions, re-run [defi-protocol-safety-checklist.md](defi-protocol-safety-checklist.md) on each: crashes trigger bad debt, oracle failures, and depegs. Withdraw from anything that fails.
14. [ ] **Check stablecoins.** Confirm each stablecoin you hold is trading near $1.00 and its issuer's reserves are unaffected. A depeg is a thesis break, not a buying opportunity.
15. [ ] Log what you did and why in one paragraph. Future you will want it.

---

## Phase 4 — What not to do (the crash-specific temptations)

- **No leverage "to catch the bottom."** Nobody knows where the bottom is, and leveraged positions are what is being liquidated *right now*. Your IPS forbids it; a crash is not the exception, it is the reason.
- **No yield "rescues."** Platforms offering suddenly higher yields to attract deposits during a crash are often the ones about to fail — the yield is the cost of your exit liquidity. Every centralized lender that collapsed in 2022 was raising rates in the weeks before.
- **No "just move it to X exchange, it's safer."** Moving coins between custodians in a panic multiplies your chance of a mistake and puts funds in a hot wallet during the most dangerous week. Cold storage is where things go; not another counterparty.
- **No selling the core because a headline said "crypto is dead."** That headline has run in every cycle. If your thesis is intact and your allocation was sized honestly, the plan is to hold and rebalance. If you discover you cannot hold, that is real information about your risk tolerance — record it, and at the next scheduled review, lower the maximum allocation permanently. Do not lower it today.
- **No averaging down into altcoins that broke thesis.** "It fell 90%, it can't fall further" — it can, to zero, and most do.
- **No "reading the market" for the next two weeks.** Twitter, YouTube, and group chats are at maximum noise and minimum signal. The plan is written; follow it.

## Phase 5 — Scam alertness: crashes bring the second wave

Every crash produces a predictable set of frauds targeting people who just lost money. Assume you will be targeted.

- [ ] **"Recovery" services.** Anyone who contacts you — by DM, email, ad, or "law firm" — offering to recover funds lost in a crash, a hack, or a failed exchange, for a fee, is a scammer. The FBI specifically warns that criminals impersonate law enforcement and attorneys in this way. Legitimate recovery happens through bankruptcy courts and law enforcement, never through a Telegram contact, and never for an upfront fee.
- [ ] **Fake exchange "support."** Messages saying your account is at risk and you must "verify" your seed phrase, move funds to a "secure wallet," or install remote-access software. No exchange asks for a seed, password, or 2FA code. Ever.
- [ ] **Fake bankruptcy claim portals.** If a platform you used fails, the claims process is run by a court-appointed administrator with an official site. Confirm the URL through the court docket or reputable news, not through a link in an email.
- [ ] **"Bailout" or "compensation" tokens and airdrops.** Do not connect a wallet to any site promising to make you whole.
- [ ] **Urgent "buy the dip" pitches** from strangers, influencers, or new "friends" — this is the pig-butchering script switching from greed to fear.
- [ ] **What to do if someone contacts you:** do not reply, do not click, screenshot it, block, and report (see [scam-red-flags-checklist.md](scam-red-flags-checklist.md) for IC3, FTC, and exchange reporting steps).

**If you held funds on a platform that failed:** document everything (balances, statements, transaction IDs, communications); file a claim only through the official process; report to IC3 and the FTC; talk to a tax professional about whether and when the loss is deductible (rules for theft losses and worthless assets are specific and have changed); and treat every unsolicited contact about it as a scam.

---

## Phase 6 — When to check again

- **Day 2:** Phase 2 and 3, as above.
- **Day 14:** Second rebalancing tranche if applicable. Re-check stablecoins and DeFi positions. Otherwise nothing.
- **Day 30:** Confirm tax records are complete for every crash-week transaction. Confirm allocation is within bands. Resume normal DCA if you paused it (you should not have paused it).
- **Next scheduled quarterly review:** the *only* time you may revise allocation limits, and only downward if you learned you cannot tolerate the volatility.
- **Not:** hourly, daily, or whenever a notification arrives. Turn the notifications off.

**Signal that it is over:** you will not get one. Prices bottom while sentiment is worst and headlines are most certain the asset class is finished. The plan does not need you to identify the bottom; it needs you to keep rebalancing into it and out of it, mechanically, for years.

---

## One-page summary (print this)

1. Verify custody; sweep exchange balances to cold storage.
2. Close the apps. 48 hours of nothing.
3. Re-read the IPS. Thesis intact? Then price is noise.
4. Rebalance to target from designated cash only. Harvest losses. Log everything.
5. No leverage. No yield rescues. No new counterparties. No selling the core on headlines.
6. Everyone who contacts you about recovering money is a scammer.
7. Check again on day 14 and day 30. Otherwise, live your life.
