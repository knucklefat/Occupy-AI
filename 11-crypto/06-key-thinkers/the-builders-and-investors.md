# The Builders and Investors

**In one sentence:** The people who build crypto's biggest platforms and fund them — Hayden Adams, Anatoly Yakovenko, Brian Armstrong, Cathie Wood, a16z crypto's Chris Dixon, and Paradigm — have theses worth understanding and incentives worth remembering, because every one of them profits from the industry's growth regardless of whether any particular token you buy does.

## Hayden Adams — Uniswap

**Who:** a mechanical engineer laid off from Siemens in 2017 who, encouraged by a friend at the Ethereum Foundation, built an "automated market maker" (AMM) from a Vitalik Buterin blog idea. Uniswap launched in November 2018.

**Thesis:** exchanges don't need order books or market makers; a pool of two tokens governed by a simple formula (x × y = k) lets anyone trade against it and anyone provide liquidity. Uniswap v3 (May 2021) added concentrated liquidity; v4 (January 2025) added programmable "hooks"; Unichain (2025) is its own layer-2.

**Record:** Uniswap became the largest decentralized exchange and has processed trillions in cumulative volume — the clearest proof that DeFi works. The UNI token (September 2020) governed the protocol without receiving fees for five years; a "fee switch" and token burn were approved only in late 2025. The SEC issued a Wells notice in April 2024 and dropped the matter in February 2025.

**Incentives:** Uniswap Labs, a company, charges an interface fee on swaps through its front end (since 2023) while the open protocol is free; Adams and venture backers hold UNI. The gap between "the protocol" and "the company" is the thing to watch.

## Anatoly Yakovenko — Solana

**Who:** a former Qualcomm engineer who published the Solana whitepaper in late 2017 and launched mainnet in March 2020.

**Thesis:** one fast, cheap global state machine beats a network of rollups. Solana's "proof of history" (a cryptographic clock that orders transactions before consensus) plus aggressive hardware requirements lets it target Nasdaq-like throughput at fractions of a cent per transaction — deliberately trading node decentralization for scale, the opposite corner of Vitalik's trilemma.

**Record:** SOL went from about $260 (November 2021) to $8 (December 2022) after FTX — Sam Bankman-Fried was a major holder and promoter — and after repeated network outages in 2021–2022 and a five-hour halt in February 2024. It then recovered to about $290 in January 2025, powered by memecoin trading (pump.fun) and stablecoin payments. Yakovenko was right that users would choose cheap and fast over maximal decentralization; critics were right that a large share of the early supply went to insiders and that "decentralized" is a stretch for a chain with expensive validators and, until the Firedancer client, a single implementation.

**Incentives:** Solana Labs, the foundation, and early venture funds hold large token positions.

## Brian Armstrong — Coinbase

**Who:** a former Airbnb engineer who founded Coinbase in 2012 with Fred Ehrsam through Y Combinator. Coinbase listed directly in April 2021, fell about 90% into 2022, was sued by the SEC in June 2023 (case dismissed February 2025), and joined the S&P 500 in May 2025.

**Thesis:** "economic freedom" — crypto updates the financial system the way the internet updated information. Coinbase's strategy is to be the regulated on-ramp: custody for most U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs, the Base layer-2 (2023), and a large revenue share from USDC stablecoin interest.

**Record:** Right about institutional adoption and about the value of being the compliant venue. Wrong or contradictory: a 2020 memo declared Coinbase "mission-focused" and apolitical; by 2024 it was among the largest corporate donors to the crypto super PAC Fairshake. A May 2025 data breach via bribed support contractors cost hundreds of millions. Coinbase's revenue is still dominated by trading fees, which spike in altcoin manias — its incentives align with volume, not with your returns.

## Cathie Wood — ARK Invest

**Who:** founder of ARK (2014), the thematic active-ETF manager. ARK was the first public fund manager to gain Bitcoin exposure (2015, via Chris Burniske's research) and launched a spot Bitcoin ETF with 21Shares in January 2024.

**Thesis:** Bitcoin is a new monetary asset that will take share from gold, emerging-market currencies, corporate treasuries, and institutional portfolios. ARK's "Big Ideas" reports publish 2030 scenarios: in early 2025, roughly $300,000 bear, $710,000 base, and $1.5 million bull; in April 2025 an "aggressive" bull case of $2.4 million; in November 2025 she cut the headline target to $1.2 million, reasoning that stablecoins were absorbing the payments role she had assigned to Bitcoin.

**Record:** ARK's flagship ARKK peaked in February 2021 and fell about 80% by late 2022; Coinbase was a top holding through that drop. The Bitcoin call itself has been directionally right for a decade. The scenario numbers are marketing with a spreadsheet attached — useful for seeing the assumptions, useless as forecasts.

**Incentives:** management fees on thematic funds whose flows follow narratives.

## Chris Dixon and a16z crypto

**Who:** Andreessen Horowitz's crypto arm, led by Chris Dixon, raised dedicated funds of $300 million (2018), $515 million (2020), $2.2 billion (2021), and $4.5 billion (May 2022 — the top of the market). Portfolio includes Coinbase, Uniswap, Solana, OpenSea, Yuga Labs, Dapper Labs, Helium, and EigenLayer.

**Thesis:** *Read Write Own* (January 2024) argues the internet went from read-only (web1) to read-write on corporate platforms (web2), and that blockchains enable a third era where users own the networks they use. Corporate networks follow an "attract-extract" cycle — generous until locked-in users can be squeezed — while protocol networks with enforced low take-rates can't. Tokens are how users get ownership.

**Record:** a16z coined and pushed "web3," then quietly retired the term in 2025. Its 2021–2022 vintage bought at peak prices; its bets on NFTs and web3 gaming largely failed; its bets on Coinbase, Uniswap, and Solana paid off. Molly White's review noted the book named no non-speculative product at scale and disclosed no holdings. a16z is also a leading funder of the industry's political operation.

**Incentives:** management and carried interest on roughly $7.6 billion of crypto funds — the strongest structural reason to be bullish that exists.

## Paradigm

**Who:** founded in 2018 by Coinbase co-founder Fred Ehrsam and Matt Huang; raised $2.5 billion in November 2021 and $850 million in 2024.

**Thesis:** research-driven venture. Paradigm's team writes foundational technical work — "Ethereum Is a Dark Forest" (2020, on front-running bots), Uniswap v3's design, open-source tooling like Foundry and Reth — and invests in the protocols it helps design (Uniswap, Optimism, dYdX, Blur).

**Record:** Its research is the best of any investor's. Its investment record includes a $278 million FTX position written to zero in 2022 and Blur, an NFT marketplace whose token incentives helped crash NFT royalties. In 2023 it briefly rebranded toward "research-driven technology" including AI, then reverted.

**Incentives:** fund economics plus the ability to shape protocols it holds tokens in.

## Rules you can steal

1. **Separate the protocol from the company.** Uniswap the protocol is free; Uniswap Labs charges fees. Which one does your token own?
2. **Ask which trilemma corner was sacrificed.** Solana chose scale; the price is validator cost and outage history.
3. **Exchanges profit from volume, not from your returns.** Their research and listings should be read as inventory management.
4. **Scenario targets are assumptions, not forecasts.** Take ARK's inputs (share of gold, treasuries, portfolios) and argue with them; ignore the output.
5. **Fund vintage tells you the bias.** A manager who raised $4.5 billion at the 2022 top needs the next cycle.
6. **Weight technical research above thesis books.** Paradigm's papers survive; venture manifestos are fundraising documents.
7. **Follow the political money.** Builders' lobbying spend reveals which rules they fear.
8. **Insider allocation is the first thing to check** on any new chain or token — before the technology.

## Key takeaways

- Adams proved automated market makers work; the open question was always who captures the fees, and it took five years to answer.
- Yakovenko proved users prefer cheap and fast; Solana's outages and insider allocation are the price.
- Armstrong built the regulated on-ramp and then the political machine to protect it; Coinbase's revenue still rises and falls with speculation.
- Wood's Bitcoin call has been right; her price scenarios are narrative devices.
- a16z's *Read Write Own* is the best-argued venture case for crypto and its weakest point is the lack of a non-speculative example at scale.
- Paradigm's research is first-rate; its FTX write-off shows research does not immunize against counterparty risk.
- Every one of these people is paid in tokens, fees, or carry; their optimism is structural.
