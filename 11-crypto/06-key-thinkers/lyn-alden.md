# Lyn Alden

**In one sentence:** Lyn Alden is the macro analyst who explained Bitcoin as a *ledger technology* rather than a get-rich scheme — placing it inside a five-thousand-year history of money and a fiscal-dominance thesis about government debt — while keeping a calmer, more evidence-based view of everything else in crypto than most of her Bitcoin-focused peers.

## Who she is

Alden trained as an electrical engineer (with a graduate degree in engineering management) and worked in aviation before founding Lyn Alden Investment Strategy in 2016, a research service covering global macro, equities, and eventually Bitcoin. She became a general partner at Ego Death Capital, a Bitcoin-focused venture fund, and published *Broken Money: Why Our Financial System Is Failing Us and How We Can Make It Better* in September 2023. Her incentives: research subscriptions and a Bitcoin-ecosystem venture fund. Neither depends on any particular price target, but both benefit from Bitcoin remaining relevant.

## Core framework

**1. Money is a ledger, and ledger technology sets the rules.** *Broken Money* argues that money has always been whatever the best available ledger could support: commodity money when ledgers were physical, then bank ledgers, then central-bank ledgers. Her key historical claim is that the telegraph (1860s) let *transactions* move at the speed of light while *settlement* of gold still moved at the speed of ships. That mismatch forced the world toward abstracted, centralized ledgers — central banking, then pure fiat after 1971. Bitcoin is interesting because for the first time the settlement layer moves as fast as the transaction layer, closing a 150-year gap.

**2. Fiscal dominance.** In heavily indebted economies, the government's financing needs eventually override the central bank's inflation mandate. Her 2024–2025 phrase for U.S. deficits — "nothing stops this train" — captures her view that structural deficits, entitlement growth, and interest costs make sustained monetary tightening politically impossible. Assets that are scarce and cannot be debased are the hedge. This is the macro backbone of her Bitcoin case.

**3. Bitcoin as a liquidity barometer.** In "Bitcoin: A Global Liquidity Barometer" (September 2024, with Sam Callahan), she found Bitcoin moved in the same direction as global M2 in 83% of rolling 12-month periods since 2013 — a tighter relationship than any other major asset class — with the honest caveat that the correlation weakens over shorter windows and breaks during crypto-specific shocks (exchange failures, extreme valuations).

**4. Bitcoin is a network, valued like one.** Her 2020 investment thesis rested on network effects, scarcity, the halving-driven supply schedule, and the observation that each cycle brought a higher floor.

## Her balanced view on altcoins

Alden is Bitcoin-focused but not a maximalist in the tribal sense. Her February 2021 "An Economic Analysis of Ethereum" concluded she wouldn't own ETH — its monetary policy changes over time, its roadmap was complex, and its base layer's history includes the DAO reversal — but she explicitly said it was not a scam and that its smart-contract platform had real utility. Her general framing: most altcoins are better understood as venture-style bets on a protocol business, not as money, and should be sized and analyzed as ventures. She has also treated stablecoins as a genuine product with real demand rather than a threat, and warned that leverage — not fundamentals — is what ends each cycle.

## Scorecard

**What she got right**
- **Turning bullish on Bitcoin in 2020** at roughly $7,000–$11,000, based on the post-COVID fiscal response and the halving; she laid out the case publicly in "3 Reasons I'm Investing in Bitcoin" (2020). Bitcoin has since made two new all-time highs.
- **Fiscal dominance (2021 onward):** U.S. deficits stayed near $2 trillion a year through an expansion, interest costs became a top budget item, and rate cuts resumed in 2024 with inflation still above target.
- **Cycle dampening:** she argued Bitcoin's drawdowns would become less extreme as the asset matured; the 2025–2026 drawdown (about 53% from the October 2025 peak to the summer 2026 low) was the shallowest cycle decline in Bitcoin's history.
- **Leverage as the cycle-killer:** she repeatedly flagged that euphoria and leverage, not Bitcoin itself, cause the busts — borne out by Terra, Three Arrows, Celsius, and FTX in 2022 and by the October 2025 liquidation cascade.

**What she got wrong or overstated**
- **Recession and stagflation calls in 2022–2023.** She leaned toward a recession that never arrived and was underweight U.S. equities during a strong 2023–2024 run.
- **Price optimism in 2025.** Her "conservative" $150,000 target for the cycle wasn't reached; Bitcoin peaked near $126,000 in October 2025.
- **Bitcoin treasury companies.** She downplayed concentration risk from firms like Strategy in 2025, pointing out Mt. Gox once held more coins; the 2026 forced-selling episode showed the risk was less about concentration than about leveraged holders becoming forced sellers.
- **Global-liquidity framework is descriptive, not predictive.** An 83% directional hit rate over 12 months says little about the next three months, as she acknowledges — but her readers often treat it as a timing tool.

## Rules you can steal

1. **Analyze money as a ledger.** Ask who keeps the record, who can change it, and how fast it settles. Most crypto pitches fail on the second question.
2. **Follow the fiscal, not just the Fed.** When deficits are structural, expect debasement over austerity and position accordingly.
3. **Use liquidity as a regime indicator, not a trigger.** Rising global M2 is a tailwind over 12-month horizons, not a buy signal for Tuesday.
4. **Treat altcoins as ventures.** Size them like early-stage equity, expect most to fail, and never confuse them with money.
5. **Leverage ends cycles.** Watch funding rates, open interest, and corporate borrowers — not the halving calendar — for tops.
6. **Write your thesis down with dates.** Alden's public, dated theses are why her hits and misses can be scored at all.
7. **Read primary history.** *Broken Money* is valuable because it grounds crypto in monetary history rather than in crypto Twitter.

## Key takeaways

- Alden's contribution is intellectual framing: Bitcoin as the first ledger where settlement matches transaction speed, inside a story of fiscal dominance.
- Her 2020 pivot to Bitcoin and her fiscal-dominance thesis are among the best-timed macro calls in the space.
- Her misses cluster in traditional macro (recession timing, equities) and in specific price targets.
- She is one of the few Bitcoin-focused analysts who assesses Ethereum and stablecoins on their merits rather than dismissing them.
- The global-liquidity relationship (83% directional agreement over 12-month windows) is real but coarse.
- Her incentives — research subscriptions and a Bitcoin VC fund — are worth remembering, but she discloses them.
