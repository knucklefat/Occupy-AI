# Vitalik Buterin

**In one sentence:** Vitalik Buterin turned the blockchain from a ledger for one currency into a general-purpose computer, and has spent the decade since as Ethereum's most persistent internal critic — a rare founder whose essays argue against the speculation that funds his own ecosystem.

## Who he is

Born in Russia in 1994 and raised in Canada, Buterin co-founded *Bitcoin Magazine* at 17, published the Ethereum whitepaper in late 2013 at 19, and launched the network on July 30, 2015. He has no formal executive role — the Ethereum Foundation is a nonprofit, and he is a researcher, not a CEO — but his blog posts function as the de facto strategic roadmap. He holds a large amount of ETH (he has periodically sold and donated portions), so he is not disinterested, but he is unusually transparent about it.

## Core framework

**Blockchains as programmable, credibly neutral infrastructure.** Bitcoin lets you own and send one asset; Ethereum lets anyone deploy code (smart contracts) that runs exactly as written. That single generalization produced stablecoins, decentralized exchanges, lending protocols, NFTs, DAOs, and — inevitably — most of the industry's scams.

Three ideas recur through his writing:

1. **The scalability trilemma** (first laid out around 2017 in the Ethereum sharding FAQ). A blockchain wants three things — decentralization (cheap to run a node), security (expensive to attack), and scalability (high throughput) — and simple designs get only two. Chains that advertise huge throughput usually pay for it in node centralization. This is the single most useful lens for comparing layer-1 blockchains.
2. **Endgame** (December 2021). His prediction that all scaling roads converge on the same shape: block *production* becomes centralized and professional, while block *validation* stays cheap and decentralized so users can verify without trusting. This is why Ethereum bet on rollups (layer-2 chains that batch transactions and post proofs back to Ethereum) rather than simply raising base-layer limits.
3. **Credible neutrality and legitimacy** ("The Most Important Scarce Resource Is Legitimacy," March 2021). A protocol's long-run value comes from being seen as fair by people who disagree with each other. Governance captured by whales, or a chain that reverses transactions for insiders, burns that resource.

## The key essays

- **"Endgame"** (Dec 2021): the rollup-centric roadmap's philosophical justification.
- **"The Three Transitions"** (June 2023): Ethereum must move to layer-2 scaling, smart-contract wallets, and privacy simultaneously or fail ordinary users.
- **"My Techno-Optimism"** (Nov 2023): argues for "d/acc" — defensive, decentralized acceleration — as an alternative to both anti-tech doom and unconditional accelerationism.
- **"Make Ethereum Cypherpunk Again"** (Dec 28, 2023): a self-critique. High fees in the 2021 bull market meant the only people who could afford to use Ethereum were speculators, which reshaped the culture around "degen" gambling. He lists the values that should define the ecosystem — open participation, decentralization, censorship resistance, auditability, credible neutrality, building tools not empires, and cooperation — and argues the technology (rollups, zero-knowledge proofs, account abstraction) now exists to return to them.
- **"Possible futures of the Ethereum protocol"** (Oct–Nov 2024): six posts (Merge, Surge, Scourge, Verge, Purge, Splurge) that are the closest thing to a formal roadmap.
- **"Lean Ethereum"** (Feb 2026, updated July 2026): a strawman plan for a simpler, faster, quantum-resistant base layer; developers agreed with the direction but criticized the three-to-four-year timeline as too slow.

## His critiques of crypto culture

Buterin is unusual in that many of his sharpest attacks target his own side. He has criticized coin-voting governance as whale-capturable, algorithmic stablecoins before and after Terra's May 2022 collapse, memecoins as value-extracting (in March 2024 he asked whether they could at least fund charity), and "SocialFi" for letting financial incentives overwhelm social ones. In a January 2026 interview he said the industry had forgotten its founding intentions and cited Trump-affiliated token launches as an example of misaligned priorities. He has also written that he hopes the ecosystem matures enough that ETH price becomes irrelevant to most of what is built on it.

## Scorecard

**What he got right**
- Programmable blockchains would matter (2013): Ethereum hosts the majority of stablecoin supply and DeFi value more than a decade later.
- The trilemma (2017): every "Ethereum killer" that promised to escape it has faced outages, node concentration, or both.
- Proof of stake could be done safely: the Merge (Sept 15, 2022) cut Ethereum's energy use by roughly 99.9% with no downtime.
- Rollups would carry most activity: after the Dencun upgrade (March 2024) introduced cheap "blob" data, layer-2 fees fell to cents.
- Algorithmic stablecoins were fragile (he wrote thought experiments on this in May 2022, days after Terra collapsed).

**What he got wrong**
- Timelines. Proof of stake was "coming soon" from roughly 2015; it arrived in 2022. Sharding as originally conceived was abandoned for rollups.
- The 2017 ICO boom ran on Ethereum and funded thousands of tokens that went to zero; his early enthusiasm for token launches aged badly.
- The DAO hard fork (July 2016), which reversed a hack, contradicted "code is law" and remains the strongest precedent skeptics cite against Ethereum's neutrality.
- The rollup-centric roadmap fragmented liquidity across dozens of layer-2 chains and shifted fee revenue away from ETH itself; ETH underperformed Bitcoin substantially from 2022 through 2025, and the "ultrasound money" narrative around fee burning has largely faded.
- Layer-2 decentralization, which he set as a priority for 2026, is still mostly aspirational — most rollups run a single sequencer.

## Rules you can steal

1. **Apply the trilemma to every chain.** Ask what it gave up. If the answer is "nothing," the answer is decentralization.
2. **Watch what the founder criticizes, not what they promote.** Founders who attack their own ecosystem's excesses are giving you a free risk map.
3. **Roadmaps slip by years.** Discount any protocol timeline by at least 2x when pricing a token.
4. **Value accrual is not automatic.** A network can succeed while its token does not — Ethereum's 2022–2025 experience is the proof.
5. **Fees reveal the user base.** When transacting costs $50, only gamblers remain; culture follows cost.
6. **Governance tokens are not equity.** Coin voting is capturable and rarely confers cash-flow rights.
7. **Distinguish "credibly neutral" from "decentralized."** The DAO fork showed a decentralized network can still make a political decision.

## Key takeaways

- Buterin's core contribution is the generalization from money to programmable contracts, and the trilemma for reasoning about the costs of scale.
- "Endgame" explains why Ethereum scales through rollups: professional block builders, cheap verification for everyone.
- His major misses are timelines and value capture, not architecture; the Merge and rollups both eventually worked.
- His cultural critiques — memecoins, coin voting, speculation crowding out use — are as sharp as any skeptic's.
- The DAO fork and the layer-2 fragmentation problem are the two strongest critiques of his stewardship.
- Investors should read him for frameworks, not for price views; he consciously avoids giving any.
