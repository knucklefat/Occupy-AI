# The Skeptics

**In one sentence:** Crypto's serious critics — Nouriel Roubini, Molly White, Zeke Faux, Nassim Taleb, Stephen Diehl, and David Gerard — have been consistently right about fraud, leverage, and the gap between marketing and reality, and consistently wrong about price and survival; an investor who reads only the bulls will be blindsided by the first set of facts, and one who reads only the skeptics will have missed every rally since 2013.

## Who they are and what they argue

**Nouriel Roubini** — NYU economist famous for anticipating the 2008 crisis. In February 2018 he called Bitcoin the "mother of all bubbles," and in Senate Banking Committee testimony on October 11, 2018 he called crypto the "mother of all scams" and blockchain "the most over-hyped technology ever." His strongest points: most ICOs were scams or failures; "decentralization" is a myth when a few mining pools, exchanges, and developers control everything; volatility makes crypto useless as money; and Tether's opaque reserves propped up prices. His weakest: the repeated forecast that Bitcoin would go to zero. Bitcoin was about $6,300 at his testimony; it exceeded $126,000 in 2025. Adding irony, in 2023 he became involved in launching a tokenized, asset-backed "flatcoin" project of his own.

**Molly White** — software engineer and Wikipedia editor who launched *Web3 Is Going Just Great* in December 2021, a running timeline of hacks, rug pulls, and collapses with a "grift counter" totaling losses, and the newsletter *Citation Needed*. Her arguments are more careful than most: crypto mostly recreates the financial system without its consumer protections; "decentralization" is usually a marketing claim; blockchains are a poor database for almost every proposed non-financial use; and the industry's political spending (which she has covered extensively, including the 2025 Trump memecoin and World Liberty Financial) buys deregulation. Her 2024 review of Chris Dixon's *Read Write Own* is the sharpest single critique of the venture-capital case: it couldn't name one non-speculative blockchain product at scale, and it contained no disclosure of a16z's holdings. She discloses that she holds no meaningful crypto and profits from neither side.

**Zeke Faux** — Bloomberg reporter whose *Number Go Up* (2023) began as an attempt to prove Tether was a fraud and ended as a travelogue through the 2021–2022 mania: Sam Bankman-Fried in the Bahamas, NFT parties, El Salvador's Bitcoin experiment, Axie Infinity's Filipino players, and — most importantly — Cambodian "pig-butchering" scam compounds where trafficked workers run romance scams paid in Tether. He was right about FTX (he interviewed SBF before the collapse and captured the hollowness), about Axie, and about Tether's role in organized crime. He was wrong about Tether itself: he never found the missing money, and Tether went on to become one of the most profitable companies per employee on earth by holding U.S. Treasuries. The book's title is its own concession.

**Nassim Nicholas Taleb** — author of *The Black Swan*, who wrote a foreword to *The Bitcoin Standard* in 2018 and then reversed. His 2021 paper "Bitcoin, Currencies, and Fragility" argues that an asset with no yield, no underlying use, and a non-zero probability of going to zero has an expected value of zero (the "absorbing barrier" argument); that Bitcoin fails as a currency (too volatile), as an inflation hedge (it fell with tech stocks in 2022), as a safe haven, and as protection from tyranny (fully traceable). The rebuttal, made by many, is that the absorbing-barrier argument applies equally to gold, art, and any non-dividend-paying asset, and that Taleb ignores network and option value. He was right that Bitcoin trades as a risk asset; his "worth exactly zero" claim has been wrong for five years and counting.

**Stephen Diehl** — software engineer whose December 2021 essay "The Case Against Crypto" and 2022 book *Popping the Crypto Bubble* (with Jan Akalin and Darren Tseng) argue that crypto is a negative-sum game — the only money coming in is from new buyers — dressed up as technology; that tokens are unregistered securities; that DeFi is regulatory arbitrage; and that the software solves no problem better than a database. He organized the June 2022 letter from roughly 1,500 technologists urging Congress to resist crypto lobbying. Right: the 2022 contagion (Terra, Three Arrows, Celsius, Voyager, FTX) was precisely the greater-fool unwind he described. Wrong: he expected regulation to smother the industry; instead spot ETFs were approved (January 2024) and a federal stablecoin law passed (July 2025).

**David Gerard** — author of *Attack of the 50 Foot Blockchain* (2017) and *Libra Shrugged* (2020), and a blogger with a decade of acid coverage. His strongest calls were specific: Craig Wright was not Satoshi (the UK High Court agreed in March 2024); "enterprise blockchain" was consultancy vapor; Tether's issuance correlated suspiciously with Bitcoin rallies; Mt. Gox-style exchange failures would repeat. His weakness is tone and totality — he has treated every downturn as the end, and has been wrong each time.

## Where the skeptics have been right

- **Fraud and collapse:** Mt. Gox (2014), the ICO wave (2017–2018, most went to zero), BitConnect, QuadrigaCX, Terra/Luna (May 2022, about $40 billion erased), Three Arrows and Celsius (June–July 2022), FTX (November 2022, about $8 billion in customer funds missing, SBF sentenced to 25 years), and the October 2025 leverage cascade.
- **Leverage and contagion:** the 2022 chain reaction matched Diehl's and White's descriptions almost exactly.
- **"Decentralization theater":** most tokens are controlled by small teams and venture funds; most users hold coins on centralized exchanges.
- **Non-financial use cases:** supply-chain blockchains, NFT-based gaming, and "web3 social" have largely failed, as White and Gerard predicted.
- **Political capture:** the industry's 2024 election spending and the 2025 executive-branch conflicts were flagged early by White.

## Where the skeptics have been wrong

- **Price.** Bitcoin has made a new all-time high in every cycle since 2013 and traded near $78,000 in September 2026, roughly 30% below its peak but 12x Roubini's 2018 testimony level.
- **Survival.** Every "Bitcoin is dead" declaration has failed. The network has never been hacked.
- **Institutionalization.** Spot ETFs, S&P 500 inclusion for Coinbase (2025), bank custody, and stablecoin legislation happened despite their predictions of regulatory extinction.
- **Stablecoins.** Skeptics said Tether would collapse; instead dollar stablecoins became crypto's clearest product-market fit, settling trillions annually.
- **Overgeneralizing.** Treating Bitcoin, Ethereum, memecoins, and outright Ponzis as one thing missed the enormous dispersion between them.

## Rules you can steal

1. **Assume fraud until proven otherwise for any new intermediary.** The skeptics' hit rate on exchanges, lenders, and stablecoin issuers is superb.
2. **Ask "who is the buyer of last resort?"** If the only answer is "the next buyer," you're in a greater-fool trade — size it that way.
3. **Read the skeptic on your own holdings.** If you can't rebut White or Diehl on a specific project, you don't understand it.
4. **Separate "this is a scam" from "this will go down."** The skeptics conflated them; markets didn't.
5. **Track the grift counter, not just the price chart.** Losses to hacks and fraud are a recurring cost of the asset class.
6. **Discount totalizing claims from both sides.** "Worth zero" has been as wrong as "$1 million by 2025."
7. **Watch political spending as a risk signal.** Regulatory capture can reverse; rules written by the industry are not permanent.

## Key takeaways

- The skeptics' durable contribution is a catalog of failure modes — fraud, leverage, fake decentralization, non-existent use cases — that has predicted nearly every blow-up.
- Their consistent error is extending "this part is fraudulent" to "the whole thing will die," which has cost their followers every rally since 2013.
- Roubini and Taleb offer economic arguments (bubble dynamics, absorbing barriers); White, Faux, Diehl, and Gerard offer evidence (timelines, reporting, code).
- Tether is the skeptics' most conspicuous miss and their most legitimate ongoing concern at once.
- The 2024–2025 regulatory turn falsified the "regulation will end it" thesis but validated the "industry buys politics" one.
- Use the skeptics for risk management and due diligence; use no one for price predictions.
