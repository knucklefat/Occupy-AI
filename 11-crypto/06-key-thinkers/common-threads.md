# Common Threads

**In one sentence:** Strip away the tribal language and crypto's bulls and skeptics agree on far more than either side admits — most tokens go to zero, intermediaries are where the money is lost, leverage ends every cycle — and the disagreements that remain are mostly about one asset (Bitcoin), one question (does the technology solve a real problem), and one variable (time).

## Where bulls and skeptics agree

**1. Most tokens are worthless and will stay that way.** Chris Burniske's taxonomy, Vitalik Buterin's memecoin critiques, Lyn Alden's "altcoins are ventures," and Molly White's grift counter all describe the same distribution: a few networks with real use, thousands with none. The 2017 ICO cohort and the 2021 NFT cohort lost 90–99% and did not come back. Nobody serious disputes this anymore.

**2. Intermediaries, not protocols, are where money disappears.** Mt. Gox, QuadrigaCX, Celsius, FTX — every catastrophic loss happened at an exchange, lender, or custodian. Nic Carter's proof-of-reserves campaign and Zeke Faux's reporting are the same lesson from opposite ends: a coin on someone else's ledger is a claim, not an asset.

**3. Leverage ends every cycle.** Alden says euphoria and leverage cause the downturns; Diehl says the greater-fool chain breaks when new money stops; Burniske called the October 2025 liquidation cascade the moment that "broke crypto for a while"; Strategy's 2026 forced selling was the same mechanism at corporate scale. The bulls and skeptics describe identical dynamics and disagree only on whether they're a bug or the whole thing.

**4. "Decentralization" is mostly a claim, not a measurement.** Vitalik's trilemma says you can't have it all; Roubini and White say most projects don't even try. Solana's outages, rollups' single sequencers, and token-voting governance captured by whales are acknowledged on both sides.

**5. Stablecoins are the product that actually shipped.** Even skeptics concede dollar stablecoins settle trillions annually and have real demand in emerging markets — while pointing out that a dollar token is a strange victory for a movement that set out to replace the dollar. Cathie Wood cut her Bitcoin target partly because stablecoins took the payments role.

**6. The industry buys politics.** Builders (Armstrong, a16z) do it openly; skeptics (White, Diehl) document it. Nobody pretends the 2024–2025 regulatory turn was organic.

**7. Bitcoin is different in kind from the rest.** Skeptics like White and Faux focus their fire on altcoins, exchanges, and NFTs; even Taleb's paper is an argument about Bitcoin's value, not its integrity. A sixteen-year record of ledger security with no founder and no company is acknowledged, if grudgingly, across the divide.

## Where they don't agree

| Question | Bulls | Skeptics |
|---|---|---|
| Does Bitcoin have fundamental value? | Yes — scarcity, network effects, settlement finality, a hedge against fiscal dominance (Alden, Saylor, Carter) | No — no cash flow, no use, absorbing barrier at zero (Taleb, Roubini) |
| Does the tech solve a real problem? | Yes — trustless settlement, programmable money, open finance (Buterin, Adams, Dixon) | Rarely — a slow database with a speculative token bolted on (Diehl, White, Gerard) |
| Will volatility fall? | Yes, as the asset matures and cycles dampen (Alden) | No, or not enough to make it money (Taleb) |
| Is liquidity-driven price action a thesis or a warning? | A thesis: Bitcoin is the best debasement hedge (Hayes, Pal) | A warning: it's a leveraged Nasdaq, not a hedge (Taleb, Roubini) |
| What does time do? | Time is the bull's friend — each cycle brings higher lows and more adoption | Time is the skeptic's friend — each cycle reveals more fraud and no new use case |
| Energy | A feature that monetizes stranded power (Carter) | A cost with no offsetting benefit (Gerard, Diehl) |
| Regulation | Clarity unlocks adoption (Armstrong, a16z) | Capture removes protections (White) |

Notice that nearly every disagreement reduces to one of two things: whether Bitcoin specifically has value, and whether a real non-speculative use case beyond stablecoins will arrive. Everything else — fraud, leverage, fake decentralization, political money — is shared ground.

## The scorecard, side by side

- **Bulls were right about:** survival, price over any multi-year horizon, institutional adoption (ETFs in 2024, S&P 500 inclusion in 2025), liquidity as the dominant driver, and the durability of Bitcoin's ledger.
- **Bulls were wrong about:** nearly every dated price target (Hayes's $50,000 for 2018 and $250,000 for 2025; Pal's $450,000; Saylor's $150,000 by end-2025), the timing of every roadmap, "fat protocols," non-financial use cases, and the safety of leveraged structures (Terra, Strategy's premium).
- **Skeptics were right about:** fraud, contagion, the emptiness of most tokens, exchange risk, the failure of web3 gaming and social, Craig Wright, and political capture.
- **Skeptics were wrong about:** zero, extinction, regulatory death, Tether's collapse, and — repeatedly — the next rally.

## Rules you can steal: fifteen principles for a non-ideological investor

1. **Decide which question you're answering.** "Is Bitcoin valuable?" and "is this token valuable?" are different questions with different evidence; most arguments fail by mixing them.
2. **Hold protocols, not promises.** Coins you control in a wallet are assets; coins at an exchange, lender, or treasury company are claims on someone's solvency.
3. **Demand proof of reserves *and* liabilities** from any intermediary you use, and treat the absence as a red flag.
4. **Size every altcoin like a venture bet** — expect most to go to zero, cap the position, and never average down on a story.
5. **Use liquidity for direction, never for targets.** Rising global liquidity is a tailwind; no macro model justifies a specific price.
6. **Treat every dated price target as information about the forecaster.** Keep a log; hold people to it.
7. **Watch leverage, not the calendar.** Funding rates, open interest, corporate borrowers, and preferred-stock coupons predict tops better than halving countdowns.
8. **Compute the premium before buying any wrapper.** ETF versus trust versus treasury stock: know what you're paying for a dollar of underlying.
9. **Classify the token before you value it.** Money, commodity-like resource, or product token — and check whether fees actually flow to holders.
10. **Read the strongest skeptic on each holding.** If you can't rebut the specific critique, you don't understand your position.
11. **Discount the salesman's incentives.** Fund carry, token holdings, exchange volume, book sales, and political goals all bend analysis; disclosure helps but doesn't neutralize.
12. **Expect the J-curve.** Surviving networks lose 80% between hype and utility. Decide before buying whether you will hold through that.
13. **Assume roadmaps slip by years** and that "decentralized" means "less centralized than last year," not "no one is in charge."
14. **Separate "scam" from "will fall" and "not a scam" from "will rise."** The skeptics conflated the first pair; the bulls conflated the second.
15. **Let time be the tiebreaker.** Over any single year, the skeptics and bulls have each been right about half the time; over five-year windows, Bitcoin's survival and the altcoin graveyard have both been reliable. Position for what has been reliable.

## Key takeaways

- Bulls and skeptics agree on the failure modes: worthless tokens, fragile intermediaries, leverage-driven cycles, hollow decentralization claims, and political capture.
- They disagree mainly about Bitcoin's fundamental value and whether a real non-speculative use case beyond stablecoins will ever arrive.
- Each camp's record is lopsided: bulls win on survival and multi-year price, skeptics win on fraud and specific collapses; both lose on dated forecasts.
- The investor's job is to take the skeptics' due-diligence checklist and the bulls' regime framework and ignore both sides' price targets.
- Custody, position sizing, leverage monitoring, and incentive-discounting matter more than being on the "right side" of the ideological fight.
- Stablecoins are the one thing everyone now admits works — which is itself the most important fact about what crypto turned out to be.
