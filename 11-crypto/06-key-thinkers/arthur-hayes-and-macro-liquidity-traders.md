# Arthur Hayes and the Macro-Liquidity Traders

**In one sentence:** Arthur Hayes and Raoul Pal built the most widely followed macro framework in crypto — that Bitcoin and its high-beta cousins are essentially a bet on global liquidity and currency debasement — and their record is a clean split: excellent on direction and regime, consistently and loudly wrong on the size and timing of the resulting price moves.

## Who they are

**Arthur Hayes** (born 1985, Wharton) was an equity-derivatives trader at Deutsche Bank and Citigroup in Hong Kong before co-founding the exchange BitMEX in 2014, where he invented the perpetual swap — the futures contract with no expiry, funded by periodic payments between longs and shorts, that now dominates crypto trading volume. In October 2020 U.S. prosecutors charged him with Bank Secrecy Act violations; he pleaded guilty in 2022, served home confinement, paid a $10 million fine, and was pardoned in March 2025. He now runs the family office Maelstrom and writes long, profane, data-heavy essays on his Substack.

**Raoul Pal** ran hedge-fund sales at Goldman Sachs and co-managed GLG's global macro fund before retiring in 2004 to publish *Global Macro Investor*, an institutional research letter. He co-founded Real Vision, the financial video platform, in 2014. He announced he was "irresponsibly long" Bitcoin in 2020 and later Ethereum and Solana; his incentives are subscriptions, media, and a very public token portfolio.

## Core frameworks

**Hayes: follow the dollar plumbing.** His essays track the mechanics of dollar liquidity — Fed balance sheet, the Treasury General Account, reverse repo, bank reserves, and Treasury issuance — arguing that the *net* flow of dollars into the financial system, not the headline policy rate, drives Bitcoin. His recurring claim is that governments with unpayable debts must eventually inflate, that central banks will bend to their treasuries ("fiscal dominance" from a trader's angle), and that Bitcoin is the purest expression of that debasement. He also published influential design essays, including "Dust on Crust" (March 2023), which proposed a synthetic dollar built from a spot crypto position hedged with a short perpetual — the design later commercialized by Ethena.

**Pal: The Everything Code.** His framework has three moving parts: aging demographics mean growth can't come from productivity, so it comes from debt; debt at these levels can only be serviced by expanding liquidity, which is debasement by another name; and this produces a four-year "debt refinancing cycle" that lines up, in his telling, with Bitcoin's halving cycle. He estimates the debasement-plus-inflation "hurdle rate" at roughly 8% a year — anything that doesn't beat it loses purchasing power — and argues only exponential-adoption assets (technology, crypto) clear it reliably. Bitcoin, he points out, has been about 87% correlated with global liquidity and the Nasdaq about 97%: these assets track the amount of money in the system, not the news. His prescription: hold assets that absorb debasement, size for the cycle, and stop expecting central banks to change course.

## Scorecard

**What they got right**
- **The 2020 liquidity turn.** Both identified the March 2020 fiscal-monetary response as the trigger for a massive asset rally and positioned early; Bitcoin went from about $5,000 to $69,000 by November 2021.
- **Liquidity as the dominant driver.** Independent work (including Lyn Alden's 2024 study) confirmed Bitcoin follows global liquidity's direction in most 12-month windows. Their core claim is correct.
- **Hayes's early-2022 call** that Bitcoin would fall to around $30,000 and Ethereum toward $2,500 by mid-2022 was roughly right; Bitcoin hit $30,000 in May and $18,000 in June.
- **Pal's Solana call.** He said publicly in 2023 that Solana was his largest position when it traded in the $20s; it reached about $290 in January 2025.
- **Hayes's January 2026 diagnosis** that Bitcoin's weak 2025 came from contracting dollar liquidity (Fed quantitative tightening, a falling dollar-liquidity index) was a coherent explanation of why price stalled despite ETF inflows.
- **Stablecoin design.** "Dust on Crust" anticipated one of 2024's largest new products.

**What they got wrong**
- **Hayes, Consensus 2018:** predicted $50,000 by the end of 2018. Bitcoin ended the year near $3,700.
- **Hayes, 2022–2023 Fed pivot calls:** repeatedly predicted the Fed would reverse course imminently; it kept hiking until July 2023.
- **Hayes, Terra (April 2022):** in "Luna Brothers, Inc." he defended the design of the UST algorithmic stablecoin and disclosed being long LUNA weeks before it collapsed to zero in May 2022.
- **Hayes, 2025:** called $250,000 by year-end, trimmed to $150,000 in May, then reasserted $200,000 in November with the comment that if he was wrong it didn't matter. Bitcoin peaked at about $126,000 in October and ended the year in the high $80,000s.
- **Hayes, 2026:** in January he forecast a move above $110,000 in the first half with upside scenarios of $200,000–$750,000. Bitcoin instead fell to about $58,500 in the summer.
- **Pal, Ethereum 2021–2022:** talked about Ethereum reaching $20,000 during the 2021 cycle; it peaked near $4,900 and fell about 80% in 2022 while he remained "irresponsibly long."
- **Pal, the "banana zone" and $450,000 (May 2025):** predicted a liquidity-driven supercycle carrying Bitcoin to $450,000; it reached $126,000. Altcoins, which he expected to outperform in the "banana zone," largely didn't.
- **Both:** the four-year-cycle-equals-liquidity-cycle mapping is a fit to a small sample (three or four cycles) and has been retrofitted after each miss.

## Criticisms

- **Right framework, unbounded targets.** The liquidity thesis says *direction*; nothing in it justifies $250,000 versus $125,000. The targets are marketing.
- **Incentive to be loud.** Both monetize attention — Substack, Real Vision, conference keynotes — and extreme numbers travel further than ranges.
- **Hayes's exchange history.** BitMEX profited from the leverage that liquidates retail traders; his essays are written by a man who built the casino.
- **Selective memory.** Misses are rarely revisited in later essays; hits are.
- **Pal's audience risk.** "Irresponsibly long" told a retail audience to hold through an 80% drawdown in an asset (ETH) he had also predicted would 5x.

## Rules you can steal

1. **Track net dollar liquidity, not just the Fed funds rate.** Balance sheet, Treasury cash balance, reverse repo, and issuance mix matter more for risk assets than a 25-basis-point move.
2. **Use liquidity for regime, never for price targets.** Rising liquidity means "long bias"; it does not mean "$250,000."
3. **Demand the falsifier.** If a forecaster says being wrong doesn't matter, treat their forecast as entertainment.
4. **Separate a trader's plumbing analysis from their positioning talk.** Hayes's mechanics are worth reading; his year-end numbers are not.
5. **Beta cuts both ways.** If crypto is the highest-beta liquidity asset on the way up, it is on the way down too — size for the drawdown, not the target.
6. **Beware retrofitted cycles.** A four-year pattern with three or four observations is a hypothesis, not a law.
7. **Watch what they do, not what they say.** Both have sold into strength while publicly bullish; that is sensible, and it tells you how seriously to take the targets.

## Key takeaways

- Hayes and Pal's central claim — crypto is a leveraged bet on global liquidity and debasement — is well supported by data and by their own best calls (2020, early 2022).
- Their price targets have missed by 50–100% in most years they gave one, including 2018, 2025, and the first half of 2026.
- Hayes's essays are valuable for dollar-plumbing mechanics and product design (perpetual swaps, synthetic dollars); Pal's Everything Code is valuable as a regime map.
- Both are paid for attention, and both built their reputations on leverage — read accordingly.
- Use their frameworks to set directional bias and position size; use someone else's tools for entries and exits.
- The "four-year liquidity cycle" is a plausible story, not a proven cycle.
