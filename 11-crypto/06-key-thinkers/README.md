# Key Thinkers in Crypto

**In one sentence:** This section profiles the people whose frameworks shape how crypto is understood — founders, analysts, macro traders, venture capitalists, and the serious skeptics — and grades each of them on what they got right, what they got wrong, and what a non-ideological investor can actually reuse.

Crypto is unusual among asset classes in how much of its "analysis" is really argument between camps. Bitcoin maximalists, Ethereum builders, macro-liquidity traders, venture capitalists, and skeptics each carry a lens, and each lens has produced both prescient calls and embarrassing ones. You cannot evaluate a crypto thesis without knowing whose framework it came from, what that person is paid to believe, and how their past predictions aged.

Every profile below follows the same structure: who the person is, their core framework, a dated scorecard of hits and misses, transferable lessons, their best writing, and the strongest criticisms of them. Read the skeptics with the same seriousness as the bulls — the record shows each camp has been right about different things. Finish with `common-threads.md`, which distills everything into fifteen principles you can use regardless of where you land on the underlying debate.

## Rules you can steal

1. Read a bull and a skeptic on the same topic before forming a view.
2. Note each person's incentives (fund fees, token holdings, book sales, political goals) before weighting their opinion.
3. Treat every dated price target here as a data point about the forecaster, not about the asset.
4. Borrow frameworks (the trilemma, proof of reserves, liquidity regimes, MV = PQ), not conclusions.
5. When two thinkers with opposite incentives agree on something, treat it as close to settled.

## Key takeaways

- Crypto analysis is unusually camp-driven; knowing the source's framework and incentives is half the work.
- Bulls have been right about survival and multi-year price; skeptics have been right about fraud, leverage, and specific collapses.
- Almost no one in this section has a good record on dated price targets.
- The most reusable tools are the trilemma, the bearer-asset/claim distinction, proof of reserves, liquidity-regime analysis, and the mNAV premium calculation.
- `common-threads.md` collects the shared ground and fifteen principles that don't require picking a side.
