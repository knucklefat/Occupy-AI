# Michael Saylor and Strategy

**In one sentence:** Michael Saylor turned a mid-sized software company into the world's largest corporate Bitcoin holder by selling stock, convertible bonds, and preferred shares to buy coins — a strategy that produced spectacular returns while the stock traded at a premium to its Bitcoin, and a painful stress test in 2026 when the premium collapsed and the company sold Bitcoin for the first time since 2022.

## Who he is

Saylor (born 1965, MIT) co-founded MicroStrategy in 1989. He is a two-time cautionary tale before he is a Bitcoin evangelist: in March 2000 the company restated its earnings, the stock fell 62% in a day, and Saylor and two executives settled SEC accounting-fraud charges in December 2000 without admitting wrongdoing. In 2013 he tweeted that Bitcoin's days were numbered. On August 11, 2020, MicroStrategy bought its first $250 million of Bitcoin as a treasury reserve. Saylor moved from CEO to executive chairman in August 2022, the company renamed itself Strategy in February 2025, and in June 2024 he and the company paid $40 million to settle a District of Columbia tax-fraud case. He is the industry's most relentless salesman — "there is no second best" — and its most watched balance sheet.

## The corporate treasury thesis

Saylor's argument has three steps:

1. **Cash is a melting ice cube.** After 2020's monetary expansion, holding dollars guarantees a loss of purchasing power. A treasury should hold the scarcest liquid asset instead.
2. **Bitcoin is digital property.** He frames it as the apex asset — scarce, portable, divisible, and un-dilutable — that will absorb value from bonds, gold, and real estate over decades. His long-range targets have ranged from $13 million per coin by 2045 (2024) to $21 million by 2046 (2025).
3. **A public company can buy more Bitcoin per share by using the capital markets.** Because Strategy's stock has traded above the value of its Bitcoin, selling shares and buying coins *increases* Bitcoin per share for existing holders. Strategy calls this "BTC Yield" and reports it as a key metric.

## The leverage mechanics

- **At-the-market (ATM) equity sales.** Sell stock continuously into the market, buy Bitcoin. When the stock trades at, say, 2x its Bitcoin value, every $2 raised buys $2 of coin while diluting holders of only $1 of underlying — accretive as long as the premium persists.
- **Convertible bonds.** Roughly $6.7 billion outstanding (September 2026), mostly zero- or low-coupon, sold to hedge funds who want the equity volatility rather than the yield.
- **Perpetual preferred stock.** Starting in 2025 the company introduced a family of preferreds — STRK, STRF, STRD, STRC, and euro-denominated STRE — paying fixed dividends. By September 2026 preferreds totaled about $14.6 billion, with STRC alone at $9.6 billion and a dividend rate raised in steps to over 11%.
- **The "21/21" and "42/42" plans** (October 2024 and 2025) announced targets of $42 billion and then $84 billion of capital to raise, split between equity and fixed-income.

The result, as of September 11, 2026: 845,050 BTC (about 4% of all Bitcoin that will ever exist) at an average cost of roughly $75,400 per Strategy's own purchase ledger (about $75,800 on trackers that include fees), against about $21 billion of debt and preferred claims that sit ahead of common shareholders.

## Scorecard

**What worked**
- From the first purchase (August 2020, Bitcoin near $11,600) through the November 2024 peak, MSTR was among the best-performing large U.S. stocks, outperforming Bitcoin itself.
- The stock joined the Nasdaq-100 in December 2024, pulling in passive index buying.
- He proved that a public company could become a Bitcoin fund with a permanent-capital advantage over ETFs (no redemptions), and dozens of "digital asset treasury" (DAT) companies copied the playbook in 2025.

**What broke**
- **The premium collapsed.** mNAV (market cap divided by the value of the Bitcoin held) peaked near 3.9x in November 2024, fell below 1.0x in November 2025, ranged from 0.82x to 1.43x over the following year, and sat at about 1.07x in September 2026. Below 1x, selling stock to buy Bitcoin *dilutes* holders instead of enriching them.
- **Fixed obligations met a falling asset.** Bitcoin fell from its $126,000 peak (October 2025) to about $58,500 in summer 2026. Strategy reported a $14.5 billion unrealized loss in the first quarter of 2026 while owing well over $1 billion a year in preferred dividends with a cash-flow-negative software business.
- **"Never sell" ended.** On June 1, 2026, Strategy disclosed a $2.5 million Bitcoin sale to fund preferred dividends — its first sale since 2022 — and the stock fell. Over the summer it sold roughly $544 million of Bitcoin across four transactions. In late July the board authorized further sales to build a $1.25 billion cash reserve, repurchase up to $1 billion of preferreds, and buy back up to $1 billion of common stock. It resumed buying on August 31, 2026 ($370 million, funded by new share sales).
- **Price calls.** Saylor's October 2025 expectation of roughly $150,000 by year-end missed badly; Bitcoin ended 2025 in the high $80,000s.
- **Index risk.** In late 2025 MSCI consulted on excluding companies whose assets are mostly digital tokens, threatening billions of passive outflows; in January 2026 it declined to adopt that 50%-crypto-holdings screen. The reprieve was temporary: in late August 2026 MSCI proposed a broader "non-operating company" test (operating assets below 50% of total assets) that, as drafted, would delete Strategy, Metaplanet and uranium holder Yellow Cake from its global indexes at the November 2026 review (consultation closes September 30; results due October 16). JPMorgan estimated roughly $2.8 billion of passive outflows from MSCI alone, up to about $11.6 billion if other index providers followed (as reported by Decrypt, September 1, 2026). Strategy formally objected. Index inclusion can be revoked.

## Criticisms

- **Reflexivity.** Stock premium funds Bitcoin purchases, purchases lift Bitcoin, Bitcoin lifts the stock, the higher premium funds more purchases. This works in both directions, and 2025–2026 showed the downside loop.
- **Dilution.** The share count roughly quadrupled between 2020 and 2026. "BTC Yield" measures Bitcoin per share, not return per share, and ignores the claims of preferred and convertible holders.
- **Capital-structure fragility.** Preferreds with 10–11% coupons and penalty rates for missed payments are expensive money attached to an asset with no cash flow; dividends must ultimately be paid by selling stock or coins.
- **The "no second best" rhetoric** — including advice to sell homes to buy Bitcoin — is sales, not risk management.
- **Concentration.** One leveraged entity holding 4% of supply is a systemic single point of failure that skeptics and Bitcoiners alike worry about.

## What a retail investor should take from it

Strategy is a leveraged, fee-free-but-dilutive Bitcoin proxy whose return relative to Bitcoin depends almost entirely on the premium (mNAV). Buying it at 2–3x mNAV means paying $2–3 for $1 of coins and betting the premium holds; buying below 1x means getting coins at a discount but also inheriting $21 billion of senior claims and an unprofitable software business. Since spot ETFs (January 2024) offer clean exposure, the only rational reasons to own MSTR are a view on the premium or a mandate that prohibits ETFs. The 2026 episode is the case study: fixed obligations, a volatile asset, and a vanishing premium are a bad combination in a drawdown.

## Rules you can steal

1. **Always compute mNAV before buying a treasury company.** Above ~1.5x, you are paying for the story; below 1x, you are being paid to take balance-sheet risk.
2. **Fixed obligations plus volatile assets equals forced selling, eventually.** Ask what pays the coupon when the asset falls 50%.
3. **Reflexive flywheels run in reverse.** Any strategy that works "as long as the premium holds" has a hidden short-volatility position.
4. **Discount the salesman, study the filings.** Saylor's 10-Qs and 8-Ks are more informative than his interviews.
5. **Per-share metrics can be redefined.** Check whether a company's headline KPI (like "BTC Yield") counts senior claims.
6. **Prefer the clean instrument.** If you want Bitcoin, spot ETFs or self-custody give you Bitcoin; MSTR gives you Bitcoin plus a capital-structure bet.
7. **Track the copycats.** When dozens of companies clone a leveraged strategy, the strategy has become the crowded trade.

## Key takeaways

- Saylor's thesis — hold the scarcest asset, buy it with cheap capital-market money — worked brilliantly while the stock traded at a large premium to its Bitcoin.
- The engine is the mNAV premium; when it fell below 1x in late 2025, issuing stock stopped being accretive.
- By September 2026 Strategy held roughly 845,000 BTC against about $21 billion of debt and preferred stock, and had sold Bitcoin in 2026 to pay preferred dividends.
- Reflexivity, dilution, and fixed coupons are the three structural criticisms, and all three showed up in the 2026 drawdown.
- Saylor's past includes a 2000 SEC settlement and a 2024 tax settlement; weigh his charisma accordingly.
- For most retail investors, spot Bitcoin or an ETF is the cleaner exposure; MSTR is a bet on the premium.
