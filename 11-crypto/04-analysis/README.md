# Crypto Analysis: How to Evaluate an Asset or Protocol

Crypto assets don't file 10-Ks, but they publish something equities never do: the entire ledger of who holds what, every fee paid, and every line of the code that moves the money. This section is about turning that transparency into judgment. It covers the fundamental side (reading a whitepaper critically, dissecting tokenomics, analyzing protocol revenue like a business, sizing up the team and the venture capital behind it, and assessing the code and bridge risk that can zero a position overnight), the data side (on-chain metrics and the tools that expose them, plus which pieces of technical analysis actually have evidence behind them), and the framing side (Bitcoin valuation models, and the narratives and cycles that move prices more than fundamentals do). Every file has worked examples, tables, a step-by-step "how to actually do it," and links to the sources used. Current figures are marked as of September 2026 and will age; the methods won't.

- [technical-analysis-in-crypto.md](technical-analysis-in-crypto.md) — 24/7 markets, what carries over from equities, the 200-week MA, funding rates and open interest, liquidation heatmaps, and the evidence on momentum versus chart folklore.

## Suggested reading order

Start with the whitepaper and tokenomics files, since most bad crypto investments fail at those two gates. Then protocol fundamentals and risk assessment for anything you'd actually deposit into. Read the Bitcoin valuation, on-chain, and cycle files together; they share vocabulary (realized cap, MVRV, liquidity) and disagree productively about what it means.
