# Research Desks and Flagship Reports

**In one sentence:** Institutional-grade crypto research is now abundant and mostly free, but nearly all of it is published by firms that sell crypto products, so treat it as well-sourced sell-side research: mine the data, discount the conclusions.

## How to read crypto research

Every desk below is funded by one of three things: asset management fees (Fidelity, Grayscale, Bitwise, ARK, Galaxy), exchange or trading revenue (Coinbase, Kaiko, K33), or data subscriptions and venture money (Messari, Coin Metrics, Glassnode, Delphi, The Block, a16z). None of them is paid to tell you to sell. The useful habit is to separate the **data** (usually excellent, often unavailable elsewhere) from the **framing** (usually bullish, sometimes by omission). The genuinely neutral sources are the central banks and academics in [academic-papers-and-research.md](academic-papers-and-research.md) and the regulators in [regulators-and-official-guides.md](regulators-and-official-guides.md).

Cost: everything below is free unless stated.

## Asset managers

**[Fidelity Digital Assets: Research and Insights](https://www.fidelitydigitalassets.com/research-and-insights)** — Free. Bias: Fidelity custodies and sells Bitcoin and Ether products. What to read: the long-form pieces on Bitcoin as an "aspirational store of value," the annual institutional investor survey (the best data on what pensions and endowments actually own), and the quarterly "Signals" reports. Written for allocators; sober tone; conclusions lean bullish on BTC and cautious on everything else.

**[Galaxy Research](https://www.galaxy.com/insights/research/)** — Free. Bias: Galaxy is a trading firm, asset manager and miner. What to read: the quarterly venture-funding reports (the standard reference for crypto VC data), the annual predictions piece, mining-economics research, and Alex Thorn's policy notes. Some of the most detailed protocol-level analysis available outside the projects themselves.

**[Bitwise: Crypto Market Insights](https://bitwiseinvestments.com/crypto-market-insights)** — Free. Bias: an ETF issuer; CIO Matt Hougan's weekly memos are explicitly written to help financial advisers justify allocations. What to read: the quarterly Crypto Market Review, the annual adviser survey (how much of the RIA channel is allocated), and the CIO memos as a clear statement of the mainstream bull case.

**[Grayscale Research](https://research.grayscale.com/)** — Free. Bias: the largest crypto fund issuer; research tends to support whatever products Grayscale is launching. What to read: the monthly market commentary, the "Crypto Sectors" taxonomy (a sensible way to categorize tokens), and the quarterly "Top 20" list — useful as a window into what institutional product teams are considering.

**[ARK Invest: Big Ideas 2026](https://www.ark-invest.com/big-ideas-2026)** — Free. Bias: ARK is the most aggressively bullish large asset manager, with published Bitcoin price targets in the millions. What to read: the Bitcoin chapter for the maximal bull case, framed in numbers you can check. Treat the targets as marketing; the on-chain charts are drawn from good sources (Glassnode, Coin Metrics).

**[Coinbase Institutional: Research and Insights](https://www.coinbase.com/institutional/research-insights)** — Free. Bias: the largest US exchange; research is written by a small team led by David Duong and has a reputation for being more balanced than the brand implies. What to read: the monthly outlook, the annual "Year in Review," and the periodic deep-dives on staking, stablecoins and market structure.

## Data-first research houses

**[Coin Metrics: State of the Network](https://coinmetrics.substack.com/)** — Free weekly. Bias: a data vendor, so the incentive is to look rigorous rather than bullish; one of the least promotional sources on this page. What to read: every issue is a short, chart-heavy essay on one topic (mining economics, stablecoin flows, ETF holdings, network activity). The quarterly market updates are a compact review of the whole space. Free charts at [charts.coinmetrics.io](https://charts.coinmetrics.io/).

**[Glassnode Insights](https://insights.glassnode.com/)** — Free weekly ("The Week On-chain") plus paid tiers. Bias: an on-chain analytics vendor; its framing is that on-chain data predicts price, which is a claim it sells. What to read: The Week On-chain for the state of holder behaviour (realized profit/loss, long-term-holder supply, exchange balances). Learn the metrics, but note that most on-chain "indicators" have been fitted to a short history with few cycles.

**[Messari Research](https://messari.io/research)** — Mostly paid (Pro subscription; some reports free). Bias: Messari sells data and takes paid "protocol reporting" engagements from the projects it covers, which it discloses. What to read: the annual "Crypto Theses" (free, long, opinionated) and the quarterly protocol reports, remembering which ones were commissioned.

**[Kaiko Research](https://research.kaiko.com/)** — Free weekly plus paid data. Bias: a market-data vendor serving institutions; neutral on price, focused on liquidity and market structure. What to read: the weekly "Data Debrief" on exchange liquidity, spreads, volumes and stablecoin depegs. The best public source on how crypto markets actually trade.

**[K33 Research](https://k33.com/research)** — Free weekly with paid tiers. Bias: a Nordic brokerage and asset manager. What to read: the weekly "Ahead of the Curve" on derivatives positioning, ETF flows and funding rates; consistently one of the more willing to say "we are bearish near term."

**[Delphi Digital](https://www.delphidigital.io/)** — Mostly paid (Delphi Pro); some reports free. Bias: Delphi has a venture arm and advisory business that works with many of the protocols it researches. What to read: the annual "Year Ahead" series and deep protocol reports, which are the most detailed in the industry. Assume every project covered may be a portfolio company unless stated otherwise.

**[The Block Research](https://www.theblock.co/research)** — Paid (Pro) with free summaries; free data dashboards at [theblock.co/data](https://www.theblock.co/data). Bias: The Block is a news and data company that was once part-owned by an FTX-linked loan, which it disclosed after the fact; the research team is independent and the annual "Digital Asset Outlook" is comprehensive. What to read: the data dashboards (exchange volumes, stablecoin supply, L2 activity) and the annual outlook.

## Venture funds

**[a16z crypto: State of Crypto 2025](https://a16zcrypto.com/posts/article/state-of-crypto-report-2025/)** — Free annual. Bias: the largest crypto VC; the report exists to show the industry is growing. What to read: the adoption data (active addresses, stablecoin volumes, developer counts) with the caveat that a16z chooses which metrics to show. The methodology notes are honest about limitations; the headline claims are not always.

**[Chainalysis: Crypto Crime Report 2026](https://www.chainalysis.com/reports/crypto-crime-2026/)** — Free (email gate). Bias: a blockchain-analytics vendor that sells to law enforcement, so it has an interest in crime looking both serious (to sell tools) and traceable (to sell tools). What to read: the annual crime report is the standard reference for scam, ransomware, hack and sanctions-evasion volumes, and the [Geography of Cryptocurrency / Global Adoption Index](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/2025-global-crypto-adoption-index/) is the best data on which countries actually use crypto. The full [reports archive](https://www.chainalysis.com/reports/) is worth bookmarking.

## Official-sector research (the neutral counterweight)

**[BIS Annual Economic Report 2022, Chapter III: "The future monetary system"](https://www.bis.org/publ/arpdf/ar2022e3.htm)** — Free. The Bank for International Settlements' clearest statement of the central-bank view: crypto's structural flaws (fragmentation, congestion, no nominal anchor) are inherent, and stablecoins borrow their credibility from the fiat money they claim to replace. Bias: the central bankers' central bank is institutionally skeptical of anything that competes with central-bank money. Also see the BIS working papers listed in [academic-papers-and-research.md](academic-papers-and-research.md).

**[Federal Reserve: FEDS Notes](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/default.htm)** — Free. Search for "stablecoin," "crypto" and "tokenization"; the notes are short, empirical and refreshingly uninterested in price. The New York Fed's [Liberty Street Economics](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/) blog covers the same ground with more charts.

**[Financial Stability Board: Crypto-assets and global stablecoins](https://www.fsb.org/work-of-the-fsb/financial-innovation-and-structural-change/crypto-assets-and-global-stablecoins/)** — Free. The G20 body's regulatory framework and periodic risk assessments; dry, but this is where global policy is actually coordinated.

## What to read each week, month and year

| Cadence | Read | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | Coin Metrics State of the Network, Kaiko Data Debrief, Glassnode Week On-chain, K33 Ahead of the Curve | Data, market structure, on-chain, derivatives — four angles, four vendors |
| Monthly | Coinbase Institutional outlook, Grayscale monthly, Bitwise CIO memos | What the mainstream product channel is telling advisers |
| Quarterly | Galaxy venture report, Bitwise Crypto Market Review, The Block data | Where the money went |
| Annually | a16z State of Crypto, Chainalysis Crime Report and Adoption Index, Messari Theses, Delphi Year Ahead, ARK Big Ideas, BIS Annual Report chapter | Adoption, crime, opinion, maximal bull case, and the central-bank rebuttal |

## Key takeaways

- Almost every crypto research desk is funded by selling crypto products or data; the data is usually excellent and the conclusions are usually for sale.
- The most neutral private-sector sources are the data vendors (Coin Metrics, Kaiko) because their business does not depend on prices rising.
- Read a16z, ARK and Bitwise for the best-argued bull case, then the BIS and Fed notes for the best-argued institutional skepticism, and form your own view in the gap.
- Chainalysis is the reference for crime and adoption numbers; note that it profits from both the problem and the solution.
- Disclosure quality varies: Messari and Delphi tell you when a report was commissioned or a project is a portfolio company; assume undisclosed conflicts elsewhere.
