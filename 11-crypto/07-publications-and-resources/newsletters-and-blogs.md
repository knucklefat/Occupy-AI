# Newsletters and Blogs

**In one sentence:** A good crypto inbox mixes one data-driven weekly, one or two thoughtful bulls, at least one professional skeptic and a primary-source protocol digest, and skips anything that is paid by the projects it covers without saying so.

## How this list is organized

Each entry notes **cadence**, **cost** and **bias**. "Bias" here is not an insult; it is the business model. Venture-fund blogs exist to raise the value of portfolio companies. Exchange newsletters exist to increase trading. Independent subscription newsletters exist to keep subscribers, which rewards being interesting and occasionally being right. Skeptic newsletters exist to keep skeptical subscribers, which rewards finding fraud and occasionally overstating it. Read across the categories.

## Data-driven weeklies (start here)

**[Coin Metrics: State of the Network](https://coinmetrics.substack.com/)** — Weekly. Free. Bias: data vendor, low. One topic per issue, charted from Coin Metrics' own data; the single best free weekly for someone who wants facts before opinions. Also in [research-desks-and-reports.md](research-desks-and-reports.md).

**[Galaxy Research: Insights](https://www.galaxy.com/insights/)** — Several times a week. Free. Bias: trading firm and asset manager. Alex Thorn's weekly brief and the research team's protocol and policy notes are among the most detailed in the industry; the firm's book is long crypto, so read the framing accordingly.

**[Glassnode: The Week On-chain](https://insights.glassnode.com/)** — Weekly. Free tier. Bias: sells on-chain analytics, so it believes on-chain analytics work. The reference for holder behaviour, realized profit/loss and supply dynamics; learn the vocabulary here.

**[Bitcoin Optech](https://bitcoinops.org/)** — Weekly. Free. Bias: none of the usual kind; funded by grants to serve Bitcoin developers. A technical digest of what is actually changing in Bitcoin Core, Lightning and wallets. Dry and hard for beginners, but if you want to know whether a "Bitcoin upgrade" headline is real, this is the source.

**[Bankless: Ethereum Weekly](https://www.bankless.com/ethereum-weekly)** — Weekly. Free. Bias: Bankless is an Ethereum-native media company with a token-holding audience. The closest current successor to *Week in Ethereum News*, which [shut down in December 2024](https://weekinethereum.substack.com/) after eight years when funding dried up; its archive is still the best record of Ethereum's development history. The [Ethereum Foundation blog](https://blog.ethereum.org/) is the primary source for protocol upgrades.

## Thoughtful bulls

**[Lyn Alden Investment Strategy](https://www.lynalden.com/)** — Monthly-ish public articles; paid premium tier. Bias: macro analyst who holds Bitcoin and says so. The best writer for a traditional investor trying to fit Bitcoin into a macro framework; she also covers energy, equities and bonds, which keeps the crypto view proportionate.

**[Vitalik Buterin's blog](https://vitalik.eth.limo/)** — Irregular. Free. Bias: Ethereum's co-founder, who nonetheless publishes more self-criticism than most projects' critics. Long, technical, and the place where Ethereum's direction is actually argued out. The 2022 collection *Proof of Stake* is drawn from here.

**[Nic Carter on Medium](https://medium.com/@nic__carter)** — Irregular. Free. Bias: Castle Island Ventures partner and Bitcoin investor; contrarian within the industry (he broke the "Operation Choke Point 2.0" story and has attacked Bitcoin's own energy-FUD and "proof of reserves" theatre). Long essays; his site [niccarter.info](https://niccarter.info/) indexes them.

**[Arthur Hayes: Crypto Trader Digest](https://cryptohayes.substack.com/)** — Roughly monthly. Free. Bias: BitMEX co-founder and family-office investor, permanently long, prone to grand macro theories and colourful language. Read for the derivatives and liquidity mechanics, which he understands better than almost anyone; discount the price targets heavily.

**[a16z crypto](https://a16zcrypto.com/posts/article/state-of-crypto-report-2025/)** — Weekly newsletter plus long posts. Free. Bias: the largest crypto VC; everything published advances the portfolio. High production quality, genuinely useful explainer content (the "Crypto Canon" reading list, builder guides), and the annual State of Crypto report.

**[Paradigm: Writing](https://www.paradigm.xyz/writing)** — Irregular. Free. Bias: a top-tier VC and research shop; publishes serious technical research (MEV, AMM design, mechanism design) that stands on its own even though it serves the portfolio. For the intermediate-to-advanced reader.

**[Multicoin Capital: Blog](https://multicoin.capital/blog/)** — Irregular. Free. Bias: a thesis-driven fund famous for early Solana conviction; the posts are investment theses, not neutral analysis. Worth reading precisely because they state the bet and the reasoning explicitly.

**[Dragonfly: Writing](https://writing.dragonfly.xyz/)** — Irregular. Free. Bias: crypto VC. Haseeb Qureshi's essays and the firm's annual airdrop and compensation reports are unusually candid about what does and does not work in token launches.

**[Variant Fund: Writing](https://variant.fund/writing)** and **[Placeholder: Blog](https://www.placeholder.vc/blog)** — Irregular. Free. Bias: VCs. Variant (Jesse Walden) on "ownership economy" theses; Placeholder (Chris Burniske, Joel Monegro) is where the "fat protocols" and token-valuation ideas originated. Dated in places, but foundational to how funds think.

**[Bitcoin Magazine](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/)** — Daily. Free. Bias: owned by BTC Inc., which runs the Bitcoin conferences and is unapologetically maximalist. Useful for Bitcoin-specific news and policy; hostile to everything else.

## Independent and mixed

**[Bankless](https://www.bankless.com/)** — Daily newsletter, weekly podcast. Free with paid tier. Bias: Ethereum and DeFi native; takes protocol sponsorships and was entangled with BanklessDAO and its BANK token before the media company separated from the DAO in 2023. Broad, enthusiastic, well-produced; treat token coverage as promotion until proven otherwise.

**[The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/)** — Daily. Free with paid tier. Bias: DeFi-focused newsroom founded by Camila Russo (*The Infinite Machine*); sympathetic to the sector it covers but staffed by real reporters. The best dedicated DeFi news source.

**[Milk Road](https://www.milkroad.com/)** — Daily. Free. Bias: a five-minute, jokey "daily briefing" that grew fast, was sold, and now sells ads and affiliate exchange links. Fine as a headline scan for beginners; do not mistake the tone for analysis.

**[Blockworks Newsletters](https://blockworks.co/newsletter)** — Daily and weekly. Free. Bias: a crypto media and research company (also runs Empire, Lightspeed and 0xResearch podcasts); institutional in tone, sponsored by protocols. The daily "Blockworks Daily" and the "Lightspeed" (Solana) newsletters are informative; note the sponsors.

**[Nathaniel Whittemore: The Breakdown](https://nlw.substack.com/)** — Daily. Free. The written companion to the podcast; a fair-minded daily summary of what the industry is arguing about. NLW's day job is now AI coverage, and the crypto output is lighter than it was.

**[BitMEX Research](https://blog.bitmex.com/)** — Irregular. Free. Bias: a derivatives exchange. Surprisingly rigorous technical research, including the serialized *Blocksize War* and long analyses of stablecoin reserves and exchange proof-of-reserves.

**[The Daily Gwei](https://www.thedailygwei.com/)** — Daily video plus newsletter. Free. Bias: Anthony Sassano is an Ethereum investor and educator; explicitly bullish, transparent about it. The best daily for someone who wants to follow Ethereum specifically.

**[Rekt News](https://rekt.news/)** — Weekly-ish. Free. Bias: anonymous DeFi insiders; irreverent. Post-mortems of every major DeFi hack and exploit, with a running [leaderboard](https://rekt.news/leaderboard/) of losses. The best education in how smart contracts actually fail.

**[Stephen Diehl](https://www.stephendiehl.com/)** — Irregular. Free. Bias: software engineer and co-author of *Popping the Crypto Bubble*; a critic who understands the technology, which makes the criticism harder to dismiss.

## Skeptics and watchdogs

**[Citation Needed](https://www.citationneeded.news/)** — Molly White. Weekly-ish. Free with optional paid support. Bias: the most prominent crypto critic, a Wikipedia editor by background who documents rather than sneers. Her [Web3 Is Going Just Great](https://www.web3isgoinggreat.com/) timeline is a running ledger of hacks, rug pulls and collapses with dollar totals. Every crypto investor should read her to know what the other side has on file.

**[Matt Levine: Money Stuff](https://www.bloomberg.com/account/newsletters/money-stuff)** — Daily. Free (email; the web version is behind Bloomberg's paywall). Bias: a former banker and securities lawyer who finds crypto hilarious rather than evil. His coverage of FTX, Terra, Celsius, "crypto is the securities laws speed-run" and the "Bitcoin is a mining game we play at Bloomberg terminals" era is the best-written financial explanation of anything in this section. Not crypto-only; roughly a fifth of issues touch it.

**[Doomberg](https://doomberg.substack.com/)** — Twice weekly. Paid (with a free preview). Bias: an anonymous energy-and-commodities team that is dismissive of Bitcoin's "digital gold" case and has a long record of correctly calling the energy side of mining. Read for the counter-narrative on inflation hedges and energy; the tone is confident and sometimes wrong.

**[David Gerard](https://davidgerard.co.uk/blockchain/)** — Weekly-ish. Free with paid support. Bias: professional critic, author of *Attack of the 50 Foot Blockchain*; assumes bad faith by default and is usually, but not always, right. Reliable for chronology of scams; unreliable for the bull case.

**[Amy Castor](https://amycastor.com/)** — Irregular. Free. Bias: independent journalist and long-time Tether and Bitfinex critic who co-writes with Gerard. Careful about documents and court filings; the place to go when a stablecoin story breaks.

**[Protos](https://protos.com/)** — Daily. Free. Bias: a small news site with a skeptical editorial line and a specialty in Tether, exchange solvency and influencer conflicts. Short, well-sourced, unglamorous.

**[FT Alphaville](https://www.ft.com/alphaville)** — Daily. Paid (FT subscription; some free). Bias: the *Financial Times'* irreverent markets blog, institutionally skeptical of crypto and very good on the financial-plumbing side of Tether, MicroStrategy and ETF flows. Also the FT's [cryptocurrencies](https://www.ft.com/cryptocurrencies) section for mainstream coverage.

## Mainstream and news

**[CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/)** — Daily. Free. Bias: the industry's paper of record; was owned by Digital Currency Group (Grayscale's parent) during the period it broke the FTX story, and is now owned by Bullish, an exchange. Editorial independence has been tested, and mostly held; the [podcasts](https://www.coindesk.com/podcasts) and daily newsletters are fine for headlines.

**[The Block](https://www.theblock.co/)** — Daily. Free with paid Pro. Bias: news and data company; disclosed after the fact that its former CEO had taken loans from an SBF entity. The reporting is good and the data dashboards are excellent.

## A sensible inbox

| If you want | Subscribe to |
|---|---|
| Facts every week, no opinions | Coin Metrics State of the Network, Bitcoin Optech |
| The bull case argued well | Lyn Alden, Vitalik's blog, Nic Carter, Galaxy |
| The bear case argued well | Citation Needed, Matt Levine, Doomberg, Stephen Diehl |
| Daily headlines in five minutes | Blockworks Daily, The Breakdown, Milk Road (with salt) |
| Ethereum and DeFi specifically | Ethereum Weekly, The Daily Gwei, The Defiant, Rekt |
| To understand token launches and VC thinking | a16z, Paradigm, Multicoin, Dragonfly |
| Court filings and exchange solvency | Amy Castor, Protos, FT Alphaville |

## Key takeaways

- The lowest-bias sources are data vendors (Coin Metrics, Glassnode) and grant-funded technical digests (Bitcoin Optech); everything else is selling something.
- Pair every bull (Alden, Hayes, a16z) with a skeptic (White, Levine, Doomberg); the disagreement is where the information is.
- Venture-fund blogs publish genuinely useful research, and every post advances a portfolio; both things are true.
- *Week in Ethereum News* is gone; use Bankless's Ethereum Weekly and the Ethereum Foundation blog, and keep the old archive for history.
- If a newsletter's revenue comes from exchange affiliate links or project sponsorships, its token coverage is advertising.
