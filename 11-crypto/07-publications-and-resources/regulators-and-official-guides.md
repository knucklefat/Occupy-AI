# Regulators and Official Guides

**In one sentence:** The official sources are free, unglamorous and the only places where the rules are actually written down — use them to check whether an exchange is licensed, what you owe in tax, how to report a scam and what the GENIUS Act and MiCA actually require, rather than relying on an influencer's summary.

## Why read regulators

Regulatory pages are the least biased sources in crypto in one specific sense: they do not want you to buy anything. They are biased in another sense (institutional caution, a preference for the existing financial system, and in the US, sharp swings with each administration), so read them as "what the law says and what the enforcer thinks," not as investment analysis. As of September 2026 the US posture has swung from enforcement-first (2021–2024) to rule-writing (2025–), and several pages below were updated within the last year; check dates.

## United States: federal

### Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)

**[SEC: Cyber, Crypto Assets and Emerging Technology](https://www.sec.gov/securities-topics/crypto-assets)** — The hub page linking enforcement history, investor alerts, FinHub and the task force. Start here.

**[SEC Crypto Task Force](https://www.sec.gov/securities-topics/crypto-task-force)** — Created in January 2025 under Commissioner Hester Peirce to write rules instead of bringing cases. The page holds [roundtable](https://www.sec.gov/about/crypto-task-force/crypto-task-force-roundtables) recordings, written submissions from the industry and the staff statements that now define which tokens the SEC considers securities. In August 2026 the Commission proposed [Regulation Crypto Assets](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/peirce-statement-regulation-crypto-assets-081826), an offering framework with startup and fundraising exemptions and a safe harbor for "delinking" a token from its original investment contract; read Peirce's statement for the clearest summary, and remember the rule is proposed, not final.

**[SEC Crypto Assets and Cyber Enforcement Actions](https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-enforcement/cyber-crypto-assets-emerging-technology/enforcement-actions)** — The list of every crypto enforcement action. Search a project's name here before investing; a settled action is a fact, not FUD.

**[Investor.gov: Crypto Assets spotlight](https://www.investor.gov/additional-resources/spotlight/crypto-assets)** — The SEC's plain-language investor education site: alerts on celebrity endorsements, "pig butchering," fake trading platforms and the difference between a broker-dealer and a crypto platform. Free and written for beginners.

### Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC)

**[CFTC: Digital Assets](https://www.cftc.gov/digitalassets/index.htm)** — Customer advisories, a primer on virtual currencies, and the CFTC's jurisdiction (commodities such as Bitcoin and Ether, and their derivatives). The [advisories and articles](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/index.htm) page includes the standard warnings on leveraged crypto trading and offshore platforms. Use [CFTC Check](https://www.cftc.gov/check) to see whether a futures platform is registered.

### Treasury, FinCEN and the OCC: stablecoins and the GENIUS Act

The **GENIUS Act** (Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for US Stablecoins), signed in July 2025, is the first federal stablecoin law: only "permitted payment stablecoin issuers" may issue dollar stablecoins to US users, reserves must be one-to-one in cash and short Treasuries, issuers face bank-style supervision and anti-money-laundering duties, and the framework takes effect on the earlier of eighteen months after enactment or 120 days after final rules. The text is at [Congress.gov (S.1582)](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582). The implementing rules are still being written; the key documents so far:

- **[Treasury: Proposed rule on GENIUS Act illicit-finance requirements](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0435)** (FinCEN and OFAC, April 2026) — anti-money-laundering, sanctions-compliance and the "technical capability to freeze and seize" requirements for issuers.
- **[Treasury: Proposed rule on stablecoin issuance, offer and sale](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0605)** (August 2026; Federal Register [notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/18/2026-16796/genius-act-regulations-on-payment-stablecoin-issuance-offer-and-sale)) — defines what counts as issuing or offering a stablecoin in the US and sets the January 18, 2027 effective date for the issuer-only rule.
- **[OCC Bulletin 2026-3: GENIUS Act Regulations, Notice of Proposed Rulemaking](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-3.html)** (February 2026) — reserve, redemption, risk-management, custody and supervision standards for issuers chartered or supervised by the OCC.
- **[FDIC: GENIUS Act requirements for FDIC-supervised issuers](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/10/2026-06974/genius-act-requirements-and-standards-for-fdic-supervised-permitted-payment-stablecoin-issuers-and)** (April 2026) — the parallel rule for bank-subsidiary issuers.
- Law-firm trackers such as [Chapman and Cutler's GENIUS Act Rulemaking Tracker](https://www.chapman.com/publication-genius-act-rulemaking-tracker) keep a running list of which rules are proposed, final and in force; useful because the official pages are scattered across agencies.

What this means for an investor: once in force, a US-offered stablecoin either has a permitted issuer with audited one-to-one reserves or it is not legally offered here. Until then, check the issuer's monthly attestation yourself and read the [stablecoin papers](academic-papers-and-research.md) on why pegs break.

**[FinCEN: Money Services Businesses](https://www.fincen.gov/resources/financial-institutions/money-services-businesses)** — Crypto exchanges serving US customers must register with FinCEN as money transmitters. The 2019 [guidance on convertible virtual currency business models](https://www.fincen.gov/resources/statutes-regulations/guidance/application-fincens-regulations-certain-business-models) explains who counts. The [MSB Registrant Search](https://www.fincen.gov/msb-registrant-search) lets you check whether an exchange is registered; registration is a minimum, not an endorsement.

### Internal Revenue Service (IRS)

**[IRS: Digital Assets](https://www.irs.gov/filing/digital-assets)** — The hub for the digital-asset question on Form 1040, the broker-reporting rules (Form 1099-DA, which began for 2025 transactions), and what counts as a taxable event (selling, swapping one coin for another, spending, receiving staking or mining income). The **[FAQs on virtual currency transactions](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/frequently-asked-questions-on-virtual-currency-transactions)** answer the specific cases (hard forks, airdrops, gifts, cost basis). Crypto is property for US tax purposes; every swap is a sale. Read before your first trade, not at filing time.

### Consumer protection and scam reporting

**[FTC: What to know about cryptocurrency and scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-cryptocurrency-and-scams)** — The plain-English list of red flags (anyone who demands payment in crypto, guaranteed returns, romance-to-investment pitches). Report at **[ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/)**.

**[FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3)](https://www.ic3.gov/)** — File a complaint at [ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov/Home/ComplaintChoice) if you have lost money to a crypto scam or hack; the annual IC3 report is the official tally (crypto investment fraud is the largest loss category). IC3's [public service announcements](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA) describe current patterns, including the "pig butchering" romance-to-investment scam and fake crypto recovery services, in detail.

**[Chainabuse](https://www.chainabuse.com/)** — Not a regulator, but the industry's shared scam-address database (run by TRM Labs, used by law enforcement); report there too.

## United States: state regulators

**[NYDFS: Virtual Currency Business Licensing (BitLicense)](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/virtual_currency_businesses)** — New York's licensing regime, the strictest in the country since 2015. The page lists every licensed entity; an exchange that serves New Yorkers without appearing on it is operating illegally there. Also the home of NYDFS's stablecoin guidance, which predated the federal law.

**[NASAA: Contact your regulator](https://www.nasaa.org/contact-your-regulator/)** — Every state has a securities regulator, and they bring more small crypto-fraud cases than the SEC does. Use this directory to find yours and to check whether a "crypto investment" promoter is registered.

**[NMLS Consumer Access](https://www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org/)** — The national database of state-licensed money transmitters, which is what most US crypto exchanges are in most states. Search the exchange's legal entity name.

## How to check whether an exchange is licensed

1. **US money transmitter:** FinCEN [MSB search](https://www.fincen.gov/msb-registrant-search) for federal registration; [NMLS Consumer Access](https://www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org/) for state licenses; [NYDFS](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/virtual_currency_businesses) if you are in New York.
2. **US securities or futures:** [FINRA BrokerCheck](https://brokercheck.finra.org/) for any entity claiming to be a broker-dealer; [CFTC Check](https://www.cftc.gov/check) for futures platforms. Most crypto exchanges are neither, which is itself useful to know.
3. **UK:** the [FCA cryptoasset firms page](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/cryptoassets) explains the registration regime; search the [FCA Register](https://register.fca.org.uk/) for the firm.
4. **EU:** MiCA-authorized crypto-asset service providers are listed by national regulators and collected by ESMA (below).
5. **Anywhere:** if the platform's website names no legal entity, no regulator and no jurisdiction, it is unlicensed. Offshore derivatives platforms that block US IPs but accept a VPN are unlicensed for you.
6. Licensing means the platform met a regulator's minimum standards on capital, custody and anti-money-laundering. It does not mean your deposits are insured (they are not, unless a bank holds cash for you) or that the platform is solvent (FTX was licensed in several places).

## European Union: MiCA

**[ESMA: Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA)](https://www.esma.europa.eu/esmas-activities/digital-finance-and-innovation/markets-crypto-assets-regulation-mica)** — The EU securities regulator's MiCA hub: the regulation applies to stablecoin issuers from June 2024 and to all crypto-asset service providers from December 30, 2024, with national transition periods ending by July 2026. ESMA publishes the register of authorized providers and the technical standards. The **[European Commission's crypto-assets page](https://finance.ec.europa.eu/digital-finance/crypto-assets_en)** explains the policy, and the regulation itself is on [EUR-Lex (Regulation (EU) 2023/1114)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1114/oj). For an EU investor, MiCA is why some stablecoins (notably USDT) were delisted from EU exchanges and why authorized providers must publish a white paper for every listed asset.

## International bodies

**[Financial Stability Board: Crypto-assets and global stablecoins](https://www.fsb.org/work-of-the-fsb/financial-innovation-and-structural-change/crypto-assets-and-global-stablecoins/)** — The G20's coordinating body; its 2023 [global regulatory framework](https://www.fsb.org/2023/07/fsb-global-regulatory-framework-for-crypto-asset-activities/) ("same activity, same risk, same regulation") is the template national rules are converging on.

**[Bank for International Settlements](https://www.bis.org/publ/arpdf/ar2022e3.htm)** — The central banks' view (see [academic-papers-and-research.md](academic-papers-and-research.md)); also sets the Basel capital rules that make it expensive for banks to hold crypto.

**[FCA (UK): Cryptoassets](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/cryptoassets)** — The UK regime: registration for anti-money-laundering, strict financial-promotion rules (the "you should be prepared to lose all your money" banner), and a full authorization regime being phased in.

## Key takeaways

- Before using any exchange, check it in FinCEN's MSB search, NMLS and (if relevant) NYDFS, the FCA Register or ESMA's MiCA register; licensing is a floor, not insurance.
- The GENIUS Act makes US stablecoins a regulated product from 2027; until the rules are final, read the issuer's reserve attestations yourself.
- The SEC has moved from enforcement to rule-writing, and Regulation Crypto Assets (proposed August 2026) would create the first purpose-built offering rules; a proposal is not a rule, and the enforcement-action list is still the record.
- Crypto is property for the IRS; every swap is a taxable sale, and 1099-DA reporting means the IRS already knows.
- Report scams to IC3 and the FTC even when recovery is unlikely; the reports are how the loss statistics that drive policy get counted.
