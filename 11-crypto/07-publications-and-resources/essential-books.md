# Essential Crypto Books

**In one sentence:** The thirty-odd books below cover how Bitcoin and Ethereum actually work, why people believe in them, why smart people think they are a bubble, and what happened when the bubble met FTX — read across all four camps or you only get half the story.

## How to use this list

Every entry carries four tags:

- **Author / year** — first edition year; some have later editions.
- **Difficulty** — *Beginner* (no prior knowledge), *Intermediate* (you know what a wallet and a block are), *Advanced* (technical or academic).
- **Stance** — *Bull* (advocacy), *Neutral* (explanatory or journalistic), *Skeptic* (critical), *History* (narrative reporting).
- **Cost** — several of the best technical books are free online; noted where so.

Crypto publishing has a bias problem: many bullish books are written by people who hold large positions, run funds or sell courses, and several skeptical books are written by people who made a career out of the criticism. Neither disqualifies a book, but read the author's incentives before their conclusions.

## Tier 1: start here (any order within the tier)

### Understanding the technology

**[Mastering Bitcoin](https://github.com/bitcoinbook/bitcoinbook)** — Andreas M. Antonopoulos (with David A. Harding), 2014; 3rd ed. 2023. *Advanced. Neutral. Free on GitHub (CC BY-NC-ND); paperback from O'Reilly.* The reference text for how Bitcoin works at the protocol level: keys, transactions, scripts, mining, the network. Chapters 1–4 are readable by anyone; the rest is for people who want to be able to say "I actually understand it." Also at [bitcoinbook.info](https://www.bitcoinbook.info/).

**[Mastering Ethereum](https://github.com/ethereumbook/ethereumbook)** — Andreas M. Antonopoulos and Gavin Wood, 2018. *Advanced. Neutral. Free on GitHub; paperback from O'Reilly.* The Ethereum equivalent: accounts, gas, the EVM, smart contracts, tokens, oracles. Dated in places (pre-Merge), but still the best single explanation of the Ethereum design.

**[Inventing Bitcoin](https://inventingbitcoin.com/)** — Yan Pritzker, 2019. *Beginner. Neutral-to-bull. Free to read online; cheap paperback.* Roughly 100 pages that explain *why* each piece of Bitcoin's design exists, building the system up from the problem it solves. The fastest honest path from zero to "I get it."

**[The Basics of Bitcoins and Blockchains](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/40541157-the-basics-of-bitcoins-and-blockchains)** — Antony Lewis, 2018. *Beginner. Neutral. Paid.* A former Credit Suisse trader explains money, then Bitcoin, then Ethereum, then everything that gets bolted on (ICOs, stablecoins, enterprise chains), without cheerleading. The best neutral primer for a finance person; the author's blog is [Bits on Blocks](https://bitsonblocks.net/).

**[Bitcoin and Cryptocurrency Technologies](https://bitcoinbook.cs.princeton.edu/)** — Arvind Narayanan, Joseph Bonneau, Edward Felten, Andrew Miller, Steven Goldfeder, 2016. *Advanced. Neutral. Free draft PDF; [Princeton University Press](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691171692/bitcoin-and-cryptocurrency-technologies) hardcover.* The academic textbook that pairs with the free Coursera course. Rigorous and unsentimental about what is and is not solved.

### The case for

**[The Bitcoin Standard](https://saifedean.com/tbs)** — Saifedean Ammous, 2018. *Intermediate. Bull. Paid.* The book that gave Bitcoin its "digital gold / hard money" narrative. The first half is a history of money that many economists dispute (its Austrian-school claims about the gold standard and the Fed are contested), and the author's certainty is a feature or a bug depending on the reader. Read it because everyone in the industry has, then read the critiques: the [ECB's "Bitcoin's last stand"](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/blog/date/2022/html/ecb.blog221130~5301eecd19.en.html) blog, [Attack of the 50 Foot Blockchain](https://davidgerard.co.uk/blockchain/book/), and the academic papers in [academic-papers-and-research.md](academic-papers-and-research.md). The sequel, *The Fiat Standard* (2021), is more polemical and less useful.

**[Broken Money](https://www.lynalden.com/broken-money/)** — Lyn Alden, 2023. *Intermediate. Bull, but measured. Paid.* A macro analyst's history of money as a ledger technology, with Bitcoin arriving in the last third. Far more careful than *The Bitcoin Standard*, willing to state the drawbacks, and the best single book for a traditional investor who wants the bull case made by someone who also holds equities and bonds. Alden runs an investment newsletter, so she has an audience incentive, but her track record is transparent.

**[Layered Money](https://www.layeredmoney.com/)** — Nik Bhatia, 2021. *Beginner. Bull. Paid.* A short, elegant framework: money as layers (gold, then banknotes, then bank deposits) and where Bitcoin and stablecoins sit. Useful mental model even if you reject the conclusion.

**[Read Write Own](https://readwriteown.com/)** — Chris Dixon, 2024. *Beginner. Bull. Paid.* The a16z partner's case that blockchains are a new kind of computer that lets users own the networks they use. Clear on the vision, thin on the failures; remember the author's firm has billions invested in the outcome he describes.

**[Cryptoassets](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/551364/cryptoassets-by-chris-burniske-and-jack-tatar/)** — Chris Burniske and Jack Tatar, 2017. *Intermediate. Bull. Paid.* The first serious attempt at a valuation framework for tokens (network value to transactions, the equation of exchange). Dated — it predates DeFi and most of today's tokens — but the portfolio-construction chapters remain the origin of how many funds still think.

### The case against

**[Attack of the 50 Foot Blockchain](https://davidgerard.co.uk/blockchain/book/)** — David Gerard, 2017. *Beginner. Skeptic. Paid (cheap ebook).* A funny, short, relentless account of Bitcoin's early scams, Mt. Gox, the DAO hack and the "blockchain for everything" hype. Gerard's tone is sneering and he has never found anything in crypto he liked, so calibrate accordingly, but almost every episode he describes happened as described. His [blog](https://davidgerard.co.uk/blockchain/) continues the coverage.

**[Popping the Crypto Bubble](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/61337011-popping-the-crypto-bubble)** — Stephen Diehl, Jan Akalin and Darren Tseng, 2022. *Intermediate. Skeptic. Paid.* A software engineer's argument that crypto is a technology-shaped financial product with no legitimate use beyond speculation and evasion. The technical critiques (throughput, oracle problem, the impossibility of "code is law") are the strongest part; the policy chapters are more one-note. Diehl's essays are at [stephendiehl.com](https://www.stephendiehl.com/).

**[Number Go Up](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/123276708-number-go-up)** — Zeke Faux, 2023. *Beginner. Skeptic / history. Paid.* A Bloomberg reporter chases Tether around the world and ends up in FTX's Bahamas penthouse and a Cambodian scam compound. Very funny, very readable, and the best single book on the 2021–22 cycle's dark side. Faux started skeptical and stayed skeptical; the book does not pretend otherwise. Author site: [zekefaux.com](https://www.zekefaux.com/).

### The history

**[Digital Gold](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0062362496)** — Nathaniel Popper, 2015. *Beginner. History. Paid.* The New York Times reporter's account of Bitcoin's first six years: Satoshi, the cypherpunks, Silk Road, Mt. Gox, the Winklevoss twins. Still the best narrative history of the early period.

**[The Infinite Machine](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0062886142)** — Camila Russo, 2020. *Beginner. History, sympathetic. Paid.* How Ethereum was founded, funded and nearly destroyed by the DAO hack. Russo later founded The Defiant, a DeFi-friendly outlet, and the book reads as an admirer's account, but it is well reported.

**[The Cryptopians](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1541763017)** — Laura Shin, 2022. *Intermediate. History. Paid.* The other Ethereum origin story, from the Unchained podcast host: colder, more forensic, and it names the person Shin believes carried out the DAO hack. Read alongside Russo.

**[Going Infinite](https://wwnorton.com/books/going-infinite)** — Michael Lewis, 2023. *Beginner. History. Paid.* Lewis had unique access to Sam Bankman-Fried before, during and after the collapse of FTX. The book is beautifully written and widely criticized for being too soft on its subject; Lewis seems to half-believe SBF's "it was a liquidity problem" story that a jury rejected. Read it for the access, then read *Number Go Up* for the correction.

## Tier 2: deeper on specific topics

**[The Blocksize War](https://blog.bitmex.com/the-blocksize-war/)** — Jonathan Bier, 2021. *Intermediate. Neutral-to-bull. Free chapters on the BitMEX Research blog; paperback available.* The 2015–2017 civil war over Bitcoin's block size, told by a BitMEX researcher. Essential for understanding why "Bitcoin does not change" is a governance outcome, not a law of nature.

**[Check Your Financial Privilege](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/60591550-check-your-financial-privilege)** — Alex Gladstein, 2022. *Beginner. Bull. Paid.* Essays from the Human Rights Foundation's chief strategy officer on Bitcoin use in Nigeria, Afghanistan, Cuba and other places where the banking system is a tool of the state. The best answer to "who actually needs this?"; less strong on the parts of the world where the answer is nobody. Author site: [alexgladstein.com](https://alexgladstein.com/).

**[Proof of Stake: The Making of Ethereum and the Philosophy of Blockchains](https://www.sevenstories.com/books/4331-proof-of-stake)** — Vitalik Buterin (ed. Nathan Schneider), 2022. *Intermediate. Bull, thoughtful. Paid; most essays free on [vitalik.eth.limo](https://vitalik.eth.limo/).* The Ethereum founder's collected essays, from the 2014 whitepaper era to post-Merge. Buterin is one of the few founders who writes down his doubts.

**[The Genesis Book](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/204250558-the-genesis-book)** — Aaron van Wirdum, 2024. *Intermediate. History. Paid.* The intellectual prehistory of Bitcoin: Austrian economics, the cypherpunks, DigiCash, b-money, Hashcash. For readers who want to know where the ideas came from before Satoshi assembled them.

**[The Book of Satoshi](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0996061312)** — Phil Champagne, 2014. *Intermediate. Neutral. Paid; everything in it is free at the [Satoshi Nakamoto Institute](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/).* Satoshi's collected forum posts and emails. Reading the primary source is the cure for a lot of "Satoshi intended X" arguments.

**[The Bullish Case for Bitcoin](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/58796512-the-bullish-case-for-bitcoin)** — Vijay Boyapati, 2021. *Beginner. Bull. Paid; the original 2018 essay is free online.* The tightest short version of the store-of-value argument. If you want the bull case in an hour, this is it.

**[Resistance Money](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/204274500-resistance-money)** — Andrew M. Bailey, Bradley Rettler and Craig Warmke, 2024. *Intermediate. Bull, academic. Paid.* Three philosophy professors make the case for Bitcoin using the tools of moral and political philosophy, and engage the strongest objections (energy, crime, volatility) seriously rather than waving them away. The most intellectually honest pro-Bitcoin book. Site: [resistance.money](https://www.resistance.money/).

**[21 Lessons](https://21lessons.com/)** — Gigi, 2019. *Beginner. Bull. Free online; paperback available.* Short philosophical reflections on what studying Bitcoin taught the author. Light, and popular as an on-ramp; not an argument, more a mood.

**[Mastering the Lightning Network](https://github.com/lnbook/lnbook)** — Andreas M. Antonopoulos, Olaoluwa Osuntokun and René Pickhardt, 2021. *Advanced. Neutral. Free on GitHub; O'Reilly paperback.* How Bitcoin's second layer for payments works. For readers who want to judge whether "Bitcoin scales via Lightning" is a real plan.

**[Kings of Crypto](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/56274031-kings-of-crypto)** — Jeff John Roberts, 2020. *Beginner. History. Paid.* The story of Coinbase from Y Combinator to the eve of its IPO. Useful for understanding the exchange business model, which is where most of the industry's real profit has been.

**[Bitcoin Billionaires](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1250217741)** — Ben Mezrich, 2019. *Beginner. History, sympathetic. Paid.* The Winklevoss twins' post-Facebook second act. Mezrich writes thrillers, and the book reads like one; enjoyable, not rigorous.

**[Easy Money](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1419766392)** — Ben McKenzie and Jacob Silverman, 2023. *Beginner. Skeptic. Paid.* The actor from *The O.C.* and a journalist tour the 2022 collapse, interviewing SBF, Celsius and Tether figures. Angrier and less funny than *Number Go Up*, but covers the retail-investor damage the industry prefers not to discuss.

**[Cryptonomicon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0060512806)** — Neal Stephenson, 1999. *Fiction. Paid.* A novel about cryptography, gold and a digital currency, written a decade before Bitcoin. Many early Bitcoiners cite it; it shows how old the dream is.

**[The Sovereign Individual](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0684832720)** — James Dale Davidson and Lord William Rees-Mogg, 1997. *Intermediate. Ideology. Paid.* The book that predicted "cybercash" would let individuals escape the nation-state. Read it to understand the political worldview behind much of Bitcoin culture, not as economics — many of its other predictions were wrong.

**[The Price of Tomorrow](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1999257405)** — Jeff Booth, 2020. *Beginner. Bull. Paid.* Argues technology is deflationary, central banks are fighting it with debt, and Bitcoin is the escape. Popular; the economics is heterodox and the argument is asserted more than demonstrated.

## Reading order

1. **Weekend one — understand the thing:** *Inventing Bitcoin* → *The Basics of Bitcoins and Blockchains*.
2. **Weekend two — hear the bulls:** *Broken Money* (or *Layered Money* if short on time), then *Read Write Own* for the Ethereum/web3 version.
3. **Weekend three — hear the skeptics:** *Number Go Up* → *Attack of the 50 Foot Blockchain* or *Popping the Crypto Bubble*.
4. **Then history:** *Digital Gold* → *The Infinite Machine* or *The Cryptopians* → *Going Infinite*.
5. **Then, only if you want to go deep:** *Mastering Bitcoin*, *Mastering Ethereum*, the Princeton textbook, *The Blocksize War*, *Resistance Money*.
6. **Read *The Bitcoin Standard* last**, once you have enough context to notice where it overreaches.

## Key takeaways

- The best technical books (*Mastering Bitcoin*, *Mastering Ethereum*, the Princeton textbook, *Inventing Bitcoin*, *The Blocksize War*) are free online; there is no cost excuse for not understanding the mechanism before you buy the asset.
- Read at least one skeptic for every bull; the bulls dominate the bestseller lists because they have more money behind them, not because they have won the argument.
- Journalists' books (*Digital Gold*, *Number Go Up*, *The Cryptopians*) age better than advocates' books because they record what happened rather than what should happen.
- *The Bitcoin Standard* is required cultural reading but not reliable monetary history; pair it with its critiques.
- Note the author's position: fund partners (Dixon, Burniske), newsletter sellers (Alden, Ammous) and professional critics (Gerard, Diehl) all have an audience to keep.
