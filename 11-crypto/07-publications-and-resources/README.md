# Crypto Publications and Resources

**In one sentence:** A curated, annotated and link-checked map of the books, research desks, newsletters, podcasts, data tools, academic papers, courses, communities and regulators worth your time in crypto, with the bias and cost of each spelled out and the skeptics given equal billing.

## Introduction

Crypto has more free information than any other asset class and less of it is trustworthy. Most outlets are funded by exchanges, token projects or venture funds; most "analysis" is marketing with charts; and the loudest critics have their own audiences to keep. This section does not try to list everything. It picks the resources that have earned attention across at least one full cycle, says who pays for each one and what that does to its conclusions, and deliberately pairs every bull with a bear.

Every link was checked in September 2026. Costs are noted because the best material (the technical books, the academic papers, the university courses, the core data tools) is free, and the worst (trading courses, signal groups, paid "alpha") is not. Start with the shortlist below, then go deeper by category. Read the regulators and the skeptics before you read the bulls; it is easier to hear the case for something once you know the case against.

## If you only follow 10 things

1. **[Coin Metrics: State of the Network](https://coinmetrics.substack.com/)** — the one weekly that is data first and sells nothing you would notice.
2. **[Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/)** — Laura Shin's interview show; a journalist who asks hard questions of guests she likes.
3. **[Lyn Alden](https://www.lynalden.com/)** — the bull case made by a macro analyst who also owns everything else.
4. **[Citation Needed](https://www.citationneeded.news/)** — Molly White; the documented case against, without the sneer.
5. **[Matt Levine's Money Stuff](https://www.bloomberg.com/account/newsletters/money-stuff)** — the best-written explanation of every crypto blow-up, from someone who does not need crypto to succeed.
6. **[DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/)** — free, open-source, no token; TVL, fees, stablecoins, hacks.
7. **[Farside ETF Flows](https://farside.co.uk/btc/)** and **[Tokenomist](https://tokenomist.ai/)** — where the institutional money is going in, and when the insider supply is coming out.
8. **[Bitcoin Optech](https://bitcoinops.org/)** and the **[Ethereum Foundation blog](https://blog.ethereum.org/)** — primary sources on what is actually changing in the two protocols.
9. **[MIT OCW: Blockchain and Money](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-s12-blockchain-and-money-fall-2018/)** — Gary Gensler's free course; the mechanism and the law in 24 lectures.
10. **[IRS Digital Assets](https://www.irs.gov/filing/digital-assets)** and **[Investor.gov Crypto Assets](https://www.investor.gov/additional-resources/spotlight/crypto-assets)** — what you owe and what the regulator warns about, from the source.

## Key takeaways

- Nearly every crypto source is funded by someone selling crypto; know the business model before you weigh the conclusion.
- The best material is free: the technical books, the academic papers, the university courses and the core data tools cost nothing.
- Pair every bull with a skeptic; the disagreement is where the information is.
- Data vendors and grant-funded digests (Coin Metrics, Kaiko, Bitcoin Optech, DefiLlama) are the lowest-bias private sources; regulators and central banks are the lowest-bias public ones.
- Unlock schedules, holder concentration, approval hygiene and licensing checks prevent more losses than any newsletter will.
