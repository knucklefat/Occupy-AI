# Buy-and-Hold and Dollar-Cost Averaging

**In one sentence:** Buying bitcoin (and, with more caveats, ether) on a fixed schedule and holding through crashes has been the single most reliable way ordinary people have made money in crypto, and it works precisely because it removes the two decisions retail investors get wrong most often: when to buy and when to sell.

## What "HODL" actually means

"HODL" started as a drunken misspelling of "hold" on a 2013 bitcoin forum and became shorthand for a strategy: buy, do not trade, do not try to time the cycle, and accept enormous interim losses in exchange for the long-run trend. Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) is the disciplined version — a fixed dollar amount bought at a fixed interval (weekly, biweekly, monthly) regardless of price. When the price is high you get fewer coins; when it is low you get more. Your average cost ends up below the average price over the period, and, more importantly, you never have to make the "is now a good time?" call.

The honest case for this strategy is not that bitcoin must go up. It is that (a) if you believe the asset has a positive long-run expected return, the evidence says you will capture more of it by not trading, and (b) the alternative — timing entries and exits — has a dismal track record for retail investors.

## The drawdown history you are signing up for

Bitcoin's long-run returns come packaged with drawdowns that would end the career of any fund manager. The table below uses daily average prices across major exchanges (Blockchain.com data), so intraday extremes were slightly worse (the October 6, 2025 intraday high was about $126,000).

Ether has been worse: roughly -94% (Jan 2018 to Dec 2018), -79% (Nov 2021 to Jun 2022), -64% (Mar 2024 to Apr 2025) and about -68% from its August 2025 high of ~$4,830 to ~$1,565 in June 2026.

As of September 2026, bitcoin trades around $77,000, roughly 38% below its October 2025 all-time high of about $126,000; ether trades around $2,500, about 48% below its high. Calendar-year returns for bitcoin were -6% in 2025 and about -13% year-to-date in 2026 — the first back-to-back down stretch since 2014–15.

## What DCA has actually delivered

We ran the numbers directly on daily price data: $100 invested every week for four years, starting on the first of every month from January 2011 through September 2022 (the last start date with a complete four-year window as of September 2026).

**Bitcoin, 4-year weekly DCA (141 start dates):**
- Windows that ended with a loss: **0**
- Worst outcome: +34% (start January 2019, ending January 2023 near the FTX lows)
- Windows starting in mid-2022 and ending in mid-2026's bear market: +39% to +45%
- Median outcome: roughly +390% — but skewed heavily by early years when bitcoin was under $1,000
- Starting at the December 2017 top: +519% by December 2021
- Starting at the November 2021 top: +183% by November 2025

**Bitcoin, 3-year windows:** 6 of 153 ended negative (worst -10%, starting January 2020). **2-year windows:** 30 of 165 ended negative, worst -49% (starting January 2021). Time horizon is the whole strategy.

**Ether, 4-year weekly DCA (58 start dates, Dec 2017 onward):**
- Windows that ended with a loss: **7 of 58** (all of them starting in 2021–22 and ending in 2025–26)
- Worst outcome: -25% (start July 2022)
- Median outcome: roughly +200%

Two warnings about these figures. First, "no losing 4-year bitcoin window" is a statement about the past; a window starting at the October 2025 peak is not finished and could easily break the streak if prices stay depressed into 2029. Second, the ether results show that a four-year horizon does not guarantee a profit even in a large, established crypto asset. Returns for the thousands of smaller tokens are far worse — most have gone to zero, and DCA into a dying asset just averages down into nothing.

Lump-sum investing beat DCA in most historical bitcoin windows (as it does in stocks), because the asset trended up. Four-year lump-sum holds of bitcoin never lost money on our monthly start dates either, with the worst being +41% (start April 2021). DCA's advantage is not higher returns; it is that it is psychologically survivable and it caps the damage of a badly timed lump sum.

## Why most retail buyers lose anyway

The Bank for International Settlements studied crypto app usage across 95 countries from 2015 to 2022 and found that new users flooded in after prices rose — daily active users went from about 100,000 to over 30 million by the November 2021 peak — and that roughly three-quarters of retail bitcoin investors were likely underwater by December 2022. The median retail investor had lost about half their money. Larger, sophisticated holders sold into the 2022 Terra and FTX collapses while smaller investors bought.

The pattern is simple: retail buys after a parabolic rise (the only time crypto makes the evening news), holds through the first 30% drop, panics near the 70% mark, sells, and swears off the asset just before it recovers. DCA works because it forces buying during the phase when nobody wants to.

Other ways buy-and-hold fails in practice:
- **Custody losses.** Exchange failures (Mt. Gox 2014, FTX 2022) and lost keys have erased more "hodler" wealth than any price crash.
- **Selling the wrong coin.** Holding through -80% only pays if the asset recovers. Bitcoin has; ether mostly has; hundreds of 2017 and 2021 "blue chips" never did.
- **Over-sizing.** A -77% drawdown on 5% of your net worth is a bad year; on 50% it is a changed life.

## How to actually do it

1. **Decide the total allocation first** (see the small-allocation file). DCA is a method of getting to a target, not a reason to keep adding forever.
2. **Pick bitcoin, optionally ether, and stop there.** The DCA evidence exists for these two; it does not exist for anything else.
3. **Choose the venue.** A spot ETF in a brokerage account is simplest and works in an IRA. A reputable exchange with a recurring-buy feature (Coinbase, Kraken, River, Swan) is the direct route; check fees — recurring buys on some platforms carry 1–2% spreads, which compound.
4. **Set a fixed amount and interval you can sustain for four years.** Weekly or biweekly matched to your paycheck is ideal. Automate it.
5. **If you buy directly, move coins off the exchange periodically** to a hardware wallet once the balance exceeds what you would be upset to lose. Write down the seed phrase; store it physically in two places.
6. **Write your selling rule now, while calm.** Examples: rebalance back to target once a year; sell 10% of the position if it exceeds 2x the target weight; never sell purely because the price fell.
7. **Keep records.** Every purchase is a tax lot. Exchanges and ETF brokers report; self-custody buyers need their own spreadsheet.
8. **Review annually, not daily.** Delete the price app if you must.

## Key takeaways

- Bitcoin has lost 53–93% from its peak eight separate times; ether has lost 64–94% four times. Assume it will happen again while you hold.
- Every four-year weekly DCA into bitcoin starting between 2011 and September 2022 ended in profit; the worst was +34%. Three-year windows failed 4% of the time; two-year windows failed 18% of the time.
- Ether is not bitcoin: 12% of four-year ether DCA windows lost money, all of them starting during the 2021–22 boom.
- BIS data shows roughly three-quarters of retail bitcoin buyers were underwater by late 2022 because they bought after rallies and sold in crashes — DCA is a tool against that instinct, not a magic return generator.
- Lump sum historically beat DCA in bitcoin, as in equities; DCA's edge is behavioral, not mathematical.
- Position size, not entry timing, is what determines whether a crash is survivable.
- As of September 2026 bitcoin is ~38% and ether ~48% below their 2025 highs after two consecutive down years — squarely in the "nobody wants to buy" phase DCA is designed for, which is not the same thing as a guarantee.
