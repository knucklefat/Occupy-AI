# Bitcoin Treasury Companies and Proxies

**In one sentence:** Stocks that give you "bitcoin exposure" — treasury companies like Strategy, miners, exchanges — are leveraged, fee-laden or business-diluted versions of the thing itself, and now that spot ETFs exist there is rarely a reason to own a proxy unless you specifically want the leverage or the business.

## The treasury-company model

In August 2020, MicroStrategy (renamed Strategy in 2025) began converting its balance sheet into bitcoin. The model it invented works like this:

1. The stock trades at a premium to the value of its bitcoin. The ratio of enterprise value (market cap plus debt plus preferred stock, minus cash) to bitcoin holdings is called **mNAV** ("multiple of net asset value"). At 2x mNAV, the market pays $2 for every $1 of bitcoin on the balance sheet.
2. The company sells new shares at that premium and buys bitcoin with the proceeds. Because it raised $2 per $1 of bitcoin it previously held, each old share now represents *more* bitcoin than before — Strategy calls this "BTC yield" and reported figures above 50% for 2024.
3. It also borrows — convertible bonds (near-zero coupons during 2021–24) and, from 2025, perpetual preferred stock (STRK, STRF, STRD, STRC) paying 8–11% dividends — and buys more bitcoin.
4. Rising bitcoin lifts the stock more than proportionally (leverage), which sustains the premium, which enables more issuance. The loop is reflexive in both directions.

At the November 2024 peak, Strategy's stock traded near 3x mNAV and around $540 a share. By late 2025 it held roughly 640,000 BTC; continued buying took it to about 845,000 BTC (845,050 after its August 31, 2026 purchase, per Strategy's own ledger; about 4% of all bitcoin that will ever exist).

## The 2025–26 shakeout

The premium is the whole machine, and in 2025–26 it disappeared.

- **Strategy** fell to about $82 a share by June 2026, roughly 85% below its 2024 high, and its enterprise mNAV dropped below 1.0 for the first time — the market valued the company at less than its bitcoin. At that point issuing equity to buy bitcoin *dilutes* shareholders rather than accreting to them. The company halted purchases for weeks, sold a small amount in Q2 2026 and more than 3,500 BTC in July to rebuild dollar reserves for preferred dividends, and faced criticism that continued buying at sub-1x was destroying value. Its preferred stock, which pays fixed dollar dividends from a company whose only real asset is bitcoin, is the structural pressure point: those dividends must be funded by selling stock, selling bitcoin, or issuing more preferred. A further overhang: MSCI's August 2026 "non-operating company" proposal would, as drafted, remove Strategy and Metaplanet from its global indexes at the November 2026 review (consultation closes September 30), which JPMorgan estimated could force roughly $2.8 billion of passive selling (see the Saylor profile for details).
- **The copycats.** Forbes counted 228 public companies that announced digital-asset treasuries in 2025, collectively investing about $148B — including Metaplanet ("Asia's Strategy"), Twenty One Capital, Nakamoto/KindlyMD, Semler Scientific, and dozens of micro-caps that pivoted from biotech or gaming. By late 2025 about 15% traded below 1x mNAV; by mid-2026 the majority did. Metaplanet's stock fell nearly 90% from its high, paused purchases for months, and briefly dipped below 1x mNAV before resuming buying in July 2026. Satsuma Technologies sold its bitcoin to service convertible debt and voted to wind down; Nakamoto sold twice; Twenty One's CEO stepped down. As of September 2026, with bitcoin near $77,000 and Strategy's basic mNAV hovering around 1.07–1.1x (mNAV.com), about 179 public companies still hold bitcoin, but the ones without operating cash flow and with expensive debt are being forced to sell into weakness — the mirror image of the accumulation loop.

The closest historical precedent is Grayscale's GBTC, which traded at a 30–40% premium in 2020 (when it was the only easy way to buy bitcoin in a brokerage) and a 49% discount by December 2022 once alternatives existed and redemptions were impossible. Strategy has more levers than a passive trust — a software business, debt capacity, the ability to sell bitcoin — but the lesson holds: **a premium that exists because access is scarce vanishes when access becomes easy**, and spot ETFs made access easy in January 2024.

## Miners as proxies

Bitcoin miners (Marathon/MARA, Riot, CleanSpark, Core Scientific, IREN, Bitdeer, Hut 8) are operating businesses whose revenue is bitcoin. In theory they are leveraged plays on the price: fixed costs, variable revenue. In practice:

- **Their revenue per unit of computing power ("hashprice") is set by bitcoin's price *divided by* network difficulty,** and difficulty only rises. Hashprice fell from about $63 per petahash-day in July 2025 to $28–35 in early 2026, post-halving lows, while bitcoin was still above $60,000. CoinShares estimated Q4 2025 all-in costs of $118,000–153,000 per coin at the large public miners — above the spot price.
- **They dilute relentlessly** to fund new machines, and the machines depreciate in two to three years.
- **They are pivoting to AI data centers.** Over $70B of AI/high-performance-computing contracts had been announced by public miners by early 2026 (Core Scientific with CoreWeave, IREN, TeraWulf, Cipher), funded by billions in new debt. By 2026 some expected 70% of revenue from AI hosting. Whether or not that is a good business, it is not bitcoin exposure.
- **The result:** in the August–September 2026 bitcoin rally, the median public miner captured about 8% of bitcoin's gain; Core Scientific and TeraWulf lagged bitcoin by 24–27 points. Over most multi-year windows the miner indices have underperformed bitcoin with far higher volatility. Miners do occasionally outperform explosively at the start of a bull market, which keeps the idea alive.

## Picks-and-shovels: exchanges, stablecoins, brokers

**Coinbase (COIN)** earns trading fees, custody fees (it custodies most spot ETFs), interest on USDC, staking commissions and subscription revenue. Its stock correlates with crypto prices but is increasingly a diversified financial company — 88% of Q2 2026 net revenue came from outside bitcoin spot trading. **Robinhood (HOOD)** is a broader retail brokerage with crypto as one high-margin segment. **Circle (CRCL)**, public since 2025, earns interest on USDC reserves (about $73B in circulation as of mid-2026) — a bet on stablecoin adoption and on interest rates staying up, not on bitcoin. **Galaxy Digital** is a crypto investment bank and data-center operator.

These are real businesses that can be analyzed on earnings, and in the 2026 rally they kept pace with or beat bitcoin. But they carry regulatory, competitive and execution risk unrelated to bitcoin, they trade at high multiples in bull markets, and they fall 70–90% in crypto winters (COIN went from $357 in 2021 to $33 in 2022). Buy them as stocks, on valuation, if you like the business; do not buy them as a substitute for bitcoin.

## Why proxies usually underperform spot

- **Leverage cuts both ways** and is applied to an asset that already falls 50–80% regularly; the proxy's drawdowns exceed 85–95%.
- **Dilution.** Every proxy issues stock. Bitcoin's supply is fixed.
- **Fees and frictions.** Interest on convertibles, preferred dividends, executive compensation, data-center capex.
- **Premium decay.** Whatever scarcity premium existed at purchase compresses as alternatives arrive.
- **Business risk.** Miners can go bankrupt (Core Scientific did in 2022 and re-emerged); exchanges can be hacked or sued; a treasury company's management can change strategy.
- **The ETF alternative.** For 0.15–0.25% a year you can own the asset itself, in an IRA, with no dilution and no CEO.

When proxies *do* make sense: you want leveraged bitcoin exposure and prefer a listed stock to margin or futures (understanding the premium can go to zero); you cannot access an ETF in your jurisdiction; you genuinely believe in the operating business; or you want to trade the mNAV cycle itself — long treasury companies at deep discounts, short them at high premiums — which is a hedge-fund trade, not an investment.

## How to actually do it

1. **Default to the spot ETF or self-custody.** If you cannot articulate why you want a proxy instead, you do not.
2. **If buying a treasury company, check mNAV first** (mnav.com or the company's own dashboard). Below 1.0, you are buying bitcoin at a discount plus a company; above 2.0, you are paying double and depending on the premium persisting. Look at debt maturities and preferred dividend obligations relative to cash.
3. **If buying a miner, read it as a data-center company:** power cost per kWh, fleet efficiency (joules per terahash), hashprice sensitivity, AI contract terms, share count growth. Ignore "bitcoin held" as a valuation anchor.
4. **If buying Coinbase, Robinhood or Circle, value them as financial companies:** revenue mix, take rate, regulatory exposure, interest-rate sensitivity.
5. **Size proxies inside your crypto allocation, not on top of it.** A miner is not a diversifier from bitcoin.
6. **Expect 85–95% drawdowns** and decide in advance whether you would add, hold or sell.
7. **Never buy a treasury company's preferred stock for "safe yield"** without understanding that the dividend is paid from bitcoin sales or new issuance.

## Key takeaways

- Treasury companies create value only while their stock trades above the value of their bitcoin; the premium is the engine, and in 2026 Strategy's fell below 1x and the stock was down ~85% from its 2024 high.
- More than 200 companies copied the model in 2025; by mid-2026 most traded below NAV and the weakest were selling bitcoin to pay debts — the accumulation loop running in reverse.
- Miners are AI data-center companies with a bitcoin hangover; hashprice at 2026 lows means most large miners' all-in cost exceeds the spot price, and they captured a fraction of bitcoin's 2026 rally.
- Coinbase, Robinhood and Circle are real businesses with real earnings; own them on valuation, not as bitcoin substitutes.
- Every proxy adds dilution, leverage, fees and business risk to an asset that is already volatile enough; since January 2024, a spot ETF does the job for 0.25% a year.
- GBTC's 2020 premium turning into a 49% discount by 2022 is the template for what happens to scarcity premiums when access improves.
- Proxies suit specific, deliberate uses — leverage, jurisdictional constraints, a view on the business — and should be sized within the crypto sleeve.
