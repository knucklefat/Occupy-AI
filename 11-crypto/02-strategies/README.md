# Crypto Strategies: From Simplest to Most Involved

**In one sentence:** There are about eight distinct ways people try to make money in crypto, and the evidence supports the boring ones — a small, rebalanced allocation to bitcoin (and maybe ether), bought on a schedule and held for years — far more than the exciting ones.

This section walks through each approach in order of complexity, with the historical record, the real risks, and step-by-step implementation. The pattern that emerges is consistent: the strategies with the strongest evidence require the least skill and the most patience, while the strategies that promise the most require constant attention, add layers of counterparty and contract risk, and have a long record of retail participants losing to better-capitalized players. That does not mean the involved strategies are worthless — staking real yield on coins you already hold, or a disciplined trend filter in a tax-advantaged account, are defensible — but every step up the ladder should be taken deliberately, with a named reason, and sized as if it could go to zero. Figures are stamped as of September 2026; check current data before acting.

## Comparison of strategies

| Strategy | Complexity | Risk | Time required | Evidence it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Buy-and-hold / DCA](buy-and-hold-and-dollar-cost-averaging.md) | Low | High (asset drawdowns of 50–93%) but simple | Minutes a month | Strong for BTC: no losing 4-year DCA window 2011–2022; mixed for ETH (12% of windows lost) | Anyone with a 4+ year horizon who can ignore volatility |
| [Small allocation in a traditional portfolio](small-allocation-in-a-traditional-portfolio.md) | Low | Low at the portfolio level (1–5% sleeve) | An hour a year to rebalance | Strong: Bitwise and Fidelity backtests show roughly doubled Sharpe ratios over 2014–2025; Morningstar cautions on rising correlation | Most investors; the default recommendation |
| [Bitcoin and crypto ETFs](bitcoin-and-crypto-etfs.md) | Low | Same as the underlying plus issuer/custodian | Minutes | Vehicle, not a strategy; tracking is tight and fees are 0.15–0.35% | IRAs, 401(k) brokerage windows, anyone who does not want custody duties |
| [Staking and yield](staking-and-yield.md) | Medium | Medium: slashing (rare), contract, counterparty, depeg; price risk dominates | An hour to set up, quarterly checks | Real: 3–7% nominal on ETH/SOL, but 1–3% after inflation; every added layer has failed somewhere | Holders of proof-of-stake coins who would own them anyway |
| [DeFi yield farming and LPing](defi-yield-farming-and-liquidity-provision.md) | High | High: impermanent loss, exploits, emissions collapse | Weekly monitoring | Weak for retail: half of Uniswap v3 LPs underperformed holding; the 2020–22 graveyard is long | Experienced on-chain users with speculative capital and tax tooling |
| [Trading and momentum](trading-and-momentum-strategies.md) | High | Very high with leverage; moderate with slow trend rules | Daily to weekly | Academic momentum effect is real; 200-day MA roughly matched buy-and-hold with smaller drawdowns; leveraged retail loses reliably | Disciplined, unleveraged rule-followers in tax-advantaged accounts |
| [Treasury companies and proxies](bitcoin-treasury-companies-and-proxies.md) | Medium | Very high: leverage, dilution, premium collapse (Strategy -85% from 2024 peak) | Ongoing | Poor since spot ETFs launched; proxies usually underperform spot with more volatility | People who want leverage or a specific operating business, sized inside the crypto sleeve |
| [Airdrops, points, early participation](airdrops-points-and-early-participation.md) | High | Medium capital risk, high wallet-drain risk, low transparency | Hours a week | Lottery-like: a few huge payouts (Uniswap, Hyperliquid), most 2024–26 drops small and declining | Active protocol users who would use the tools anyway; treat as a bonus |

## Reading order

If you are new: read 2, then 3, then 1. That covers the strategy most people should actually use and the two ways to implement it. Read 4 only if you hold proof-of-stake coins. Read 5–8 to understand what you are choosing *not* to do, or to do it with open eyes.
