# International Overview: How Other Jurisdictions Regulate and Tax Crypto

**In one sentence:** Most major economies now have a licensing regime for crypto businesses and a tax rule for individuals, ranging from Germany's tax-free-after-one-year to Japan's 55% top rate, but for a US citizen none of it matters much because the US taxes you on worldwide income wherever you live.

*Current as of September 11, 2026. Foreign tax rules change often; confirm with a local adviser before acting.*

## Comparison table

| **United Kingdom** | FCA financial-promotion rules since Oct 2023; full authorization regime finalized June 2026 with go-live for authorized firms in late 2027 (exact date per FCA rules) | Capital gains tax 18% (basic) / 24% (higher) above a £3,000 annual exemption; staking/mining/airdrops taxed as income | UK adopted the OECD Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) from January 1, 2026; exchanges report user data to HMRC from 2027 |

Other frequently cited tax-friendly places: Cayman Islands and Bermuda (no income or capital gains tax; regulated VASP regimes), Malaysia (no tax on non-business individual trades), Malta, and Panama. Portugal ended its zero-tax status for short-term gains in 2023; the Netherlands taxes deemed returns on wealth rather than realized gains.

## Two global reporting regimes to know

- **OECD CARF (Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework):** roughly 50 jurisdictions committed to automatic exchange of crypto account data, with first exchanges in 2027 for 2026 data (UK, EU via DAC8, Switzerland, Singapore, Japan, and others). The IRS's broker regulations were drafted with CARF in mind and Treasury has said it intends to participate; the exact US start date was not confirmed in the sources reviewed.
- **FATF Travel Rule:** exchanges must share sender/receiver identity on transfers above set thresholds (US $3,000 proposed; EU €1,000; many jurisdictions €/$1,000). This is why exchanges ask who owns the wallet you are withdrawing to.

## The US citizen's problem: worldwide taxation

The United States (with Eritrea) taxes its **citizens and green-card holders on worldwide income regardless of where they live**. Moving to Dubai or Lisbon does not change your US capital gains tax. Specifically:

- The **Foreign Earned Income Exclusion** (about $130,000 for 2025, indexed) covers *earned* income (salary), not capital gains, staking rewards, or interest.
- The **foreign tax credit** offsets US tax with tax you paid abroad. In a 0% jurisdiction there is nothing to credit, so you pay full US tax.
- **FBAR and Form 8938** filing obligations follow you (see the reporting file).
- State tax can also follow you if you keep ties to a high-tax state; California and New York scrutinize "moves" abroad.

**Example.** A US citizen living in Dubai sells BTC with a $400,000 long-term gain in 2026. UAE tax: $0. US tax: 20% federal on most of it plus 3.8% NIIT (the NIIT cannot be offset by foreign tax credits) ≈ **$90,000+**, plus state tax if the taxpayer failed to sever state domicile. The Dubai residence saved nothing on this gain.

## Renouncing: the exit tax

The only way out of worldwide taxation is to give up citizenship (or a long-term green card), and Section 877A imposes an **exit tax** on "covered expatriates": anyone with net worth of **$2 million or more**, or average annual US income tax over the prior five years above an inflation-indexed threshold (about $206,000 for 2025; the 2026 figure was not confirmed in the sources reviewed), or who cannot certify five years of tax compliance.

A covered expatriate is treated as having **sold all assets at fair market value the day before expatriating**. Gain above an exclusion (about $890,000 for 2025, indexed) is taxed immediately, at long-term rates for crypto held over a year.

**Example.** Net worth $6 million, of which $4.5 million is BTC with $500,000 basis. Deemed gain $4 million, minus roughly $890,000 exclusion = about $3.1 million taxable, at 20% + 3.8% ≈ **$740,000** due, with no cash from an actual sale. Gifts and bequests from a covered expatriate to US persons are later hit with a 40% transfer tax on the recipient. Renunciation also requires a $2,350 State Department fee and an in-person appointment, and it is irreversible.

## Puerto Rico: the in-between option

Puerto Rico is a US territory with its own tax code. Under **Act 60**, a bona fide PR resident (183+ days, closer connection, tax home there, plus buying a home and a $10,000 annual donation) pays **0% Puerto Rico tax on capital gains that accrue after moving**, and those gains are exempt from federal tax because PR-source income of bona fide residents is excluded. Appreciation from *before* the move remains US-taxable if sold within 10 years (fully) and partially thereafter. You keep your citizenship and passport. The IRS runs a dedicated audit campaign on Act 60 claimants, so the residency must be real.

## How to actually do it

1. If you are a US person, start from the premise that your US tax bill does not change with geography, then look for legitimate reductions: Puerto Rico, foreign tax credits, timing of sales.
2. If you use a foreign exchange, check whether it is licensed locally (MiCA register, FCA register, MAS, SFC, VARA) and understand that it will report to your home tax authority under CARF from 2026–27.
3. Non-US readers: check your country's holding-period rule (Germany, Portugal) before selling; a few extra weeks can be worth 28 percentage points.
4. Before any expatriation or Puerto Rico move, run the exit-tax or Act 60 numbers with a cross-border tax lawyer; the mistakes are seven-figure and irreversible.
5. Do not confuse "no capital gains tax" with "no tax": wealth taxes (Switzerland), professional-trader reclassification, and VAT on crypto purchases all exist.

## Key takeaways

- The EU (MiCA, fully in force July 2026), UK, Hong Kong, Singapore, UAE, and Japan all now have licensing regimes; China remains banned; El Salvador retreated from mandatory bitcoin acceptance in 2025.
- Individual tax ranges from 0% (UAE, Singapore, Switzerland, Hong Kong, Germany after one year) to 55% (Japan, pending reform to 20%).
- CARF and DAC8 make cross-border crypto data sharing automatic from 2026–27; offshore exchanges are no longer invisible.
- US citizens are taxed on worldwide income; the foreign earned income exclusion does not cover crypto gains, and NIIT cannot be offset by foreign taxes.
- Renouncing citizenship triggers a deemed sale of everything for anyone worth $2 million or more.
- Puerto Rico's Act 60 is the only zero-tax route that keeps US citizenship, and only for post-move appreciation with genuine residency.
